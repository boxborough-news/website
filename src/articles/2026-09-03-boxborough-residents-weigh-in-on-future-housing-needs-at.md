A small but engaged group of residents attended the Boxborough Housing Board's Housing Production Plan (HPP) Forum on Tuesday, August 25. Associate Town Planner Ian Gilson introduced members of the Housing Board and welcomed the public.

Gilson gave a brief introductory presentation explaining the Housing Production Plan process, its goals, and important housing concepts such as the Subsidized Housing Inventory (SHI), Chapter 40B, and Area Median Income (AMI).

He emphasized that an HPP does not rezone parcels or approve any specific housing developments. What it does, he said, is "allow us to identify areas of town where we would like to see growth." After answering questions from attendees, Gilson then invited them to participate in an interactive exercise using four posters, each focusing on a specific topic.

One poster asked "what types of housing could help meet Boxborough's needs, and what would help new housing fit within the community?" Another poster depicted a map of Boxborough and asked participants to place stickers where additional housing may be appropriate, regardless of current zoning.

The third poster was dedicated to the town-owned 72 Stow Road property and asked participants to identify their desired outcome for the property. The "Priorities Into Actions" poster asked participants what strategies and actions the town should explore to advance the housing priorities that were identified from data gathered at the Housing Board's Fifer's Day booth.

After the forum, Boxborough News reached out to Gilson to obtain a summary of the public's feedback. Gilson responded that "the responses suggest several clear themes…including interest in smaller, more attainable homes (like) starter homes, duplexes, tiny homes, and small-scale senior rentals."

Additionally, there was "support for shared or community-oriented models, such as cooperative housing, cohousing, and housing connected with agriculture." Gilson also said that the data showed "interest in reusing existing properties, including converting large single-family homes into cooperative housing and allowing multifamily housing in office parks." The responses regarding the 72 Stow Road property indicated "a desire for focused housing opportunities."

"I always appreciate the opportunity to engage with residents directly on issues like housing that affect both their lives today and the future of the community," Gilson wrote. "Participants shared thoughtful ideas about smaller and more attainable homes, cooperative housing, senior rentals, and the reuse of existing properties. We plan to bring these poster exercises to future events so that more residents can participate and help shape the Housing Production Plan."

When reached for comment, Housing Board Chair Al Murphy told Boxborough News, "The HPP will serve as an important guide for developing housing for low- and moderate-income households. The Housing Board will continue to seek input from Boxborough residents about the housing the town needs."
