At its August 27 meeting, the Boxborough Fire Station Building Committee voted unanimously that the "schematic design" phase for the town's new fire station is complete and that consultant Context Architecture ("Context") should commence the next phase of the project, "design development."

According to the town's contract with Context, there are three design phases before the fire station construction project goes out to bid. The first was "schematic design," during which Context created "schematic drawings including floor plans, elevations and space criteria to establish basic design ideas and respective cost estimates."

In the next phase, "design development," Context is expected to "complete design development documents consisting of plans, outline specifications, and cost estimates and other documents to fix and describe the size and character of the project as to architectural, structural, mechanical, and electrical systems, materials, and such other elements."

In the third phase, "construction documents," Context is expected to "complete working plans and specifications in sufficient detail to permit firm bids in open competition for construction of the project, and a detailed cost estimate."

In connection with the end of the "schematic design" phase, the FSBC reviewed an updated "construction cost" estimate for the project. The town's Owner's Project Manager, Vertex, reported that the estimated total construction cost is now $28.3 million. Steve Kirby of Vertex emphasized that this an "early estimate" and not the actual cost of the project; the numbers will change in the coming months as the estimate is refined and updated. The current $28.3 million estimate is down from the prior "conceptual" estimate of roughly $32 million.

Along with the updated construction cost estimate, Kirby also presented a list of several potential "alternate" items that would add or subtract from the total cost. The "alternate" items are related to variations in roof types, building materials, overhead doors, and HVAC systems.

The Committee voted to direct Context to pursue a flat roof over the apparatus bay and a pitched roof over the administrative portion of the building. The Committee discussed several other alternates, but no other votes were made.

After the cost discussion, Chris Logan of Context gave a short design update, noting that the design team has reconfigured the floor plan and is also working on landscaping, grading, and parking.

The FSBC also heard a presentation on a "life cycle cost analysis" for four different mechanical systems options, one of which is a geothermal system. The committee discussed the uncertainty around the tax incentives associated with a geothermal system and discussed how the drilling costs for a geothermal system are still unknown. There is funding for drilling one geothermal test well already in the design budget, and the drilling is expected to be done in this phase. The committee did not vote on whether to pursue further testing for a geothermal system; they expect to revisit the issue at their next meeting.

The FSBC's next meeting is scheduled for Thursday, September 10. On Wednesday, September 30, the FSBC will hold another "design update" for the public.
