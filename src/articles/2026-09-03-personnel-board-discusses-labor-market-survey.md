At its hybrid meeting on August 25, the Personnel Board heard from Jennifer Romboldi of the Collins Center for Public Management, a consulting firm hired to conduct a labor market survey of both union and non-union employees. The funding for this project was approved at Annual Town Meeting in May 2026.

Romboldi explained that periodic market surveys are critical for the town to support fair, competitive and sustainable employee compensation that supports both the employees and the long-term success of the community. Competitive wages are essential in attracting and retaining qualified employees across all departments, she said.

According to the report provided by the Collins Center, "The purpose of a market survey is to evaluate a town against other similarly situated towns within a market or region. While none of the municipalities surveyed were exactly the same as Boxborough, there was sufficient common purpose, responsibilities and structure to make accurate assessments on matters of compensation."

That being said, the towns in the market survey were not strictly comparable municipalities with similar populations and economic indicators. The municipalities selected for the Boxborough salary survey were municipalities in the same "labor" market. The labor market includes towns that hired former Boxborough employees, towns where current employees live, and towns where employees might realistically commute from.

According to Romboldi, the data shows that Boxborough's current pay rates are aligned with the labor market. Finance Committee Chair Tony Newton said that his review of the data indicates that Boxborough "appears to be paying above the market median." Romboldi said that Boxborough is not so far above the market that we need to change the ranges.

Personnel Board member Cheryl Mahoney asked why recruitment and retention are still problematic, if the town is in line with labor market communities. Romboldi said if the minimum data point is lower than other communities, we may need to make adjustments to the pay range. In other words, eliminate the current minimum and add to the top current maximum.

Romboldi suggested that employees with 5-6 years of service to the town should be compensated above the mid-point; somewhere between steps 4 and 9. She further recommended that the town use the data to plot wages/salaries on a graph showing where each employee lands on the salary range and make adjustments if needed.

In conclusion, the Collins Center report says "The salary survey data is intended to serve as a guide to assist Boxborough in evaluating the competitiveness of its current wage schedules. The survey …. should be used as a reference benchmark not as the sole determining factor when considering wage adjustments."

In other matters, Assistant Town Administrator Rajon Hudson reported two open positions in the Police department. The position of detective has been posted both internally and externally. There are also open positions for the Recreation Services Coordinator/Administrative Assistant, Local Transit Operator and Assistant Town Accountant. All positions have been advertised. The town is currently interviewing for the Assistant Town Accountant.

The next Personnel Board meeting is scheduled for Tuesday September 15 at Town Hall.
