At its virtual meeting on Monday, August 31, the Boxborough Planning Board conducted a public hearing to consider three proposed Zoning Bylaw Amendments for Fall Town Meeting on November 16.

The board opened the public hearing and first considered the proposed bylaw to place a temporary moratorium on data centers. Town Planner Alec Wade provided background information for members of the public. Wade explained that the town's current zoning bylaw does not address "data centers" as a use, nor does it define the term "data center."

The proposed bylaw would add a "data center" definition and temporarily prohibit its use across all zoning districts until December 2028. The purpose of the delay is to provide "sufficient time to study the use, to consult with other Town boards, committees, departments, and the public, and to develop and present to Town Meeting such amendments" that the Planning Board determines to be appropriate to regulate data centers.

The board focused on electrical usage as a defining parameter for data centers rather than the amount of space one would occupy. They studied information from Littleton Electric about the town's top users of electricity and determined that "having a total connected electrical load of five thousand (5,000) kVA or more" would provide a defined and measurable capacity.

Planning Board Clerk Chris Dowdy, the sponsor of the amendment, told Boxborough News, "The proposed moratorium on data centers will pause any potential data centers for 2 years, giving us time to draft a permanent bylaw that will prohibit things we don't want to see in Boxborough, like massive hyperscale data centers, without causing unintended consequences for existing businesses, like those already thriving at Beaver Brook. We have already reached out to local businesses for feedback and will closely review bylaws and moratoriums enacted in other towns."

After hearing from members of the public, all of whom viewed the moratorium favorably, the board continued the hearing until their September 23 meeting, which will be in hybrid format.

The board also reviewed language for two other proposed bylaw amendments–one that revises language in the Boxborough Zoning Bylaw to conform with recent changes to Massachusetts General Law (MGL) Chapter 40A and another that would bring the Town's MBTA Communities overlay district into compliance by changing the bylaw's written maximum density from 13 units per acre to 17 units per acre. The board voted unanimously to recommend both articles for Fall Town Meeting.

When asked to comment, Planning Board Chair Rich Guzzardi wrote, "The purpose of these changes to the Zoning Bylaw are to ensure the town's compliance with changes to MA GL Chapter 40A that took effect immediately upon signature by the Governor on July 9, 2026. We need to bring these changes to Fall Town Meeting to ensure that our Bylaw is current and in compliance with state law…The changes to density in our MBTA Communities District correct a technical issue with the current bylaw that was adopted at Annual Town Meeting in June and change the affordable housing threshold to 20%."

After completing their business during open session, the board entered into executive session to discuss litigation pertaining to 242/244 Adams Place. This was the third time that the board has used executive sessions to discuss the Adams Place litigation. See July 2026 Boxborough News article summarizing the case here: https://www.boxboroughnews.org/news#h.2hz4q8oehe10. The board did not discuss the litigation in open session.
