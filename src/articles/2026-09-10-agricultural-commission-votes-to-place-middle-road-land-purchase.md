At its meeting on Tuesday, September 8, the Boxborough Agricultural Commission (AgCom) members voted in favor of asking the Boxborough Select Board to place two articles on the 2026 Fall Town Meeting warrant (November 16). Draft language of the first article asks voters to approve the town's purchase of 276 Middle Road for $1,475,000 for agricultural and conservation purposes. The second article asks voters to approve $20,000 "for costs associated with and incidental to the acquisition of 276 Middle Road."

Relatedly, AgCom voted in favor of submitting an application for $300,000 from Community Preservation Act funds, which would be used toward the Middle Road land purchase.

According to AgCom's warrant article summary, the 18.57 acre property at 276 Middle Road (the Richardson Family land) has "a rich history of agricultural use, central to Boxborough's rural and agricultural heritage. This land will join contiguously to 93 acres of existing conservation and agricultural land."

The residence (ca. 1866) was the home of Clyde and Virginia (Ginny) Richardson. Ginny Richardson served as Boxborough Town Clerk for almost four decades. Together, the Richardsons farmed the land and sold a variety of fruits and vegetables, including Halloween pumpkins. Clyde died in 2015, and Ginny died in 2024. The property is now being offered for sale by their heirs.

In other business, AgCom resumed its discussion about the beaver dams blocking Beaver Brook. The dams have caused flooding of agricultural parcels while also impeding the flow of water into the fire pond that serves Paddock Estates.

As previously reported in Boxborough News, AgCom is a stakeholder (responsible for the management and maintenance of the affected parcels), but neither they nor the Board of Health have the authority to file a permit application to breach the dams or take any action; the decision lies with the property owner (Paddock Estates).

Boxborough News learned that Paddock Estates hired a contractor who breached the three dams last week. According to Town Planner Alec Wade, water flow in Beaver Brook has been restored. However, the beavers will likely attempt to rebuild their dams, so the contractor will make weekly visits to monitor the situation.

Wade told Boxborough News that Paddock Estates is working toward a long term solution, such as a Beaver Deceiver™, which manages water levels in beaver-inhabited areas and prevents beavers from building dams. AgCom Vice-Chair John Neyland also advocated that AgCom explore electric fencing as a long-term solution to prevent beaver migration. The electric wire, installed close to the ground, would not kill the beavers. Neyland told Boxborough News that the goal is for the beavers to "decide it's not a good area to settle in" and locate elsewhere.
