The end of summer brings so many noticeable changes in the world around us and the world within us. We watch as the birds we have seen all summer begin to change their behavior and organize themselves into mixed flocks and start to migrate for warmer climates. We watch the summer flowers begin to fade and the fall flowers begin to bloom. Days get shorter and the light changes everything it bathes as the colors begin to change.

It is a perfect time to add a flow to your movement patterns and to sway with the changes that are inevitably arriving.

Start standing up in Tadasana (Mountain pose) facing a chair. Feel the natural curves of your spinal column and remind yourself of the effort it takes to actively stand up tall. Resist gravity and its constant pull to shorten us over time. Breathe deeply and on the next exhalation, bend forward at the hips into a standing forward bend (Uttanasana). Place your hands on the chair seat even if you can easily reach lower. Keep your back straight with no rounding of your lower back. This is a change in how Yoga has been taught over the years but important due to osteopenia or osteoporosis diagnosis that many of us find ourselves facing. It is also an important change for all because we slouch and round our backs so frequently throughout our day, putting unnecessary pressures on the discs, that we do not want to continue that rounding in our Yoga practice. Yoga should be used to counter the wear and tear we accumulate in our normal life.

In your flat-backed Uttanasana, breathe deeply and work the legs straighter if you can do it without rounding. This is a fine line to travel and one that brings benefits to our comfort outside of our practice. From here, bend your knees and move to Chair Pose (Utkatasana) with your hands gently pressed against each other chest height. Hold here for a few breaths then stand up in Tadasana again.

This flow can be repeated 1-100 times a day and your breath will begin to flow with the body as you move. Exhale at any time your body is asked to transition from one pose into another. Stay in each pose, Tadasana, Uttanasana or Utkatasana for as many full breath cycles you have time for, prolonging your exhalation as you move into the next pose in the flow before breathing normally again.

The fluidity of the movements and the brainless flow to it once you get comfortable is remarkably beneficial to your sense of well-being physically, mentally and emotionally.
