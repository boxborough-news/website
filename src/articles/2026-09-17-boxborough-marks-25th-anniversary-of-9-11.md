On Friday, September 11, Boxborough public safety personnel, town officials, and residents gathered at the Fire Station to mark the passage of 25 years since the terrorist attacks of September 11, 2001. The ceremony began with a moment of silence, the Firefighter's Prayer, and a memorial prayer for the victims, first responders, and families affected by the attacks.

Select Board Chair Robert Stemple thanked those who had gathered for the remembrance, and he reflected on the civilians and emergency responders who died in New York, at the Pentagon, and in Shanksville, Pennsylvania. Stemple extended appreciation to the firefighters, police officers, and EMTs who serve the Boxborough community. Town Administrator Michael Johns recalled where he was on Sept. 11, 2001, and spoke about those who ran toward danger to help others. He reflected on the sense of national unity following the attacks.

Following the ceremony, Johns told Boxborough News that he believes "a person is never truly gone as long as they are remembered," and that for this reason, he is "sincerely appreciative that the Town of Boxborough takes the time and makes the effort each year to thoughtfully remember those who perished on that devastating day, those who rushed to save lives despite the danger to their own, and all who have served ever since, to protect the lives of others."

During the closing remarks, Fire Chief John Kivlan noted that some newly minted firefighters were not yet born in 2001. He emphasized the importance of teaching younger generations about the thousands of people who lost their lives in the attacks, as well as the sacrifices of first responders who were present and survived.
