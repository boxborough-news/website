In May of this year, the newly-established Boxborough chapter of the Weed Warriors hosted four "work parties" to remove invasive plants from Patch Hill Conservation Land. The "party" continues with three Fall "work parties," all at Patch Hill beginning at 9 a.m., on Saturday, September 26, Tuesday, September 29, and Tuesday, October 6.

The Boxborough's Weed Warriors chapter is led by Boxborough's Land Stewards Coordinator Dave Barnett and managed by regional land trust organization, Sudbury Valley Trustees (SVT). Barnett explained that the goal of the Weed Warrior program is "to have a unified force of trained volunteers to tackle invasive species management throughout the Sudbury, Assabet and Concord River (SuAsCo) Watershed."

"While a lot of progress was made (in May), we still have much more work to do to get the invasives in this area 'under control'" Barnett told Boxborough News. "While it is impossible to completely eliminate the invasive species, it is very likely that in this area of focus we can get ahead of them and then stay in control with long-term monitoring and continued volunteer work. Our goal is to prevent the continued spread of the invasives further into the Patch Hill woodland because of its significant ecological value."

According to Barnett, "The Patch Hill Conservation Land is one of the Town's largest conservation properties, comprising nearly 220 acres of woodlands. With vernal pools and streams, Patch Hill has great ecological and wildlife habitat value. An extensive network of trails also provides recreational value and access to this important woodland. This area, however, is being threatened in several locations by invasive plant species. The Town is currently working with Oxbow Associates to develop an inventory of plant species and an overall invasive species management plan that will be utilized over the next several years. We are initiating the Weed Warrior program at the trailhead on Liberty Square Road because it has been identified as one of the most threatened locations and of highest priority."

"We have scheduled these work days in coordination with the work being done by Oxbow Associates to selectively treat some of the most dense populations of Asiatic Bittersweet and Burning Bush, and we will continue to coordinate all volunteer activities with the professional guidance of Oxbow and Sudbury Valley Trustees."

"We will be hand pulling and/or digging these species that have spread into the woodland," Barnett wrote. Tools will be provided, but volunteers "are encouraged to bring their own digging tools (spades, mattocks, etc.) and/or lopping or pruning shears." Volunteers are also reminded to wear long pants, closed-toed shoes, and work gloves.

Barnett emphasized that no experience is necessary–training will be provided–but the work party is not recommended for children.

According to Mass Audubon, "all invasive plants were first introduced to our area by humans as landscape specimens, or in some cases, accidentally. Because they did not evolve in our region, the natural mechanisms that normally control these species in their home ranges don't exist. As a result, these non-natives can out-compete, displace, and kill our native species."

When asked whether residents can independently remove invasives from Boxborough conservation land, Barnett replied, "the short answer to this question is that residents should first attend one or more work parties or training sessions, and once their knowledge has been confirmed, they will be allowed and encouraged to remove invasives on their own."

To learn more about the Weed Warrior program or to participate in any of the work party days, contact Land Stewards Coordinator Dave Barnett at <dbarnett@boxborough-ma.gov>.
