At the Boxborough Select Board's September 14 meeting, State Senator Jamie Eldridge and State Representative Dan Sena presented a legislative update and answered questions from Board members and the public.

The conversation ranged from housing, to economic development, to municipal and school funding. Senator Eldridge and Representative Sena also responded to requests from Board members to support specific Boxborough projects – including attracting new businesses, protecting open space, funding the new fire house, pursuing Complete Streets projects, and handling various "unfunded mandates" from the state.

Board member Kristin Hilberg pressed the legislators about the challenges associated with Proposition 2 ½. Proposition 2 ½ limits the amount of revenue a city or town may raise from local property taxes each year to fund municipal operations. Citing the increasing number of municipalities seeking Proposition 2 ½ overrides, Hilberg commented, "If every community is looking at overrides, what's happening above us? Are you thinking about changing Proposition 2 ½?... It's either give us more money or change the way we fund ourselves…it's kind of one or the other…"

In his response, Representative Sena commented that "we have been advocating very strongly and I think there is a consensus among members that we need to revisit this formula…." In earlier comments, Senator Eldridge noted that "over the next three or four years… the Foundation Budget Review Commission [will conduct] all of its hearings and due diligence to change the formula that I do believe will help Acton-Boxborough significantly, but not for a few years."

The Massachusetts Foundation Budget Review Commission was created to review education funding formulas and recommend improvements; a report is due in 2028.

Returning to regular Select Board business, the Board reviewed the twelve articles currently on the draft warrant for Fall Town Meeting on November 16 and voted unanimously to close the warrant.

The articles on the draft warrant include a correction to the MBTA Communities Overlay District; a temporary moratorium on data centers; revisions to the zoning bylaw; funds to design and engineer a "permanent flow control system" related to beaver activity; and a potential purchase of 276 Middle Road.

The Board also voted to recommend adding a warrant article for costs related to joining the Devens Regional Household Hazardous Waste program ("DevensHHW").

In its discussion before this vote, the Select Board noted that it has received public feedback about the difficulty of disposing of hazardous waste in Boxborough, since the town currently hosts a household hazardous waste disposal day once every two years.

Department of Public Works (DPW) Director Ed Kukkula proposed that the town join DevensHHW, which would allow Boxborough residents to dispose of hazardous waste at Devens on 20 dates each year. Kukkula explained that there would be savings associated with joining the program, compared to the town's current hazardous waste disposal system.

The Board voted unanimously to recommend that the town pursue joining DevensHHW and put related FY27 costs on the Fall Town Meeting warrant.
