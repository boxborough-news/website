A local dance studio is settling into a new home in Boxborough after moving from neighboring Bolton, bringing with it longtime students, new opportunities, and a philosophy centered on enjoying dance without the pressure of competition.

Bloom Dance Center, formerly known as Bolton Dance Center, recently moved to 1233 Mass Ave. in Boxborough, just a few minutes from its former location. Owner and teacher Monique Dorian said the studio had been looking for a better space while remaining close enough for its existing students in Harvard, Stow, Bolton, and Lancaster.

The move came somewhat unexpectedly. Dorian said she happened to walk into the plaza where the studio is now located and asked about available space. The owner later contacted her to let her know a space would become available in June. Dorian recalled, "It was just really good timing."

The transition was relatively smooth, in part because the studio was also welcomed by the businesses and neighbors in the plaza. She shared, "The pizza guys next door have been great; the landlord's been great. All the neighbors are really nice to each other."

Bloom Dance Center offers ballet, tap, jazz, modern, and creative movement for younger children, as well as adult classes and dance company training. The studio also hosts birthday parties.

The studio takes a different approach from many dance schools by choosing not to participate in dance competitions. While its dance company provides students with an opportunity for more intensive study, Dorian describes the overall program as recreational.

That does not mean the studio takes training lightly. She said proper technique remains an important part of instruction, both to help dancers develop their skills and to prevent injuries. "I think it should be about the artistry," Dorian explained, adding that she does not want students' enjoyment of dancing to depend on whether they place in a competition.

The philosophy reflects her own extensive background in dance. According to the studio's website, she has been dancing throughout her life and has taught since 2001. She has trained in ballet, modern, jazz, hip-hop, lyrical, and other styles and has taught at dance studios throughout Massachusetts. Dorian was also a guest instructor for Boston Ballet's Summer Dance Workshop.

At Bloom Dance Center, Dorian said she wants students to have a positive experience regardless of their age or experience level. The studio offers adult classes with students ranging from 18 to their 60s, including people returning to dance after years away.

The studio is also welcoming younger dancers. Abby and Lydia, two students at Bloom Dance Center, began attending the studio over the summer after previously dancing elsewhere. The two also participated in the studio's summer camps, and said they enjoy dancing at Bloom.

For families considering classes, Dorian said younger children can try a class before committing to enrollment, which can be especially helpful for children who may be nervous about entering a new classroom. "All are welcome," she stated. "We have a very positive, unintimidating environment."

As Bloom Dance Center becomes established in Boxborough, the studio is also looking for ways to connect with the wider community. The studio plans to have a booth at Blanchard's Fall Fest at the end of October.

Dorian is particularly excited about the new studio's physical space. Its higher ceilings will allow dancers to explore movements such as group lifts that were more difficult to accommodate in the previous location. Ultimately, however, her hopes for Bloom Dance Center are less about expansion than becoming a lasting part of the community.

"I think we need more dance and more arts right now," she said. "I just hope it stays for a long time and we provide that for the community."

Bloom Dance Center is located at 1233 Massachusetts Avenue in Boxborough. More information about classes and enrollment is available through the studio, which can be reached at 978-835-5665.
