On Wednesday, September 23, the Boxborough Planning Board held a "preliminary hearing" on the town's fire station construction project.

The Planning Board held no votes but provided preliminary feedback to the fire station design team. The Board expects to issue written feedback to the applicant at their October 5 meeting. Planning Board member Mark White recused himself at the start of the hearing and spoke only as an abutter.

The hearing began with the design team at Context Architecture ("Context") offering a brief presentation on the fire station design and site plan including driveways and parking, floor plans, building elevations, and landscaping. Project engineers from Fuss & O'Neill also gave an overview of the stormwater management system, wells, septic system, and "tight tank."

Town Planner Alec Wade then outlined the three anticipated variance and waiver requests related to the height of the training tower, the visitor parking dimensions, and the curb-cut widths.

After the presentation, Planning Board Chair Rich Guzzardi invited comments and questions from the Board and the public.

Board member questions focused on the height of the training tower and visual impact, decontamination systems, amount of impervious surface, the width of the "curb cut" onto Sara's Way, and tree planting. Members of the public commented on many of the same issues. Two abutters also spoke and requested an "abutter walkthrough," which the Town Administrator agreed to schedule.

The town is expected to make their formal application for Site Plan Approval on October 19. The first of three planned public hearings is scheduled for November 18.

At the Fire Station Building Committee (FSBC) meeting the following evening, September 24, Chris Logan of Context noted that the feedback from the Planning Board members and the public was very helpful and that "we know what we need to do to put a good package together [for the Planning Board application]."

FSBC members discussed several of the issues that came up at the Planning Board hearing, including the training tower and decontamination operations.

Fire Chief John Kivlan, who was not able to attend the Planning Board hearing, clarified that the proposed tower structure is primarily a training tool; although it would also be used to dry hoses, the ability to train on site "is a very important tool for us." Chief Kivlan also offered more information about current decontamination practices, explaining that water involved in decontamination processes is disposed of through a "tight tank," which does not feed into the septic system.

Context will host a "public design update" open to anyone interested in learning more about the fire station project on Wednesday, September 30 at 7 p.m. at Town Hall.
