At its meeting on September 15, the Boxborough Finance Committee (FinCom) reviewed year-end results for Fiscal Year 2026 (FY26) and warrant articles for the upcoming Fall Town Meeting (November 16).

FinCom Chair Tony Newton reported that the town ended FY2026 with $1,464,056 under budget. Those funds will eventually flow into free cash, which can be used by the town to fund future warrant articles. As yet, free cash has not been certified by the Department of Local Services, a division of the Department of Revenue.

When reached for comment after the meeting, Town Administrator Michael Johns explained that the town underspent the budget by $852,245 due to unfilled positions in the Police and Fire departments and, to a lesser extent, at Town Hall. Additionally, health insurance benefits were $515,377 lower than budgeted because several employees opted out of the Town's health insurance plan. On the revenue side, local receipts, which include motor vehicle excise taxes, investment income, hotel/motel taxes, fees, licenses/permits, rentals and charges, came in at 172% higher than projected due to continued growth in existing and new small businesses.

Johns said he is "very confident" that the town will not need an operating budget override for the upcoming fiscal year if a number of factors align with his financial projections.

In other matters, FinCom heard reports from Director of Land Use Alec Wade, Planning Board Chair Rich Guzzardi, and Agricultural Committee (Ag Com) Vice Chair John Neyland with respect to warrant articles for Fall Town Meeting. Those articles included: a correction to the MBTA Communities Overlay District; a temporary moratorium on data centers; revisions to the zoning bylaw; funds to design and engineer a "permanent flow control system" related to beaver activity; and a possible purchase of property at 276 Middle Road. FinCom did not take any votes on the warrant articles.

Much of the discussion centered around funding to design and engineer a "permanent control flow" system relative to beaver lodges at the Hager bridge and at a second 25-year-old bridge upstream. FinCom members expressed concern about undertaking a study at the cost of $25,000, when the ultimate cost of fixing the problem was unknown. Wade said he would work with engineers to get an estimate of a projected cost and update FinCom before they had to vote on the warrant article.

There was also considerable discussion around the purchase of 18.5 acres of land including a house, barn and garage at 276 Middle Road. Wade explained that the land abuts Hager land and a "deep network of conservation trails." Purchasing the property would allow the town to "restore the land to an active agricultural site." Neyland said the property, currently appraised at $1,475,000, has always been a high priority for the town. To help offset the cost of the property, Ag Com reached out to the Boxborough Conservation Trust and the Sudbury Valley Trust and plans to submit an application to the Community Preservation Committee for a grant of $300,000.

Neyland said, "as a town volunteer, our job is to be stewards for the town – not for one year or five years, but protecting the town for 500 years." He added, "once the land is gone, it's gone forever."

The next FinCom meeting is scheduled for Tuesday, September 29 at 7 p.m. The meeting is hybrid.
