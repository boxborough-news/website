For many people, October means apples and pumpkins, but in Boxborough it also means that Diwalifest is coming. This is the fifth year of the annual celebration, which will be held on Sunday, October 18, from noon to 4:30 p.m. at the Sargent Memorial Library. The rain date is October 25, but organizer Deepika Prakash is manifesting great weather for October 18.

“This is our fifth year, and as usual we will have food from local vendors, music, dancing, and a full day of activities…plus a few new things,” says Prakash. The traditional events will happen as expected, such as the Diya lighting, the Rangoli contest, and the incredible performances.

But this year, the festival will also include a games corner for teens (on the library patio area), 17 food vendors, and folk Indian music instead of the classical music that is normally featured. “This is a community celebration,” says Prakash, “and we want to reach out to some parts of the community that we haven’t reached as well before.”

This year’s celebration will also include a story reading at The Silver Unicorn—a festival first.

Prakash notes that the celebration has always been all-inclusive. The festival features many Indian cultural items, of course, thanks to the support of cultural programmer Arpita Das Pathak of Littleton. But there are many non-Indian aspects that also reflect the community festival of lights.

Past events have featured a local rock band, a student photographer, and a local event planner. Working with local businesses, especially ones that are just starting out, and including a variety of vendors and cuisines helps bring people together, explained Prakash. Visitors come from all around, especially Boxborough, Acton, and Littleton. The recurring theme is community.

As usual, the celebration is supported by the Boxborough Fire Department for emergency services, the Boxborough Police Department for traffic control, and the Department of Public Works for such logistics as trash cans. “Big lesson learned from year one—when you have a big crowd and a lot of food vendors, there is an enormous amount of trash” Prakash said.

The celebration also depends heavily on volunteer support, but Prakash is happy to report there is no shortage of that. “(Volunteers) have to empty all the trash cans and then clean up the grass area at the end of the day,” she explains. “After the first year, I learned never to have confetti again!”

The primary partner for Diwalifest is the Sargent Memorial Library, which provides the celebration space. Library Director Peishan Bartley beams about the celebration. “Deepika is incredible, and the celebration just keeps growing! We have talked about looking for a larger space, such as Flerra Meadows,” says Bartley, “but Deepika loves the cozy nature of the library space. It’s nice that it is so walkable with the new sidewalk, and that it is in the center of town instead of off in a corner.”

Although there is not nearly enough parking at the library, Deepika has arranged for parking at Blanchard Memorial School, Middlesex Savings Bank, the Town Hall, and the UCC church. Visitors can walk from any of these locations, but there will also be shuttle service. “Look for the Diwalifest parking signs, but please drive carefully when you are parking—there are a lot of young pedestrians who attend,” Prakash advises.

“Come join the fun on October 18 and celebrate our multi-cultural community. Bring your appetite, your family, your friends, and a lawn chair, if you want. Most people sit on the grass, especially on the hill, but during the music and dance performances some people like to have a lawn chair,” says Prakash. “I’m fine with it—whatever it takes to make this a community celebration!” Full details are available on the Diwalifest website: [https://www.diwalifest.net/home](https://www.diwalifest.net/home)
