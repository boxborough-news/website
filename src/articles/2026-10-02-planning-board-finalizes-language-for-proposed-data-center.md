At its meeting on Wednesday, September 23, the Boxborough Planning Board continued its public hearing to consider the proposed bylaw to place a temporary moratorium on data centers in town.

Currently, the Boxborough Zoning Bylaw does not address “data centers” as a use, nor does it define the term “data center.” The proposed bylaw would add a “data center” definition and temporarily prohibit its use across all zoning districts until December 2028.

Planning Board Clerk Chris Dowdy, the sponsor of the proposed bylaw, told Boxborough News “Our first priority was to establish a moratorium because it is clear that larger, hyperscale data centers would not be a good fit for Boxborough. We have low cost municipal electricity, and some open spaces without current development, so there seemed a potential risk. Large data centers can have significant environmental impact, including serious impact on local aquifers.

While we know the town has little appetite for a larger data center, we wanted to avoid unintended consequences to existing businesses in the town by being too broad. A moratorium will give us time to carefully consider how to craft a bylaw that avoids unintended consequences and clearly defines what is (and is not) allowed.”

During a prior public hearing on August 31, the board focused on electrical usage as a defining parameter for data centers rather than the amount of space one would occupy.

Dowdy wrote, “(we) reached out to LELWD to anonymously get usage data for the top ten power users in Boxborough. They supplied 2025 data with the peak user coming in at almost 4,000 kVA. We originally thought to use a power usage line approximately 20 percent above our current highest user (to allow for some growth). That is how we came up with the 5,000 kVA limit in the earlier draft language.”

Board members discussed the power usage parameter in depth during the September 23 hearing and heard comments from a few members of the public, who suggested that the usage limit should be lower than 5,000 kVA. In the final language voted by the Board (5 - 0), the electrical load threshold was removed to avoid legal ambiguity.

Dowdy explained the rationale for striking the electrical usage from the definition. “... Adding power usage to the definition could create a loophole for something we did not want. With a moratorium, the simplest solution made the most sense. We struck the power requirement from the definition to match many other (Massachusetts) towns enacting similar moratoriums, and will leave the more rigorous definitions for the full bylaw after careful review.

If this article passes, we will have two years to carefully consider if something small might make sense, and how to clearly define what might be acceptable. (I’m) looking forward to more public input as we continue the process.”

Following the public hearing, Boxborough News reached out to Town Planner Alec Wade who wrote, “I commend the board for its hard work to date. I believe a moratorium was the right path for Boxborough. While many may be ready to write off this Use Code entirely, I think ample due diligence before out-zoning any use is always the best course of action. I look forward to Town Meeting, collecting public feedback, and the work still to come.”
