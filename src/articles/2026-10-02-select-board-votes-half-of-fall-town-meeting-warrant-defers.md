At their meeting on September 28, the Boxborough Select Board walked through the draft warrant for Fall Town Meeting to vote their recommendations. Ultimately, the Board tabled votes on about half of the warrant articles, citing a need for more information.

The Board chose to postpone votes on a proposed data center moratorium; design and engineering funding for a “permanent flow control system” to address beaver activity; the proposed purchase of property at 276 Middle Road; a proposed change to per diem inspector fees; and funding related to the AFSCME municipal employees union. The Board is expected to vote these articles at their next meeting on Wednesday, October 14.

The Board voted unanimously in support of the MBTA Communities article; an article to join the Devens Regional Household Hazardous Products collection center; an article about the effect of military service on public employee salaries; and several housekeeping articles. Fall Town Meeting will take place on Monday, November 16.

The Board also discussed but took no action on a Climate Action Plan (CAP) presented by Associate Town Planner Ian Gilson on behalf of the Sustainability Committee and Office of Land Use and Permitting.

Gilson explained that the CAP is meant to be a “guiding document” to help the town address challenges related to climate change. The CAP includes ten recommendations in five focus areas: water conservation; sustainable transportation; waste management; energy goals and opportunities; and climate resilience and preparedness. Each recommendation has a series of “action items” associated with it.

Select Board members were hesitant to “approve” or “adopt” the CAP. Member Kristin Hilberg explained that while she supports and appreciates the Sustainability Committee’s efforts, she does not necessarily support or agree with everything in the 100-page CAP document. The Board asked Gilson to bring back a smaller number of priority items that the town could focus on.

In other business, the Board unanimously approved a proposal from Town Assessor Lynda Cajka to change the town’s “Abutters List” fee from a price-per-name variable fee to a flat fee. An abutters list is used to notify abutters as required by state or local law. Cajka proposed a $25 flat fee. After discussion and comparison to fees in surrounding towns, the Board voted on a flat fee of $35.
