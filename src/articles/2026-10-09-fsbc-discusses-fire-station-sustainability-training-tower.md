At their meeting on October 8, the Fire Station Building Committee (FSBC) tackled two topics that have been the subject of much public comment in recent months: the sustainability measures that will be incorporated into the fire station building and the 50-foot “training tower” that is currently part of the building design.

Chris Logan of Context Architecture (“Context”) presented a “Sustainability Measures Memo” that differentiated between the 28 sustainability measures that are already incorporated into the scope and cost of the project versus six “add-on” sustainability measures that would represent an added cost.

The FSBC discussed each of the six “add-on” items. They voted to move forward with one, not move forward with three, and defer two for a later decision.

Fire Chief John Kivlan then addressed recent questions around the necessity of a 50-foot training tower in the building design, which has recently come under some scrutiny by members of the Planning Board and the public.

Kivlan made the case for a tower’s “operational value” to the Boxborough Fire Department, explaining how the tower would allow for “needed” training opportunities that the department is not currently able to provide for its firefighters due to the costs associated with off-site training. Kivlan explained that the tower would also be used for hose drying/maintenance, which the department is currently handling in makeshift fashion.

Kivlan invited any residents with questions about the tower to call or email him directly, saying “I can go on and on about the value of this…” Several FSBC members indicated their support for the Chief’s position and the necessity of the training tower, and FSBC Chair Mary Brolin asked the Chief to be present at the Planning Board’s upcoming public hearings.

The town is expected to submit an application to the Planning Board for Site Plan Approval on October 19. The first of three planned public hearings is scheduled for November 18.

Context also presented a brief fire station design update that included interior and exterior renderings of the building. Context asked for FSBC feedback on exterior color options; the consensus was “simple and classic,” with a preference for neutrals over red elements.

The FSBC’s next meeting is scheduled for Thursday, October 22.
