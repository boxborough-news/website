Sergeant Brett Pelley, currently the longest serving police officer in the Town of Boxborough, retires after 33 years of continuous service in law enforcement (30 years in Boxborough).

On Wednesday, October 7, the Boxborough Police Department sent Pelley off with the ceremonial “last call” tribute where dispatch broadcasts a solemn sign-off over the airwaves to honor his years of service in Boxborough. Eighty residents, town employees, and police officers from twelve towns across Massachusetts attended the emotional ceremony.

Sergeant Pelley started his career in law enforcement in 1993 as a dispatcher in Westford, MA. From there, he went to the police academy and was hired in Boxborough in 1996 as a part-time officer which led to a full-time position.

Growing up, Pelley said he used to visit his godfather at the Massachusetts State Police barracks, and those visits were the catalyst for his decision to pursue law enforcement. Pelley said “I can’t remember a time when I wanted to do anything else.”

Boxborough News asked Pelley what he would have done if he hadn’t pursued law enforcement as a career. Pelley laughed, “The guys at the Fire Department will love this…” saying he would have been “a firefighter or an attorney.”

Police work involves everything from wellness checks to traffic control to drug busts, vandalism, burglaries, and even helping stranded drivers change a flat tire. Pelley has seen it all and done it all.

As a law enforcement officer, Pelley said his top priority has been to provide fair, impartial and consistent policing. “I believe every person should be treated with dignity and respect, regardless of their background, circumstances, or personal beliefs. I have focused on enforcing the law objectively, making decisions based on facts and circumstances rather than assumptions or personal opinions and maintaining professionalism is every interaction.”

After a temporary assignment working with the State Police on a local field crash investigation, Pelley realized that accident reconstruction was something he wanted to do on a regular basis. He undertook training in the field and became an expert in accident reconstruction, consulting for police departments across central Massachusetts. He said he felt good about his work in accident reconstruction investigations because it gave families some measure of comfort when they had a clear understanding of what happened to their loved ones.

Lieutenant Steven Patriarca said, “Brett is truly a cop’s cop. He is the kind of person who would do anything for his fellow officers, and his dedication to the job and the people he serves has always been evident.”

Sergeant Max Bressi said “Aside from my own father, Brett is the single greatest influence in my life. He is the type of supervisor who makes you want to be a better cop, not because he asks you to, but because his example inspires you to be. His dedication, professionalism, and commitment to his peers, has left a lasting impression on everyone fortunate enough to work alongside him. I am incredibly grateful for everything he has taught me, both as a police officer and as a person.”

At the ceremony, Boxborough News spoke with several residents who expressed their gratitude for Pelley’s service to the town. One relayed a situation in which his home had been burglarized and Pelley answered the call. “He was kind, professional and made us feel comfortable” in a stressful situation. Another resident summed up the feelings of many: “He will be missed…and we wish him the very best in a well-earned retirement.”

For Pelley, retirement means spending more time with Sherry, his wife of 21 years, his three adult children and two grandchildren. He plans to play a lot of golf and spend time boating at Lake Winnipesaukee. There’s also a Disney vacation for the whole family in the very near future.

While Pelley is officially retiring, he’ll still be around town, working as a reserve officer at events like Fifers Day or the UCC Boxborough’s Merrie Christmas Fair.

Boxborough News asked Pelley for his final thoughts about his service in Boxborough. He said, “Thank you. It has been a privilege to serve this community and to work alongside so many residents, families, town employees and fellow officers who care deeply about Boxborough. Law enforcement is built on relationships and trust, and I am grateful for the support, conversations and experiences that have shaped my time here.”
