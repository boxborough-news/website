Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, and Town Clerk Rebecca (Becky) Harris is prepared.

Harris has updated the town’s website, outlining important dates regarding voter registration, voting by mail, and early voting ([HERE](https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/211/Elections-Voting)). She has also posted a link to a sample ballot ([HERE](https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/DocumentCenter/View/6942/BOXBOROUGH_ENG_439_ICP-SAMPLE-PDF?bidId=)).

In addition to candidates running for statewide office, this year’s ballot contains nine questions. Last month, Secretary of the Commonwealth William F. Galvin’s office mailed a booklet containing detailed information on each of the nine questions to all registered voters. Hard copies of the information booklet are available at Town Hall and also online ([HERE](https://ma.omniballot.us/sites/25/vg/app/vig/voter-guide/info)).

In the booklet, the Secretary informs voters that all nine questions are binding: “Questions 1 - 8 on your ballot are laws proposed by initiative petition. Voters are asked if they approve of the proposed law. If passed by the voters, these proposals would become law…Question 9 on your ballot is a referendum on an existing law. Voters are asked if they approve of the existing law. If a majority vote “yes,” the law will remain in place. If a majority vote “no,” the law will be repealed.”

Harris wants voters to be prepared. “The ballot is two pages (front and back), so it will be important for people doing mail-in voting to return both pages in the brown envelope, which must be signed.” She also wants to remind voters that they can drop their mail-in ballots at Town Hall, either in the ballot dropbox next to the door or at the Town Clerk's office.

The two-page ballot may lengthen the amount of time it takes to tabulate the results, Harris told Boxborough News, but what consistently slows down the tallying and results reporting are the write-ins.

Filling in the ‘write-in’ oval and then not writing anyone in or writing in fictional characters, statements, or non-eligible candidates, like children, really slows down the process," Harris explained.

“It's not that I don't want people to have a voice, but when the counters are trying to decipher each thing that is written in and have to decide whether it gets counted as a write-in or a blank (‘all of the above’ are counted as blanks),” it takes time.

On Tuesday, November 3, polls will be open from 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. at Town Hall. For additional information on the election and voting schedule, visit the town’s website: ([https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/211/Elections-Voting](https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/211/Elections-Voting)).

Fun fact: This is not the first time that a Massachusetts ballot has had nine questions. Ballots in 1972, 1976, and 1994 also had nine questions. To view the history of Massachusetts ballot questions from 1919 to 2024, visit the Secretary of the Commonwealth’s website [HERE.](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/elections/research-and-statistics/balmresults-open.htm#dates)
