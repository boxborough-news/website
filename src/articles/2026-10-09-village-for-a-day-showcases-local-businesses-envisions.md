A bright and sunny Saturday was the perfect backdrop for the second “Village for a Day” event sponsored by the Boxborough Economic Development Committee (EDC). The event helped to promote local businesses and helped residents imagine what a walkable town center could be like.

The event took place across three locations – Town Hall, the UCC Church lawn, and the Middlesex Bank plaza – and Alpha Cars and The Art Studio welcomed visitors and conducted open houses at their respective Mass Ave locations.

People traveled by foot between the sites, with a steady stream of visitors at each location. At noon, the Boxborough Minutemen Company mustered in front of Town Hall, fired their muskets, and marched down the Mass Ave sidewalk to the Middlesex Bank plaza, where they performed another ceremonial musket fire.

Behind Town Hall, a mini golf course was set up, with the holes and their prizes sponsored by local businesses. The UCC Church lawn hosted booths from town committees and local organizations, including the Boxborough Conservation Trust, the Boxborough Emergency Reserve Corps, Danny’s Place, and many more.

Hungry attendees could visit Craft Food Halls’ booth and buy a wrap and a drink or wander over to Oscar’s Burritos, where owners Oscar and Dalia Garcia were selling their signature burritos. For those with a sweet tooth, the Boxborough Regency provided free cookies.

Outside the UCC Church, visitors were treated to live music by guitarist and singer Sara Rice and steel drum player Bob Lucas of the Pan Loco Steel Band.

Down the road, businesses who operate in the Middlesex Bank Plaza also participated. Two Friends Chocolates and Thinking Flower Bakery held open houses, while Gina’s Dance Studio, Pattern Review /Threaded, and Middlesex Bank engaged with attendees outside in the parking lot, providing activities for adults and kids alike.

C'est la Fête!, a Boxborough-based catering company, showcased their services and provided free samples of tarts and mini cupcakes, while the owners of Clean Slate chatted with visitors about their home organization and decluttering services.

On the south side of the parking lot, Dephy, Inc., whose corporate office is in Boxborough, encouraged visitors to test out their product, the SidekickTM, a wearable ex oskeleton designed to make walking easier. Keeping with the “movement” theme, Adaptive Fitness & Movement, a special needs fitness training facility co-owned and directed by Boxborough resident Andree Proulx, provided information about their programs and offered visitors a chance to play a tic-tac-toe bean bag game.

When asked how the day was going, Town Planner Alec Wade said “I'm really excited to see different people, different ages, different family types out here walking around and enjoying different features. Some people are stopping for the food, some kids enjoying mini golf, and that's our vision for it. We want a village center that provides a variety of resources for different people and different groups.”

The Boxborough EDC staffed tables at both locations, meeting with residents and soliciting their ideas on what they wanted in a town center. The committee also “soft launched” an online survey to gather input for the town’s economic development plan. The survey can be found at [mapc.ma/boxborough](https://mapc.az1.qualtrics.com/jfe/form/SV_3w9zuOKazhhhtNI).

EDC Chair Abby Reip said “Residents have consistently voiced their desire for a walkable village center, and the Village for a Day event is, essentially, an exercise to help residents envision and experience what it would be like. The EDC is continuing to gather information from residents and local business owners and explore the types of changes that may be necessary, like zoning, dimensional requirements, parking, etc., to make a walkable village center a reality.”

Many more photos from the day are posted on the EDC's website at [https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/404/Economic-Development-Committee.](https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/404/Economic-Development-Committee)
