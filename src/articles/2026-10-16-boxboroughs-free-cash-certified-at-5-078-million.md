The Boxborough Finance Committee (FinCom) opened its October 6 meeting with a presentation about free cash from Finance Director Honghoa Le.

Free Cash is a term that refers to unrestricted funds remaining from the prior year’s operations.

Le reported that the Massachusetts Department of Revenue, Division of Local Services certified Boxborough’s free cash at $5.078 million. The increase in free cash is due in large part from underspending the fiscal year 2026 budget by $1,464,056 and underestimating revenues by $1.5 million, for a total of $2.9 million. The remaining balance comes from free cash from prior years.

Town Administrator Michael Johns previously explained to Boxborough News that town government under-spent the budget by $852K due to unfilled positions in the police and fire departments and to a lesser extent at Town Hall. Additionally, the cost of health insurance benefits was $515K lower than expected. Those two budget items account for 93% of the under-spent budget. Assistant Town Administrator Rajon Hudson said that now that the town has a clearer picture of the cost of health insurance, that line item will be reduced in next year’s budget.

FinCom members expressed concern that the town was taxing residents higher than necessary and agreed that budgeting needed to be tighter, with less of a cushion.

On the revenue side, local receipts, which includes motor vehicle excise taxes, investment income, hotel/motel taxes, fees, licenses/permits, cell tower rental income, and charges for services, such as ambulance services and transfer station fees, came in $1 million higher than projected.

FinCom member Michelle Ryan wanted to know what was driving the increase in revenues and whether that was sustainable. Both Ryan and member Maria Neyland asked for a detailed line-by-line accounting of revenues from last year to understand the source of the revenue.

The town makes a number of assumptions when building a budget. Two of those assumptions are revenues and new growth. “New growth” is defined as property values that increase due to new construction, including new buildings, additions, or major renovations to existing properties; newly taxed parcels that were previously exempt; and parcels taxed as separate entities for the first time, like new subdivisions and condominium conversions.

FinCom Vice Chair John Connor posed the question, what if revenues come in lower than projected and there are insufficient funds to pay expenses at any time during the budget year? Other FinCom members explained that in the case of “extraordinary and unforeseen expenditures,” the town could use the reserve fund, which represents .6% of the annual budget. (This year, that fund started at $189,000). Or, since the town cannot change the tax rate mid-cycle to raise emergency funds, the Select Board would have to call a Special Town Meeting and ask for funding through either free cash or the stabilization fund.

Connor presented a preliminary five-year budget forecast using the Acton Boxborough Regional School District (ABRSD) assessment projection of 6.22% and the Town Administrator’s projection of a 5.5% increase in the town’s budget. Based on those numbers, Connor projected the preliminary excess levy limit capacity for the next fiscal year at $294K. Connor explained that that number will likely change once ABRSD provides the town with the debt exclusion amount. Debt exclusion refers to funds that were bonded for school capital projects and are “excluded” from the levy limit.

FinCom members Ryan, Neyland and Joe Stulpin were uncomfortable with $294K as the excess levy limit. Stulpin said going from $1M in excess levy capacity last year to $300K next year is “a big jump that could blow up in our face.” Ryan and Neyland both said they would be more comfortable with the excess levy limit closer to $700K to $800K but would consider a lower excess levy limit if they had a clearer picture of revenues.

Turning to the upcoming Fall Town Meeting, FinCom heard from Director of Land Use and Permitting Alec Wade and Vice-Chair of the Agricultural Commission (Ag Com) John Neyland, regarding the purchase of property at 276 Middle Road for $1.8 million.

Wade and Neyland explained that their goal is to lock in alternative funding sources prior to the town meeting scheduled for November 16 and reduce the cost to taxpayers to between $800K and $900K.

Ag Com filed an application for $300K with the Community Preservation Committee (CPC), which has scheduled a special hearing for November 5 to consider the application. If the application is approved by both CPC and Town Meeting, the funds would become available July 1, 2027 (fiscal year 2028). Additionally, Ag Com is working with the Sudbury Valley Trust and the Boxborough Conservation Trust for additional contributions.

FinCom Chair Tony Newton asked about the optics of a land and property purchase in a year when the town will ask for a debt exclusion for a Fire Station. FinCom members Ryan, Stulpin and Neyland believe that if the town can reduce the cost of the land purchase, the optics would be good because the land would be used for agriculture. FinCom members voted 4-1 to recommend the article at Town Meeting.
