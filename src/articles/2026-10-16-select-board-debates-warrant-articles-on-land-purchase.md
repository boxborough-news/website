At their October 14 meeting, the Select Board held a spirited discussion of two warrant articles for Fall Town Meeting, scheduled for November 16: Article 4, the purchase of property at 276 Middle Road for $1.8 million, and Article 7, which asks for $25,000 for a “flow control system” at the Hager Bridge to address flooding caused by beaver activity.

Ultimately, the Board voted 3-1-1 in favor of the 276 Middle Road purchase and voted unanimously in favor of the Hager Bridge “flow control” funding.

Town Planner Alec Wade presented the status of the potential land purchase, explaining various alternative funding sources that are expected to bring the cost to taxpayers down to about $900,000. This includes Community Preservation Act funding, Boxborough Conservation Trust donations, and a Sudbury Valley Trust contribution.

Wade and Agricultural Commission (AgCom) Vice Chair John Neyland also explained to the Board the AgCom’s vision for land: an arrangement where the town will lease out the land and existing farmhouse as a “working farmstead” to promote farming and create a revenue stream for the town.

Select Board Clerk Sam Anderson expressed his support for the land purchase and the possibility of a leasing arrangement, but asked Wade to confirm that the town would still have the option to sell off some of the land if the “farmstead” vision becomes “a money pit for the town.” Wade confirmed that the town will preserve that option.

Select Board member Priya Sundaram stated that she is opposed to the land purchase because “the timing is not right.” She voted against Article 4, saying “I personally cannot support this…because of the timing and the amount of money involved.”

The land purchase will require a two-thirds vote at Fall Town Meeting.

Board member Kristin Hilberg recused herself from the Article 4 discussion, as she represents the family that is selling the land.

Regarding the Hager Bridge, Select Board members discussed how the proposal in the warrant article had changed since they last reviewed it. Previously, the article asked for design-only funding to address a large 25-year-old beaver dam near Blanchard Memorial School.

The current iteration of Article 7 limits the scope of the project to the Hager Bridge, downstream from the dam. Wade is now requesting funding for “design, engineering services, and construction” to address only the flooding of the Hager Bridge. Planner Wade explained why and how his proposal had changed, in part due to community pushback on the original scope of the project.

Board members shared conflicting opinions about the change in scope, with at least one member preferring to tackle the larger dam project as contemplated by the original version of Article 7, and at least one member questioning the necessity of the revised project at Hager Bridge. Eventually all voted in favor of the current iteration of Article 7, the Hager Bridge project.

The Board also voted unanimously to recommend a Planning Board article to implement a temporary data center moratorium and voted unanimously to recommend an article changing inspector compensation that had been recently endorsed by the Personnel Board.

With their recommendations complete, the Board approved the final warrant and authorized printing and mailing. Fall Town Meeting is scheduled for Monday, November 16 at 7 p.m. in the Blanchard Memorial School Gymnasium.
