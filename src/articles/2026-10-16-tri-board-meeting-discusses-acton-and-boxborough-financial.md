The Acton and Boxborough Select Boards, Finance Committees, and the Acton-Boxborough Regional School District (ABRSD) Committee met on Tuesday, October 13 to discuss the financial challenges each town and the school district face in the upcoming budget year.

Superintendent Peter Light presented the preliminary budget for ABRSD. Overall, the budget is projected at a 4.47% increase over last year. Light described the budget as a “maintenance services budget.” The budget drivers include increases in health insurance at 15-16% per year over the next three years, personnel costs, and costs associated with special education. The assessment to Boxborough is projected at $18.8M, a 5.96% increase over last year. Acton’s assessment is similarly projected at a 5.97% increase because of stabilizing student enrollment. Previously, Acton’s student enrollment was going down, while Boxborough’s enrollment was going up. That is no longer the case.

The ABRSD budget does not include capital expenses, such as the HVAC units that need to be replaced at the high school, at a cost of approximately $8M. The district plans to use some portion of the “excess and deficiency” reserve funds to cover those expenses. The “excess and deficiency” fund functions similarly to “free cash.”

Light said the biggest risk to the school district is meeting the state requirements to provide 990 hours of instructional time for high school students, which may necessitate hiring additional teachers. The administration is exploring other options that may avoid adding staff.

State aid is also a concern for the district. ABRSD Committee Chair Tori Campbell explained that the district no longer receives automatic increases in state education aid through the Chapter 70 formula. Instead, the district receives “minimum aid,” described as a baseline level of state funding. Any increase above that baseline depends on the state budget each year. In the meantime, expenses like healthcare, salaries, and pensions continue to increase.

Campbell also addressed the school committee’s concern around Ballot Question 5, one of nine questions on this year’s statewide election ballot. If passed, Question 5 would change how Massachusetts calculates limits on state tax revenues. The new formula would include revenue from the “Fair Share Amendment,” which currently can only be used for education and transportation. When “Fair Share” revenue is included in the formula, state revenue limits would be reached sooner and would reduce discretionary state revenue available for education and other priorities. Less state revenue means greater pressure on local property owners to fund the same essential services.

Turning to Boxborough, John Connor, Finance Committee (FinCom) Vice Chair, presented preliminary projections for the Town’s levy limit, but indicated that FinCom needed to firm up projections from the town administrator and finance team before the town can fully understand what the budget will look like. Connor said that “estimates will tighten up as the budget develops.”

Alissa Nicol, Acton Select Board Chair, presented the preliminary budget for the Town of Acton, where expenses exceed revenues by $3,067,643. Nicol said that health insurance, salaries, and pensions continue to be the budget drivers and that no decisions have been made yet on how to handle the $3M budget gap. Nicol explained that the only options are to cut budgets or use some portion of the “untaxed levy” of $1.2M. The untaxed levy was part of the override voted by tax payers in 2024, but was set aside for future use.

Nicol said that over the years, the town side of the Acton budget has gone from 37% down to 33.7%, while the combined school budget (ABRSD and Minuteman Vocational school) has gone from 63.3% of the budget to 67%. To account for the reduced expenditures, the Town of Acton has deferred hiring, re-organized departments, reduced or eliminated programs, reduced spending on incidentals, and pursued grants to manage budget gaps.
