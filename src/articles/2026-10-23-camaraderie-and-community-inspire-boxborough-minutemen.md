Ceremonies and celebrations in Boxborough almost always include the Boxborough District Minutemen Company. With their tricorn hats and replica 18th century muskets, the omnipresent Minutemen are part of Boxborough’s identity.

Marching and muskets aside, the Boxborough Minutemen Company is “not a reenactment company,” says Minutemen Past Captain Rich Grady. “We march in parades and other events, but we don't do battle reenactments, which have strict criteria for uniforms, equipment, grooming, and training.”

Established in 1967 “to perpetuate the memory of the Minutemen of 1775 and their historic fight for the cause of liberty and justice,” the Boxborough District Minutemen Company is “devoted to a variety of community service activities,” Grady told Boxborough News, including “running the town's annual Fifer's  Day celebration; picking up trash along Mass. Ave. (Route 111\) twice a year; Christmas tree pickup and disposal; supporting the local Boy Scouts; serving at various suppers and lunches; helping with FreeBee Market and other things too numerous to mention.” Grady added that the Minutemen also have social events throughout the year for “camaraderie and entertainment.”

The Company also awards annual scholarships to Boxborough students at the Acton-Boxborough Regional High School and provides financial donations to a variety of community projects.

Grady told Boxborough News that there are “60 members on (the) roster. Some (members) are more active than others. Not everyone marches, and it is not a requirement that they do.” Many members have been with the Company for 30 to 40 years, Grady said.

Grady recalls that “in the 1990s, entire neighborhoods of men were joining, but in recent years, the pace of new members joining has slowed down.” Membership opened to women in 2014, and several women have joined since then. Grady says, “We would like to recruit more men and women to keep the organization growing and vibrant with new energy.”

The Company meets monthly at the Boxborough Community Center.  Anyone aged 18 or older is welcome to join.

At its most recent meeting on October 18, the Company elected new officers to its executive board. Boxborough News reached out to the newly elected leaders, Captain Tim Blankenship and Lieutenant Nathan Soule, to congratulate them and learn what inspires their membership.

**Captain Tim Blankenship:**

**1\. This is your second time as captain. Has the role changed since you last held it (2014)?**   
I was first Captain the year we admitted women for full membership. It was quite an honor to be part of that. Since my first term, we have moved to a more of a community service organization rather than just parades and Fifers Day. Our former Captain Tony Newton has played a huge role in this. Of course, we still love our Parades, though\!

**2\. When did you first join the Minutemen? And what was it that made you want to join?**  
I first joined the Minuteman in 1997 so will be celebrating my 30th year next year. I was asked to join by a colleague of my wife's and fellow Monday night basketball player. Personal invitations are still the best way for people to feel welcomed and how most people join.  
>   
**3\. What do you like about being a member of the Company?**  
The best part of being a Minuteman is the good we can do for the community as well as the camaraderie with our fellow members. Even the parades are a lot of fun, though my uniform is a bit more snug than it was.

**Lieutenant Nathan Soule:**

**1\. What are you looking forward to the most while serving as lieutenant?**

I'm looking forward to taking the next step in helping the Company continue to thrive. I've served in a variety of eBoard roles since 2019, and becoming Lieutenant is both a new challenge and an exciting chapter. One of the things I'm most excited about is learning from Tim over the next year, then mentoring the next Lieutenant after me.

**2\. When did you first join the Minutemen? And what was it that made you want to join?**

My father was a member of the Company throughout the 1980s and 1990s, so in many ways I feel like I grew up with the Minutemen. After moving back to Boxborough in 2017, I became an official member so joining felt like a natural way to reconnect with a tradition I had known for much of my life while becoming more involved in the local community.

**3\. What do you like about being a member of the Company?**

The Company feels like an extended family. We celebrate each other's milestones while also supporting one another through difficult times. At the same time, we're connected by a shared mission of serving our community while honoring and preserving our local history.
