At its meeting on October 22, the Boxborough Fire Station Building Committee (FSBC) voted unanimously not to pursue a ground-source heat pump (geothermal) system for the town’s new fire station. The unanimous vote came after extensive discussion, with members expressing opposing viewpoints and several repeatedly stating that they were “torn.”

Jeff Shaw of Context Architecture began the conversation by presenting the question, “should we move forward with doing geothermal test wells?” and asked for the FSBC to vote their response.

Shaw explained that the geothermal test wells would allow Context to “validate the design and cost estimates” for a geothermal system and give the town more concrete data about the cost of a ground-source system versus an air-source system.

Shaw said that if the FSBC voted to do the test wells, then the geothermal design and cost data would be available in early December and the town could then make a final decision on whether to pursue a geothermal system. Alternatively, if the FSBC voted against the test wells, Context would abandon plans for a geothermal system and move forward with an air-source heat pump system instead.

Although cost estimates of the two different systems have varied, Shaw estimated that a ground-source system would be roughly \$1 million to \$2 million more expensive than an air-source system, not counting potential federal tax credits.

Shaw first told the committee that the test wells would cost \$20,000. After a considerable amount of committee discussion, Shaw announced that he had misspoken and that the test wells would cost roughly \$75,000 to \$95,000.

In the first half of the discussion, when the FSBC believed the cost of the test wells would be \$20,000, members expressed differing viewpoints. Several members believed that even the rough estimates for a geothermal system were too expensive; that the community’s highest priority is reining in the cost of the fire station; and that the town should not spend money to explore a system that it is unlikely to pursue.

Other members believed that it would be worthwhile to invest in the test wells so that the committee could decide which system to pursue based on actual cost instead of estimated costs. Some also noted that the community has expressed support for a “green” building by voting in favor of the FSBC’s “sense of the meeting” article on this topic at the last Town Meeting.

After Shaw corrected the cost of the test wells from \$20,000 to \$75-95,000, the committee eventually came to the consensus that they would be unlikely to pursue the geothermal system due to its higher cost and thus should not spend any more money to explore that option. In coming to that consensus, members focused on the feedback that they have collected from public forums – that is, that the cost of the fire station is the “number one” concern of the community.

The FSBC’s November meetings are scheduled for Thursday, November 5 at Town Hall, and Thursday, November 19 at the Sargent Memorial Library.
