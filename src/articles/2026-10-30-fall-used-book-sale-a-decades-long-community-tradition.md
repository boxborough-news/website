Continuing a longstanding community tradition that raises thousands of dollars for library programs, museum passes, and other educational initiatives, the Friends of the Boxborough Library (FOBL or Friends) will open the doors to a waiting crowd on Friday, November 6 at 5 p.m. for the FOBL’s fall used book sale (See [https//boxlib.org/news-events/events](https://boxlib.org/news-events/events) for complete information).

Preparing for the event begins months in advance, with dates selected in coordination with library staff and consideration of holidays and other community activities. In the week leading up to each sale, around 20 volunteers collect, sort, and arrange donated materials in the library’s meeting room.

Shoppers can generally expect a selection of fiction, nonfiction, and children’s books, alongside cookbooks and titles covering gardening, travel, art, technology, spirituality, health, and crafts. Depending on donations, the sale may also include puzzles, board games, audiobooks, CDs, DVDs, music, and other audiovisual materials.

According to John Fallon, treasurer of the FOBL, the organization typically raises around 2,500 to 3,000 at each book sale. The Friends hold two sales annually, one in the fall and one in the spring, and the sales account for approximately 40% of the organization’s total revenue. These funds help sustain a range of library services. Over the past three years, the Friends have purchased nearly 22,000 in museum passes and contributed 15,000 toward library programs and activities, including the library's summer reading programs.

“The museum passes that we offer to our patrons are fully financed by the Friends,” said Peishan Bartley, Director of the Sargent Memorial Library.

The library offers passes to 27 institutions, including the Boston Children’s Museum, the Museum of Fine Arts, and New England zoos. The passes allow patrons to purchase admission at discounted rates. According to Bartley, the library pays annual subscription fees for these passes through the Friends’ support. The organization also sponsors many programs for children and young adults, covering craft materials and fees for special presenters. Without that assistance, Bartley shared, the library would struggle to maintain its current range of programming and museum pass offerings.

The book sale’s impact extends beyond fundraising. Rebecca Caprara and Kim Kleine, co-presidents of the FOBL, said the event provides an opportunity for residents to connect through a shared interest in books while supporting the library’s educational and cultural resources. “We hope that the sale helps connect community members, promoting literacy efforts and public engagement through a shared love of books,” Caprara and Kleine told Boxborough News.

Anne McNeece, a former FOBL president, said the sale has evolved considerably since its beginnings in the 1970s. Before the library moved into its current building in 2005, volunteers often stored donated books in their homes and arranged them shortly before the event. Outdoor sales were also common, with volunteers hoping for favorable weather. Today, the library’s meeting room gives volunteers a dedicated space to sort donations throughout the week before the sale. Remaining books are collected by Baystate Book Company, which redirects materials to literacy initiatives and other organizations rather than allowing them to go to waste.

McNeece described the sale as a community tradition that brings together regular shoppers, volunteers, and families. She particularly enjoys seeing young children leave with armloads of books and watching friendships develop among people who attend each event.

“The book sale is a small miracle that happens twice a year,” McNeece said. “It brings joy to those who come to the sale and to the volunteers, and it rehomes books so that they can be read and appreciated again.”

Residents can support the upcoming sale by donating gently used hardcovers, paperbacks, and music and audiovisual items. Complete puzzles and board games in like-new condition are also welcome. Donations will be accepted Monday, Nov. 2, through Thursday, Nov. 5, during regular library hours in the library meeting room. VCR tapes, magazines, and materials in very poor condition will not be accepted.

The Friends welcome new members throughout the year. Residents and local businesses can contribute through memberships and donations, helping sustain the museum passes, children’s programming, and other library initiatives. Business memberships may sponsor museum passes or support programs, and donations to the Friends are tax-deductible.

For the Friends of the Boxborough Library, the fall sale offers residents more than an opportunity to find affordable reading material. It is also a chance to support the library’s services, give books a second life, and contribute to a community tradition that has endured for decades.
