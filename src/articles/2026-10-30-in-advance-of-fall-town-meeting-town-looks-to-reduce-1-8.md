At Fall Town Meeting (FTM) on November 16, voters will decide whether to authorize the Town of Boxborough to purchase 18.5 acres at 276 Middle Road for \$1.8 million (Article 4). As FTM approaches, town officials have been working with several organizations to offset the cost of this proposed land purchase.

The property at 276 Middle Road, including a house, barn, and 18.5 acres of land, belongs to the Richardson family. The residence (ca. 1866\) was the home of Clyde and Virginia (Ginny) Richardson. Ginny Richardson served as Boxborough Town Clerk for almost four decades. Together, the Richardsons farmed the land and sold a variety of fruits and vegetables, including Halloween pumpkins. Clyde died in 2015, and Ginny died in 2024\. The property is now being offered for sale by their heirs and has an offer from a developer to purchase the land for \$1.8 million.

According to the warrant, the town would like to purchase the property “for the purposes of active agriculture;” additionally, “the land will join contiguously to 93 acres of existing conservation and agricultural land.” Article 4 is sponsored by the Boxborough Agricultural Commission. Their vision for the property, explained in the warrant, is to lease the farmhouse, barn, and at least part of the property to a farmer for agricultural use.

The warrant article for the land purchase would authorize the town to borrow and spend the full \$1.8 million. However, the town has been working to secure financial contributions to offset the cost.

The three potential contributions named in the FTM warrant are: \$300,000 from the Fiscal Year 2028 Community Preservation Fund, \$100,000 (or more) from the Boxborough Conservation Trust, and \$500,000 from the Sudbury Valley Trustees.

The Community Preservation Fund contribution of \$300,000 is subject to a vote of the Community Preservation Committee (CPC) after a public hearing scheduled for Thursday, November 5\.

At their October 1 meeting, the CPC voted that the request for funding towards acquisition of the Richardson property was eligible to receive FY2028 Community Preservation Act funds (for the fiscal year starting July 1, 2027). To allow the CPC to vote on the funding for the Richardson property before the November FTM, the CPC voted unanimously to hold a public hearing on this project on Thursday, November 5\.

The Boxborough Conservation Trust contribution of \$100,000 was voted by the BCTrust Board on October 5\. On October 20, the BCTrust also launched a fundraising campaign with a goal of raising an additional \$200,000 towards the land acquisition. According to BCTrust board member Becky Harris, the campaign had raised about \$31,000 in its first week. The campaign is expected to continue until December 31, 2026\. Harris told Boxborough News, “This is probably the most money we've tried to raise in the shortest amount of time.”

The Sudbury Valley Trustees (SVT) contribution of \$500,000 is subject to a vote of the Sudbury Valley Trustees Board of Directors that is expected to take place in December. At least part of the SVT’s contribution is also contingent on the negotiation of one or more “conservation restrictions” on the three adjacent parcels already owned by the town.

According to the Massachusetts Division of Conservation Services, a conservation restriction is “a legally enforceable agreement whose purpose is to ensure permanent protection of specific conservation values while permitting limited land uses consistent with the protection of said conservation values.”

Boxborough Land Use Director Alec Wade told Boxborough News that the town is actively working with SVT to figure out the “logistics” of their contribution, but that the town is “still working towards \$500,000.” Wade said that the details of potential conservation restriction(s) continue to be discussed with SVT. According to Wade, the process to finalize a conservation restriction with the required state approval can take up to 12-18 months. Wade indicated that if the town did not agree to a conservation restriction on the adjacent (municipal) parcels, SVT’s contribution would be less than \$500,000.

If Article 4 passes at FTM, the town would enter a 90-day due diligence period and expect to complete the land acquisition in early 2027\.

The FTM warrant reflects a unanimous vote in favor of Article 4 from the Agricultural Commission (3-0-1) and split votes from the Select Board (3-1-1), Finance Committee (4-1-0), and Capital Committee (1-3-1). The warrant also reflects that the Community Preservation Committee, Conservation Commission, and Water Resources Committee have deferred their vote and recommendation to Town Meeting floor.

Article 4 requires a two-thirds majority to pass at FTM on November 16\.
