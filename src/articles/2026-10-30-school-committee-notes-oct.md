*DESE mandate for more instructional hours*

The “hot topic” of the [October 15 School Committee](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nq-Sfb071eU) meeting was the corrective steps the district will need to take after the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (DESE) determined that the Acton-Boxborough Regional High School (ABRHS) [does not currently meet regulatory requirements regarding structured “time on learning”](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1dHLe7StRFMulwcnU63ZIYK5_NkXluyyy/edit?slide=id.p4#slide=id.p4) during the District’s recent Integrated Monitoring Review.

[DESE regulations require that every high school student is scheduled for 990 instructional hours.](https://www.doe.mass.edu/lawsregs/603cmr27.html?section=04) However, because most 10th, 11th and 12th graders have an average of one free period each day, they are not currently scheduled for enough instructional hours. (Ninth graders are currently fully scheduled.) In order to meet the requirement, 10th12th graders will need to be “fully scheduled” as well, likely eliminating the free periods that are beloved by both faculty and students.

“For the high school, free periods and students having the independence to have those free periods in the 10th, 11th, and 12th grade is what some may consider a signature part of the Acton-Boxborough experience,” explained ABRHS Principal Joanie Dean to the School Committee. “… There is also, on balance, a state mandate for 990 instructional hours. And those free periods right now are getting in the way of all of our students having 990 instructional hours in the classroom every year. And so we’re going to have to kind of balance our values as a community with what is required by the state and what they have asked us to do.”

“There is some concern around the possibility of losing free periods and moving to a full schedule,” said ABRHS Junior and School Committee Representative Will Mailer at the October 1 School Committee Meeting. “Currently, students rely on free periods to get teacher help, manage homework, and reset during a demanding day.”

Second, 18 total instructional hours in the school year will need to be added to the school year, because DESE found that even when fully scheduled, students at the high school currently only have 972 instructional hours. Third, seniors are currently getting out of school too early; the District needs to change the last day for seniors so that it is no more than 12 days before the end of school.

The District has until July 2027 to rectify the issues, though it recently asked DESE for an extension to allow for a slower, phased-in approach over three years, beginning with 10th graders next year.

The 990 hours of instructional learning is not a new regulation; it has been on the books since the late 1990s, but Dean said that DESE seems to have renewed interest in ensuring that districts adhere to it.

“I believe the state in our conversations with them are also looking more closely at this now when schools are going through this review because in 2019 it wasn’t looked at as closely for AB as it is now,” said Dean. “Some schools are fully scheduled. But also some aren’t. And I think the state is aware of that and is looking to more strictly enforce their mandate of 990 instructional hours.”

DESE’s renewed commitment to the 990 instructional hours comes at a cost, at a time when the District has not been able to afford a level-service budget in several years, a point that frustrated some School Committee members.

“We’re in the top five percent of public school districts in the state. Our outcomes are excellent. People move to this area to be in these public schools. The free periods in 10th, 11th, and 12th grade are distinctive to the academic experience,” said School Committee Member Glen Cote. “There is no indication that any part of this would be an improvement overall to either the student experience or educational learning outcomes. I’m very confident in saying this is a stupid unfunded mandate. It’s unhelpful. It’s a dumb law. It puts a massive burden on our administration, teachers, and staff. And for what?”

Cote said that instead, state legislators should be working to change the regulation. “So, we have state legislators. We have partners in the State House and the State Senate. They should be filing a bill to get rid of this. This is stupid.”

To fully schedule 1,200 high school students who currently have a free period will be a significant challenge for the District. Not only are there staffing implications, but there are also space implications, because the high school’s renovation many years ago was done with this type of high school schedule in mind.

“We have the number of classrooms built for the current schedule that we run,” explained Superintendent Peter Light, citing a conversation he had with past ABRHS Principal Larry Dorey.

“I think there are a lot of actual cultural implications to this,” continued Light. “Because, you know, it’s a benefit to teachers and it’s a benefit to students when teachers can kind of stay in their room all the time. And if teachers have to start moving around and sharing classrooms, it’s just an added layer of burden on everything. And so, you know, the tighter we get, the more we push ourselves in that direction. And sometimes it’s not can you do it, it’s at what cost, right? And I think that’s an important element of thinking about it.”

The high school has assembled a [990 Staff Committee](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1hq13UDIjHLa07YTNc2s2_0z_dPLvbwVub-PUkLknT8I/edit?tab=t.0) of 20 faculty and staff, chaired by English teacher Dan Goldner, to develop and recommend solutions to bring the high school into compliance. Solutions will be considered for low (adding 0-1 FTE, or full-time equivalent), mid (adding 4-5 FTE) and high (adding 10 FTE/1 million in additional cost) budgets. Recommendations are due by late December 2026

The 990 Staff Committee is soliciting feedback, including holding Student Forums during school lunch and Parent/Caregiver Forums on October 29, November 18 and December 8

*Superintendent search underway*

The Superintendent search is underway, with applications closed and first round interviews being scheduled for the first week of November, School Committee vice chair (Boxborough) and [Superintendent Screening Committee](https://abschools.community.diligentoneplatform.com/document/4dc0804f-b516-40cb-b773-a09e84896183/) chair Adam Klein reported. Finalist candidates will be invited to visit the district on November 16-18, including a public forum, followed by final interviews with the School Committee on December 3 Public feedback will be sought again after the finalists visit the district. A representative from the search firm also summarized the [results of the Community, Staff and School Committee Input](https://abschools.community.diligentoneplatform.com/document/10762016-8859-4cab-a72f-6a787e1a0e2a/) from a community survey and stakeholder interviews. 357 community members completed the survey; 569 responded in part, predominantly parents and caregivers.

*Rising costs continue*

Engineering estimates for HVAC rooftop units for the high school came in at 8 million, twice the original projection of 4 million. Likewise, projections for MIAA Health Benefits Trust health insurance increases are coming in at a 15-16 percent increase per year for the next three years.

*Parker Damon Playground planning authorized*

Superintendent Light explained that the Parker Damon playground is meeting the end of its useful life, and the Extended Day Program has allocated 200,000 of Extended Day funds towards a new playground, which Light estimates will cost 500,000. The District is planning to fundraise for the playground in the community and to seek grants. The School Committee unanimously moved to authorize the district to proceed with planning upgrades to the Parker Damon Building playground, including community engagement and design development, and to solicit donations and pursue grants to support the project, with any District capital contribution subject to subsequent School Committee approval.

*Challenges and successes in elementary school reorganizations*

Lower Elementary School principals and the Blanchard principal spoke with the School Committee at the October 1 Committee meeting. The principals were all positive about the relationships that were being developed with students and faculty, and the PTSOs. The principals also spoke about logistical challenges stemming from larger class sizes and more students, ranging from needed technology upgrades at Blanchard to the need for more parking at the Boardwalk school and the overall need for additional collaborations between teachers.

Superintendent Light also spent time discussing the addition of a new second grade section to Boardwalk Lower School given that nine new students arrived over the summer that were geographically zoned to Boardwalk.

*Accolades for teachers and students in the District*

Melissa Mastrolia was named 2027 Massachusetts Art Education Association (MAEA) Art Educator of the Year.

Brittany Martinez was named the 2027 MAEA Elementary Art Educator of the Year.

Aadya Goel and Robby Ranjitkar, ABRHS seniors, were each awarded the Massachusetts Association of School Superintendents Certificate of Academic Excellence Award.

*Student feedback*

ABRHS junior and School Committee Representative Will Mailer reported that student excitement is high around new electives like robotics, jewelry design, and AP African-American Studies. Students also appreciate the clear AI policies, and the open gradebook on PowerSchool.
