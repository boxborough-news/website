In a lengthy meeting on October 20, the Boxborough Water Resources Committee (WRC) discussed the potential impact of neighboring towns’ efforts to connect to the Massachusetts Water Resource Authority (MWRA), the Littleton-Boxborough waterline project, and PFAS contamination in a private, residential well on Flagg Hill Road.

The committee first heard a presentation from Town Planner Alec Wade  and Agricultural Commission Vice Chair John Neyland regarding [Fall Town Meeting Warrant Article 4](https://www.boxboroughnews.org/news#h.9nj1di89ypkd) about the proposed acquisition of 276 Middle Road, including the plan to maintain the property as a working farm.

Wade reported that Sudbury Valley Trustees (SVT), who have pledged up to \$500,000 to offset the cost of the property, have indicated that at least part of their contribution is contingent on the negotiation of one of one or more “conservation restrictions” (CRs) on the three adjacent parcels already owned by the town.

Several WRC members expressed concerns that CRs on the four parcels could conflict with water-supply uses and that future access to water sources might be compromised. In 2002, a WRC consultant ranked one of the four parcels as the second highest in town for developing a high-yield water supply.

Neyland emphasized that the CRs have not been written yet and could be written to preserve the town’s water rights on the parcels.

The WRC did not vote a recommendation and will meet on Friday, November 6 at 7 p.m. to continue their discussion and possibly take a vote.

Turning their attention to regional water solutions, WRC Chair Les Fox and Clerk Bryon Clemence reported that nine Metrowest towns have formed a coalition to apply to MWRA for water. The consortium towns are Acton, Ayer, Bedford, Concord, Groton, Lincoln, Littleton, Maynard, and Weston. A cost allocation study has been done to determine how much each town might be assessed for connecting to MWRA.

“What that means for Boxborough,” Clemence said, “is that if Acton and Littleton are getting MWRA water…we (Boxborough) will be getting MWRA water because Littleton supplies the western part of Boxborough.” Clemence stated that he was told by MWRA that “if we get one drop of MWRA water, we’re going to have to join MWRA, which is enormously expensive.”  Clemence received conflicting information from Acton Water Department regarding the MWRA connection. It is an unresolved issue and will have to be dealt with by the Select Board in the future, Clemence told the committee.

Chair Fox reported on the status of the Littleton-Boxborough waterline project, which is bringing clean, treated public water through a pipeline from the Whitcomb Avenue Water Treatment Plant (in Littleton) to properties located on Beaver Brook Road, Swanson Road, and Codman Hill Road.

Although initially expected to be completed by the end of 2026, Fox told the committee that the “schedule for the Littleton-Boxborough waterline has slipped” by approximately one quarter.  The water main is completed and hydrants for firefighting are fully operational. However, drinking water will not be available from Littleton Water Department before the end of the year, Fox said, and added that he has not heard an explanation for the delay in the drinking water connection.

According to the Littleton Electric Light and Water Department (LELWD) website, “this project brings public water to about 1,100 residents living along the western edge of Boxborough, where drinking water wells are contaminated with PFAS and road salt.”

PFAS contamination was recently found in a private home well on Flagg Hill Road near Stow Road. In his report, WRC and Board of Health member Bryan Lynch updated the committee regarding elevated PFAS levels at the home. The contamination was discovered when the homeowners listed the house for sale. As of May 1, 2026, Boxborough Board of Health regulations require water quality testing prior to property transfers.

PFAS (Per- and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances) are a group of chemical compounds designated as a contaminant. In 2020, Massachusetts Department of Environmental Protection (MassDEP) published its PFAS public drinking water standard of 20 nanograms per liter (ng/L) (or parts per trillion (ppt)). More information from the State can be found in their “[PFAS in Private Well Drinking Water Supplies FAQ](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/pfas-in-private-well-drinking-water-supplies-faq#what-are-the-levels-of-concern-for-pfas-chemicals).”

According to Lynch’s report to the WRC, the PFAS level at the Flagg Hill Road home initially came in at 1100 ppt, which is well above the MassDEP threshold of 20 ppt. A second test was performed by the state and registered 1400 ppt according to the Board of Health minutes from September 16, 2026\.

State officials “stepped in and requested the help of the town in writing to surrounding well owners to do free well testing for PFAS,” Lynch told the committee. They want “to understand where or maybe how impactful this finding is.”

The Board of Health prepared and sent a letter to homeowners within a 500-foot radius of the contaminated well. The state is requesting permission to test the water, and the Board of Health is “highly recommending” that homeowners allow the state to do so, Lynch said.

“It is a significant area around Flagg Hill and it involves quite a few houses…We don’t know the full impact yet…The testing hasn’t been sent out for contract because…permissions to test haven’t been completed yet,” Lynch told the committee.

Lynch said that the state has not speculated on the source of contamination and is trying to determine if this is an isolated incident because of something that happened at the house or if it is subject to a wider area.

Other WRC members recalled that elevated levels of PFAS were also found in a well on Fifer’s Lane earlier this year and emphasized that ameliorating private well contamination is the responsibility of the homeowner.

It’s going to be a while before we get the results from the state or if we’re going to be allowed to see them,” Lynch said. “We’re not out of the woods yet…the Town of Boxborough still has to face up that we have some water problems.”
