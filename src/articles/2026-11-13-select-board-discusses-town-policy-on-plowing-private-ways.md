At its meeting on November 9, the Boxborough Select Board discussed town plowing services on “private ways” in Boxborough.

After a lengthy discussion among Board members with public input by multiple residents, Select Board Chair Bob Stemple asked Town Administrator Mike Johns to develop a draft policy for the plowing of private ways. Stemple also stated that no changes to town plowing services would be made until July 2027.

At the beginning of the discussion, Chair Stemple explained that the town had received an inquiry regarding the plowing of a private way and Stemple had become aware that “we don’t have a policy on it.”  Stemple stated that of the many private ways in Boxborough, two have been plowed by the town in the recent past: Applewood Drive and Wetherbee Lane.

Stemple initially proposed to the Board that “we don’t plow [Applewood Drive and Wetherbee Lane] any more.” Stemple explained that he was concerned about the town’s liability related to plowing private ways.

Other Board members proposed alternatives to discontinuing plowing services. These included continuing to plow only those roads currently plowed by the town; taking each road on a case-by-case basis; or expanding plowing services to all private ways.

Select Board member Kristin Hilberg provided some historical context, noting that “in my first rodeo on Select Board we had discussed private ways and we decided that all private ways would be plowed… there has been past practice of the Select Board supporting it.”

A group of residents from Wetherbee Lane and Applewood Drive attended the meeting and voiced concerns in response to a “rumor” that the town was planning to discontinue plowing on these two roads this coming winter.

Board Clerk Sam Anderson expressed concern about the town’s decision-making and communications on the issue to date, noting that this Select Board had not discussed this issue before this meeting. “To me… it’s a little bit like the Town Hall hours thing,” said Anderson. “It’s either a Select Board policy and we have to talk about it and vote on it, or it’s Mike’s responsibility.”

Johns agreed to conduct relevant research and develop a draft policy on town snowplowing services to present to the Select Board.

The Board also heard a presentation about the town’s “ADA Self-Evaluation and Transition Plan” from the Institute for Human-Centered Design**.** The presentation identified areas for improvement in town programs and services, town facilities and parks, and the town website. The Board asked Assistant Town Administrator Rajon Hudson to develop a list of priorities for further action.

The Select Board’s next meeting will be immediately after the Fall Town Meeting on November 16 in order to call a special election, if needed, following the vote on Article 4 (purchase of property at 276 Middle Road).
