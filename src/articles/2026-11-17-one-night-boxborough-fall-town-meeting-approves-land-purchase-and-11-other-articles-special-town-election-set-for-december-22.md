The Town of Boxborough’s Fall Town Meeting was called to order by moderator Dennis Reip at 7:02 pm on Monday November 16 in the Blanchard Memorial School Gymnasium. Peak attendance totaled almost 310 during the discussion of the most controversial issue, acquisition of a local farm for $1.8 million, which ultimately passed by the required two-thirds vote.

After opening remarks by the moderator,  the meeting took up three zoning articles. Article 1, an amendment to the MBTA Zoning Bylaw, passed without debate by a show of hands vote. Article 2, a twelve-month moratorium on data centers, passed by a two-thirds vote as declared by the moderator after brief debate.  Article 3, a technical amendment conforming the Boxborough Zoning Bylaw to state law also passed by a two-thirds vote as called by the moderator, after no debate.

Article 4, which was sponsored by the Agricultural Commission (AgCom) and presented by AgCom Vice Chair John Neyland, asked the town to purchase the property at 276 Middle Road (also known as “the Richardson Farm”) for $1.8 million. The property is 18.5 acres and includes an 1866 house and an antique barn.  

In his presentation, Neyland noted that the final cost of the property might be significantly less after applying Community Preservation Act funds and contributions from the Boxborough Conservation Trust (BCT) and Sudbury Valley Trustees (SVT). During Neyland’s presentation, Moderator Reip also allowed comments by a representative of SVT.

During the ensuing debate on Town Meeting floor, which lasted almost an hour, roughly 15 residents voiced opinions for and against Article 4. The debate centered around the value of preserving open space and agricultural land versus the impact on the tax rate and potential future expenses associated with the historic house and barn.   

The motion under this article asked voters to approve borrowing up to $1.8 million, which required a two-thirds vote by Town Meeting and is subject to a Proposition 2 ½ debt exclusion vote by a majority at a Special Town Election. 

The article passed by the required two-thirds vote, as declared by the moderator, and will proceed to the required ballot vote. In the meantime, Neyland’s presentation indicated that the clock will start on the town’s “90-day due diligence period” for the property purchase, and the town will continue to work with SVT on the conditions of their contribution.

At the conclusion of Town Meeting, the Select Board met to set the date of the Special Town Election. The election will be held on Tuesday, December 22 in the Grange Room at Town Hall. 

When reached for comment, Moderator Reip told Boxborough News, “The voters of Boxborough showed, once again, that democracy is alive and well at last night's Fall Town Meeting. With over 300 voters in the room, we had a thorough and thoughtful hour-long discussion on the proposed Article 4 land purchase, leading up to its passage as the highlight of the evening.”

Neyland also provided a comment to Boxborough News: “On behalf of the Agricultural Commission, I thank everyone for their overwhelming support in conserving the Richardson farm. Boxborough will benefit for generations to come from this working farm in the heart of our town.”  

After Article 4, the remainder of Town Meeting proceeded relatively quickly. Article 5 called for the transfer from free cash of $20,000 to pay for costs related to the acquisition of 276 Middle Road and passed by a show of hands after brief debate. Article 6, an amendment of the Personnel Plan to change the formula for compensating the wiring and plumbing and gas inspectors was adopted after no comments.

The next article (Article 7) requested a transfer of $25,000 from free cash for the design, engineering, and construction of a water flow control system to protect the Hager Bridge behind Blanchard School from flooding related to beaver activity. After a short discussion, it too was approved by a show of hands.  

Article 8 proposed transferring $6,000 from free cash to support Boxborough’s initial costs to join the Devens Regional Hazardous Products Collection Center.  The meeting approved this program.

Article 9 asked Town Meeting to accept a state law that entitles Town employees who serve in the active or reserve armed forces “to receive pay without loss of ordinary remuneration (certain pay and benefits) as a public employee during service in the uniformed service.” This was passed unanimously with no debate.

Article 10 was a placeholder to allow funding of the first year of the collective bargaining agreement covering a new union of Town employees. Since the bargaining was not complete, the meeting unanimously passed over this article. 

Article 11 and 12 asked the Town Meeting to rescind two unneeded borrowing authorizations in the amount of $72,000 and $133,000, respectively.  Both were passed quickly without comment, as was Article 13, which asked for a transfer of $182 to pay an invoice from Fiscal Year 2026 that was inadvertently not processed.         

The meeting dissolved at 9:19 pm.
