The Sargent Memorial Library Youth Department is launching two special programs for young book lovers. Teens and children ages three and older can now apply to be a “Junior Librarian of the Month” and create a themed, month-long book display in the Children’s Room at the library.

The “Junior Librarians” will bring their book display vision to life with the help of Youth Services Librarian Heather Waddell, who designed the new program.

“There were several things that added up to creating this program,” Waddell explained to Boxborough News. “I've loved putting together themed book recommendations since I was 10 and made a book display for a 4-H project. Not only did it earn a blue ribbon, but that display also planted the idea that everyone loves sharing things they're passionate about. The next layer of inspiration came from my teen volunteers, who get very excited when I ask them to help me choose display books because they get to think creatively. And finally, many of the young patrons who visit the Children's Room love telling me about books they've read, and regularly ask if I have "jobs" for them to do. So sharing books, thinking creatively, and helping out all pulled together into this program!”

Waddell modeled the program on similar programs at other libraries. “I'm always on the lookout for display inspiration from other libraries, and several of them have programs for kids to design a display,” said Waddell. “Some have a display case for kids to display art or collections, and some have programs similar to our Junior Librarian program. Seeing those programs showed me that it was logistically possible, and I adapted those programs and policies to the specific needs of our library.”

The Junior Librarian’s job will include four steps: choosing a topic or theme, using the catalog to make a list of books, making signs and/or decorations, and putting the display on the shelves.

“Anyone ages 3-18 can be a Junior Librarian,” said Waddell. “Whether you come to the library a lot or not at all; whether you need extra help or you want to do it all yourself; whether you're a big reader or there's only one book you've ever really liked; no matter what, we want to see your display.”

Prospective Junior Librarians can submit an application on the library website, at [https://boxlib.org/services/childrens/junior-librarian](https://boxlib.org/services/childrens/junior-librarian).

The second new program for Boxborough’s youngest book lovers is the “1,000 Books Before Kindergarten” challenge, for families with children ages 0-5.

This nationwide program aims for each child to read 1,000 books with an adult before entering kindergarten. Any books count, including repeated reads. To support these young readers and their families, the Sargent Memorial Library is now offering startup kits, tracking tools, and milestone rewards. Families are welcome to sign up on the library website at [https://boxlib.org/services/childrens/1000books](https://boxlib.org/services/childrens/1000books).
