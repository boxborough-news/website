At their meeting on November 17, the Finance Committee (FinCom) discussed the town’s bond rating, financial dashboard, and revolving accounts, and briefly touched on the upcoming budget.

Standard & Poor’s (S&P) Global Ratings assigned its ‘AAA’ long-term rating to the Town of Boxborough for both bonds and existing general obligation debt. According to the S&P Global Ratings report of November 10, 2026, “the ‘AAA’ rating reflects the strength of Boxborough’s underlying economic metrics and its solid financial performance through various economic cycles, supported by its primarily residential makeup and stable property tax base.” General obligation bonds allow a municipality to borrow funds for long-term capital projects and refinance existing debt.

Treasurer/Collector Emily Roux said “a ‘AAA’ bond rating is indicative of superior creditworthiness, lowers the borrowing costs on municipal bonds, and signals strong financial health.”

FinCom also discussed the financial dashboard that tracks expenses, revenues, reserves, debt, outstanding taxes, and staffing on a quarterly basis. All items were in line with fiscal year 2027 projections. The dashboard is available on the Finance Committee page on the Town website ([https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/334/Finance-Committee](https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/334/Finance-Committee)) after FinCom reviews the results.

In other financial matters, the committee turned to “revolving funds.” According to the Massachusetts Division of Local Services (DLS) Financial Management Research Bureau (FMRB), in Massachusetts, all revenue is considered general fund revenue that must be appropriated by the legislative body (Town Meeting) unless another law provides for alternate treatment ([**M.G.L. c. 44, §53**](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleVII/Chapter44/Section53)).

“Revolving funds are an example of an alternative revenue source. These receipts are segregated from the general fund, earmarked for expenditure without appropriation for specified purposes by statute to support the activity, program, or service that generated the revenue, and spent from the actual collections on hand.” For example, Town Meeting approved a revolving fund for recreation programs that are self-funded.

In reviewing the revolving funds report, FinCom found some discrepancies where some funds were overspent – specifically the plumbing and gas inspector and electrical inspector funds. FinCom Chair Newton asked Assistant Town Administrator Rajon Hudson, “When we hit the spending limit (as voted at Town Meeting), how do we prevent it from going over the limit?” Hudson did not have an answer, but said he would check into this.

FinCom members suggested that the town finance team needs to trace back how and where the plumbing, gas, and electrical fees were collected and deposited. They also suggested developing a written policy about how revolving funds should be handled.

Lastly, FinCom briefly discussed next year’s budget. Hudson informed the committee that preliminary budgets from department heads were due by Friday, November 20 and would be presented to both the Select Board and FinCom during the week of December 14. FinCom member Maria Neyland said that the budgets must be in a workable/readable format that doesn’t require FinCom members to do pivot tables.

When asked about a 5-year budget plan, Hudson said that the town has not developed a 5-year budget plan.

FinCom’s next meeting is scheduled for Tuesday, December 1 at Town Hall. The meeting is hybrid.
