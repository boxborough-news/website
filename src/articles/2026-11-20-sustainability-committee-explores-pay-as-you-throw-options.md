At its meeting on November 12, the Boxborough Sustainability Committee heard a presentation from Associate Town Planner Ian Gilson that provided usage and financial data from the Boxborough Transfer Station. The Sustainability Committee’s Waste Management Working Group has been exploring ways that the town can reduce waste and save money, including the possibility of moving to a Pay-As-You-Throw (PAYT) system at the town’s Transfer Station.

According to the Massachusetts Department of Environmental Protection (MassDEP), “Pay-As-You-Throw (PAYT) is a municipal solid waste program where residents pay a variable, per-unit (bag) fee for the disposal of the trash they generate.” Typically, “recycling is offered at no direct cost.” Currently, residents who use the Boxborough Transfer Station pay an annual flat fee of $150, which allows unlimited disposal of trash and recycling.

Sustainability Committee Chair Kate Davies introduced Gilson and thanked him for collecting and organizing the data. She noted that the data will provide the information they need to “put together a couple of different scenarios for PAYT in Boxborough,” and she emphasized that the group is just exploring options at this point. “These are still very much preliminary ideas…for what could be, and…this spreadsheet enables us to play with a lot of the numbers and options (to) tweak our waste management system to not only save us money but also save on waste disposal…None of this is us proposing a particular solution,” Davies said.

Gilson first outlined the Transfer Station cost data from Fiscal Year 2025 and noted that “the Transfer Station has been struggling financially.” The operating cost, including hauling and disposal fees, as well as personnel fees, totaled approximately $178,000. The station’s only source of revenue comes from sticker fees, which totaled only $98,000. The deficit amount (approximately $80,000) is covered by the town’s operating budget.

Gilson shared a spreadsheet to model showing how PAYT (a per bag disposal fee) would positively influence the Transfer Station’s bottom line. He then provided other options to help meet the deficit, including the implementation of fees for the disposal of items such as refrigerators, air conditioners, large furniture, mattresses, and electronics.

“The Select Board and DPW and Town Administrator have been working together to figure out what makes sense for the Transfer Station and how to get it back on track to being a net-even part of our budget rather than a ‘red’ budget line that is losing money year after year,” Gilson told the committee.

The committee discussed the scenarios and the challenges of implementing a PAYT system, including enforcement, which all agreed is currently problematic.

Gilson agreed. “PAYT does have challenges, including enforcement…We need a change that will take the balancing of the Transfer Station budget seriously. If we’re going to implement PAYT, then enforcement needs to be taken seriously.”

At their next meeting on December 9, the committee will continue their discussion and formulate a “multi-pronged” campaign to engage with residents about trash disposal and its impact on the environment as well as the town’s budget.
