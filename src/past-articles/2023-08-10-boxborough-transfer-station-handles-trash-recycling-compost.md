On a recent Saturday morning, the Boxborough Transfer Station buzzed with activity. With trunks and hatchbacks open, cars stopped between the long containers as residents hopped out of their vehicles to deposit their trash and recyclables before proceeding to the north side of the property, where large, open containers for wood and scrap metal and an informal swap area attracted a group of residents disposing of items and browsing what others left behind.

Boxborough News reached out to Ed Kukkula, Department of Public Works Director, to learn about how the Transfer Station operates. Only Boxborough residents may visit the Transfer Station, and a sticker is required. Stickers can be purchased for a full year, six-month, three-month, or one-time use. Senior citizens (65+) and residents with an affordable housing trust waiver receive their stickers for free. The Transfer Station is open on Wednesdays (11 a.m. to 7 p.m.) and Saturdays (8 a.m. to 4 p.m.).

Sticker holders can dispose of trash, also known as solid waste, and household recycling (paper, cardboard, glass, metal, and plastic) in a single-stream container; no sorting is required. Other items that residents can dispose of include mercury (e.g., thermostats, batteries, fluorescent light bulbs), propane tanks, electronics, CRTs, textiles and clothes, mattresses, metal, clean wood, white goods (i.e., appliances), and food waste (compost).

Where does our trash go? Boxborough has a contract with Wheelabrator in North Andover, which is a waste-to-energy plant. That means that the trash is not transported to an out-of-state landfill, which is a less expensive option and one used by many municipalities and private contractors. Instead, the Andover plant uses the energy it produces from the trash to run the plant, and the unused energy is sent to the grid.

The recycling is taken to the Waste Management Material Recycling Facility in Billerica. When the recycling is brought to Waste Management, it is pushed into existing piles where it is then put on a conveyor belt to be sorted out by material type. Boxborough and most communities switched from sorting to single-stream recycling in 2009, and our recycling process has remained the same since. The only major change to recycling is mattresses: they are no longer allowed in the trash and must be recycled, and they are accepted at the Transfer Station.

The composting bin was added two years ago. The town is working with Black Earth Compost Company to help keep food waste out of the trash and turn it into valuable nutrient compost that is reused by many farmers and homeowners all over Massachusetts. An extensive list of food items that can be composted in the bins at the Transfer Station can be found on the town’s website: [https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/documentcenter/view/1651](https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/documentcenter/view/1651)

When asked if he would like to implement any changes at the Transfer Station, Director Kukkula responded that he would like to hire a part time Transfer Station attendant who only works for the town two days a week. This would allow one of the employees who currently works there to work for the DPW full time instead of just three days per week. “Other than that, I believe the Transfer Station operates very smoothly and provides a great service to the residents. Larry and Greg do a great job!”

To request a sticker, view the layout of the transfer station, learn about what is accepted at the transfer station and how to recycle or dispose of items not accepted, residents can visit [www.boxborough-ma.gov/266/Transfer-Station](https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/266/Transfer-Station).
