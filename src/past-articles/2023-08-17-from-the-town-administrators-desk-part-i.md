Editor’s Note: The following is written by Town Administrator Michael Johns. This is Part I of TA Johns’ inaugural note to Boxborough News readers. Part II will run next week.*

It has been nine months since my November 7 arrival, and it has been a wonderful experience interacting with the fine people of Boxborough, working with the exemplary staff serving the community, collaborating and communicating with the many boards, committees, and commissions, serving the town, and getting to know the business community. In this monthly note, you can expect regular operational updates, as well as strategic goal initiatives and accomplishments. Since this is my first update through Boxborough News, it will cover a lot of ground, but you can expect future notes to be shorter and to get further into the details of specific topics below.

Special Town Meeting – On November 6, the town will meet in Blanchard Elementary School Auditorium to discuss and vote on such items as accepting provisions of Massachusetts General Law to provide for clean drinking water, regional dispatch services, and other articles to be discussed in next month’s update once the town meeting warrant closes.

Citizen Academy – Last week, our assistant town administrator wrote about this program, which both he and I have experience offering in our prior communities. This is a highly engaging, hands-on approach to learning about how your local public services work, and if interested, how you might get involved in positively influencing local government. At the very least, participants will meet directly with department heads and committee chairs to learn more and get questions answered. The program will culminate with graduation and certificates. No prior experience is required and we highly encourage newcomers to the community, as well as those who may not have participated in local government at all, to check it out. There is no requirement or expectation for participants to actively join committees after graduation, although all are certainly welcome to do so. Slots are limited and the program begins soon, so contact Administration at [rhudson@boxborough-ma.gov](mailto:rhudson@boxborough-ma.gov) to sign up or get more information.

Boxborough News – When Cassie and Abby approached me with the idea of contributing to a regular column, I was very reluctant at first to take on another task, with my plate already overflowing, but I also felt it was the right thing to do, using one more avenue to get factual, timely information out to the community. I know they and the team at Boxborough News have reached out to some department heads and committee chairs. If you are contacted by one of the dedicated volunteers of this quickly growing high-quality publication, I highly encourage you to give serious consideration to either being interviewed or contributing your input as requested, if at all possible. At a time when local newspapers are shutting down at an alarming pace, this dedicated group of residents have found a way to rapidly gain traction in print and electronic format. Hats off to all of you!

Recreation – I had the good fortune to work closely with Recreation staff and Commission members this summer in town hall, in the stands at Fenway Park at Boxborough Day at the Red Sox (sponsored by the Commission with over 120 participants), and on Flerra Summer Playground during two unannounced visits. I am so impressed with the energy, dedication, fun and safety features built into all the programs. Our town is so fortunate to have so many dedicated people offering amazing programs to our youth and adults. More to come in future updates.

Library – This past week, our library was visited by one of the groups that makes it their business to visit public buildings and film staff and patrons, looking for flaws, mistakes and reaction, to then publish online for reactions. In my opinion, they didn’t find flaws or mistakes, but they did elicit reaction from some staff, who to their credit, were trying to protect the rights of children and families by enforcing the library’s policy of no recording of children without parental permission. In short, staff members were informed by the visiting videographers, in no uncertain terms, that federal laws trump local policy, and they continued filming. Again, to the credit of the highly dedicated library director and staff, services continued throughout the day and week, despite having felt bullied by an online monetized movement that thrives on follows and likes. In the words of one staff member, “we’re just introverted bookworms” who are dedicated to serving the public. I say, keep up the great work and keep doing what you do best, providing in-demand resources, guidance in finding materials for research and pleasure reading, as well as highly subscribed programming in the community for all ages and interests. Two thumbs up for our dedicated bookworms and public servants; we appreciate all that you provide our community! You are true professionals who make this community a wonderful place to live, work and play, every day.

Town Hall Parking – While there is plenty of parking at town hall, the premium parking is in the front row, at the main entrance, two left turns around the building after entering the parking lot. We are working on several initiatives to improve parking for visitors to the town’s main administration building. First, we are seeking grant funding to assess our compatibility with guidelines and regulations pertaining to access for all to and within the building. We know there is work needed relative to signage, handicapped parking, and access. We want to make sure we approach the issues wisely, using any grant funding available for this purpose and approaching any upgrades with the appropriate plans and resources in hand. Second, we are in the process of getting signs installed to direct visitors around the building to the main entrance. And third, we are encouraging employees to leave front row parking on the main entrance side of town hall open for visitors to the building.

Regional Dispatch – With the difficulty recruiting and retaining a sufficient number of trained and State 911 certified dispatchers in our local dispatch center in the police station, we are looking into the possibility of joining a regional dispatch center in calendar year 2024. We are currently in the study phase and have received no-cost assistance from both the State and Nashoba Valley Regional Dispatch District (NVRDD) in coverage of our calls while we have been short-staffed. Our Boxborough dispatchers have been doing a terrific job keeping us operational, with assistance from NVRDD and Boxborough Police and Fire staff, who have both stepped up to fill shifts in an operational non-911 certified status. As more and more communities move to regional dispatch districts and Massachusetts continues to send additional funding to these districts, it continues to become increasingly difficult to staff and operate local dispatch centers. For these reasons, we are researching to see if joining a district is appropriate for Boxborough. On October 11, I will be presenting at the monthly Council on Aging Luncheon in the Community Center across from town hall. The Chiefs will be joining me to share what we know at that time and entertain questions from the audience. We will also be coordinating a field trip that will be free of charge to the dispatch center at NVRDD located about 10.3 miles north at Devens. Transportation will be available, and more information will be forthcoming as details are available. We look forward to learning more and sharing the information with you.
