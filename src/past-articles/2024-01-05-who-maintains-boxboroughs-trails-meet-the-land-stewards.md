The “Land Stewards” are a group of roughly 20 volunteers who maintain the almost 30 miles of conservation trails throughout Boxborough.

It all started in 2001 when Boxborough residents Norm Hanover and Barbara Warren cleared the Wolf Swamp trail, going from an old trail head on Burroughs Road to Old Harvard Road. Hanover and Warren experimented with various methods to mark the trail until they found one that worked. At the same time, members of the Conservation Commission were developing the basic layout of trails on town-owned parcels.

By 2003, word of the efforts had spread and an informal group of volunteers came together. Liz Markiewicz was recruited to spearhead the initiative. Fifteen volunteers attended the very first meeting, and the land steward program was born.

The program evolved to where each volunteer is assigned one or more specific parcels. Volunteers engage in trail maintenance, removing debris and obstacles and ensuring that trails are clearly visible and marked. Boardwalks are used to cover most of the muddy trails, so that the trails can be walked during any season. Most of the work involves simple tasks like walking the trails to identify any problems or removing branches on the trails.

Major projects that require a chainsaw to cut a fallen tree that hangs over or blocks a trail are reported to the land steward coordinator, who may engage other volunteers or the Boxborough Department of Public Works (DPW) to help remove the obstacle.

Additionally, the land stewards manage and coordinate with other groups, such as Acton-Boxborough high school students and corporate groups who participate in an annual cleanup day.

Over the years, eagle scout projects have also enhanced Boxborough’s trails. Eagle scouts have built boardwalks and bridges over wetlands, created a new trail between Hager Meadow and Have-Not-Pond, and updated kiosks.

Dave Barnett, a 30-year resident of Boxborough, recently took over from Markiewicz as the land steward coordinator. Barnett decided to get involved after he retired, when he had more free time to devote to preserving Boxborough’s woodland areas.

What’s the next big thing for the land stewards? Barnett would like to focus on tackling invasive species like burning bush, Japanese knotweed, and bittersweet. Markiewicz said they are also looking at using QR codes at kiosks to help residents navigate the trails.

Maps of all Boxborough conservation trails can be found at [https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/351/Conservation-Trail-Maps](https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/351/Conservation-Trail-Maps).
