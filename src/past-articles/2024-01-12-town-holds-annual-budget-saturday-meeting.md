The Finance Committee and the Select Board didn’t waste any time starting the new year with the annual Budget Saturday meeting on January 6. On Budget Saturday, the FinCom and Select Board sit down together to discuss preliminary operating budgets for fiscal year 2025. The budgets will ultimately be presented at Annual Town Meeting starting on May 13.

The five and a half hour marathon meeting featured presentations by most of the major departments, including the Department of Public Works (DPW), Police, Fire and Town Hall. Budget presentations for the library and the Acton-Boxborough Regional School District were deferred to a later date.

Town Administrator Michael Johns opened the meeting by outlining budget goals, namely to provide level services, live within our means, “manage the pain,” and improve the Boxborough experience. When asked what he meant by “manage the pain,” Johns said that “although I think this could be a challenging and perhaps a painful budget year, we are well equipped and prepared to manage the challenges ahead of us by being smart and resourceful.”

Johns also discussed opportunities to improve services like the consolidation of several departments into a Human Services Department, reducing costs through the regionalization of dispatch, expanding animal control services to additional towns, and updating fees where appropriate.

DPW Director Ed Kukula presented a budget with a modest increase of 2%. Additionally, “tipping fees,” which represent the cost of transporting solid waste and recycling off site, were increased by 5%. The Select Board may consider increasing transfer station fees to offset expenses.

Police Chief Szewczyk presented a combined police/dispatch budget with an increase of 8.28% of which 91% represented salaries. FinCom recommended two separate budgets: one for police and a second for dispatch. FinCom also suggested that other line items in the police budget need to be more clearly defined or removed and presented as warrant articles.

With the regionalization of dispatch, the state will reimburse the town at 100% of most costs for the first three years of operation, then 50% in year four, and 25% in year five. There was some discussion about whether to frontload some of those future costs in a separate fund or warrant article or zero out most of the budget to reflect reimbursement from the state for the first three years of regionalization. No decisions were made.

Interim Fire Chief Shawn Gray and Captain Jason Malinowski presented an initial proposed budget for the fire department. The largest increase was in the salary line at 7.3%, which included a full year for a new firefighter hired in January 2024 and salary increases in the collective bargaining agreement.

Assistant Town Administrator Rajon Hudson presented the Town Hall budgets. Changes to the budget include a modest increase in the Town Clerk budget due to four elections in the upcoming year; the consolidation of three part time department assistants into one full time department assistant at an additional cost of $15,000; and town-wide training for employees and department heads to develop or improve non-technical skills like customer service, collaboration, and communication. FinCom provided feedback for additional changes to some department budgets.

FinCom Chair Maria Neyland said that Saturday’s meeting was the first step in the budget review process. Budgets will shift and change over the course of the next few months, until they are finalized by FinCom and voted by both the Select Board and FinCom. All budget meetings are open to the public. FinCom meets every Tuesday at 7 p.m. at Town Hall. Meetings are hybrid.
