The Boxborough Housing Board (BHB) met at Town Hall on Thursday, January 18. After two presentations and discussion, the BHB passed a motion to inform the Boxborough Building Committee (BBC) that the BHB will accept payment for the property at 70-72 Stow Road in the amount of $1,200,000, according to the terms of a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) that was prepared in 2011. The funds will be used to provide more affordable housing for Boxborough residents and to preserve existing affordable housing.

The first of the two presentations was by North Central Massachusetts Habitat for Humanity (Habitat NCM), describing affordable housing that Habitat NCM could build on 70-72 Stow Road. Habitat NCM builds new affordable homes and performs critical home repairs. To date, it has built 42 homes and repaired 152 homes in north central Massachusetts, at a typical rate of two homes per year. They have also built duplexes. Habitat NCM requires that land be donated to them for building. It has evaluated the property at 70-72 Stow Road and concluded that the property can be divided into four lots suitable for home building.

In the second presentation, BHB member Al Murphy described the most recent appraisals of the Stow Road property and discussed factors involved in the monetization of the property. The most recent valuations are similar, at approximately $1,200,000. He listed examples of how funds resulting from a monetization could be used to make housing more affordable to Boxborough residents, such as a program to repair and preserve the town’s aging affordable housing stock or to expand the Boxborough Rental Assistance Program.

The motion to inform the BBC initiates the process described by the 2011 MOU, which specifies that: “In the event that some portion or share of 72 Stow Road is not used for affordable housing, the Town shall reimburse the Board for said portion or share. Representatives of the Board, Trust, and Town shall negotiate a fair payment to the Board for said portion or share. The Board shall convey this payment to the Trust.”

In addition, the Homeowner Opportunity for Preservation Extension program was discussed. The HOPE program addresses the preservation of Boxborough’s affordable units. Because of administrative concerns, the Community Preservation Committee has declined to approve funding for HOPE. The BHB will continue to develop the program and address these concerns.

The next meeting of the BHB will be a public, in-person meeting at Town Hall at 7 p.m. on Thursday, February 15.
