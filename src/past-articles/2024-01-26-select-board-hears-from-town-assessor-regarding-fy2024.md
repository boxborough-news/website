At its meeting on January 22, the Select Board heard a presentation from the town’s finance team (town accountant, town assessor, and treasurer/collector) on the FY2024 Q2 Financial Report.

Town Assessor Kelly Szocik acknowledged that the FY2024 property revaluation process has led to an increase in property valuations and higher tax bills for many residents. Due to calendar year 2022 sales numbers, residential homes have seen an increase in the base price per square foot. Szocik also explained that Boxborough land values have not been updated since 2019 and that the increased valuations taxpayers are seeing can result from “catching up on data gaps.”

In response to a question from the Select Board, Szocik confirmed that all properties in town have been brought up to an appropriate assessment value. More information about the FY2024 property revaluation can be found on the Town Assessor’s page of the Town website: [https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/195/Town-Assessor.](https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/195/Town-Assessor)

Szocik will be at the Boxborough Community Center at 30 Middle Road for a coffee hour on Wednesday, January 31, at 10:30 a.m. to answer questions from residents. She also encouraged members of the public to contact her office with any questions.

The Select Board also approved a Common Victualler’s license for Craft Food Halls, a restaurant that is expected to open this year at 500 Beaver Brook Road. The license is contingent on receipt of certificates from the Building Commissioner and the Board of Health.

In a new “community spotlight” series, the Board heard from Recreation Commission Chair Megan Connor, who presented on the Rec Com’s projects and programs. Connor shared that the Rec Com is planning a second annual “RunBXB” 5K road race for April 7, 2024 and that Liberty Field is expected to fully reopen in June 2024. She also noted that the Rec Commission and Town Planner Alec Wade are evaluating the possibility of a softball/cricket field and a walking path at the Town’s new Erikson Landry Field.

Finally, the Select Board reopened the 2024 Town Meeting warrant to add potential articles for DPW equipment, a “green burials” study, a Hager Well chlorine system, Town Hall administrative staff, a truck for the Building Commissioner, painting and other maintenance for Town Hall, and software for the Treasurer-Collector.

Chair Kristin Hilberg previewed that the town will need to make “hard choices” at this year’s Town Meeting about what to fund, and suggested that the Select Board should weigh in on every warrant article to help inform and guide Town Meeting voters.
