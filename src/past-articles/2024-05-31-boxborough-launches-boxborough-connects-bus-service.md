On Thursday, May 16, the Town of Boxborough, in collaboration with Montachusett Regional Transit Authority (MART), launched “Boxborough Connects,” a new public transportation service.

“Launch week has been incredibly exciting,” said Town Planner Alec Wade. “I get a new call or email every day, asking us to bring service to another neighborhood. We’re continuing to improve the route, and I’m hopeful we can reach people all over town.”

The service currently consists of two bus routes that run Monday through Friday, from 7:30 a.m. to 6 p.m. The cost is $1 per ride. Tickets can be purchased when boarding the bus (cash only) or at Boxborough Town Hall. A full bus schedule is available at [https://tinyurl.com/y5px2xa2](https://tinyurl.com/y5px2xa2).

One route currently starts at Beaver Brook Road, then stops at 290 Swanson Road, Adams Place, Boxborough Meadows, Summer Fields, Nashoba Rink, Gardner Field in West Acton, Ace Hardware in Acton, and the South Acton MBTA Station.

The second route currently starts at the Acton Plaza (Roche Bros.), then stops at the Acton-Boxborough Regional School District Admin Building (A-B United Way Resource Center), Villageworks in West Acton (Danny’s Place), Applewood, Sargent Memorial Library, Middlesex Bank, Boxborough Town Hall, “Boxborough Green” Plaza, Paddock Estates, and Beaver Brook Road.

“The route will continue to grow and change in the coming weeks and months,” said Wade. “Our drivers, Stacey and Bob, are both very communicative, and we’ve empowered them to

share their experiences. We’ve already found stops that can be cut and ways to improve travel time. We’ll make changes to the route soon, which opens the door to connecting new neighborhoods; like Codman Hill Road or Liberty Square Road.”

“It’s nice to know you are a part of something new that will be a benefit to your town and fellow residents,” said driver Stacey Lynn Callahan. “Being a Boxborough resident for about three years now, I was pleasantly surprised to find out about so many hidden gems in this town! There are so many wonderful local businesses tucked away that residents can enjoy!”

“I enjoy meeting the residents of town,” said driver Bob Stemple. “Being part of this new service allows me to give back to the community that has helped me and my family for the past 31 years.”

In the first week, Wade estimated ridership at “around twenty trips in total…. We already have a few regulars, which is exciting to see.”

“Users are already using the route to reach the South Acton Train Station, which is a core goal of this service, reducing dependency on single occupancy vehicles,” continued Wade.

“The stories of impact and the phone calls we’re getting show the great work we’ve done. As a planner, you go into a new community hoping to see the change you’re trying to make. A year and a half in, and I can already see the impact we’re having.”
