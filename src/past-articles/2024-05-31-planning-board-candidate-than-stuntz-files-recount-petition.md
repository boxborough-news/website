On May 30, Planning Board candidate Nathaniel “Than” Stuntz filed a petition with the Town Clerk for a recount of votes in the Planning Board race. On election day, Town Clerk Rebecca Harris reported that Stuntz lost the election to incumbent Rebecca Verner by only three votes. According to the Town Clerk’s election results, available at [bit.ly/4brBJLK](https://bit.ly/4brBJLK), incumbent Verner received 336 votes and challenger Stuntz received 333 votes.

Town Clerk Harris reported that Stuntz’s recount petition, which must be signed by at least ten voters and notarized, met the State’s requirements and that she had certified the signatures on the petition.

Harris has called a Board of Registrars meeting for Monday, June 3, at 4 p.m. at Town Hall, at which the recount date will be set. She expects that the recount will likely take place the week of June 10th or June 17th.

The recount will be conducted by election workers hired by the Town Clerk. The election workers will conduct a hand count of all 679 ballots.

This is different from how votes are counted on election day. Harris explains, “[o]n election day, 667 ballots were run through the machines so they were counted by optical scanner. An additional nine ballots were not able to be read by the scanner. These nine were hand counted because the machine rejected them due to people not filling in the ovals completely, over-voting (voting for more than one person for a race that only had one seat available), or stray marks on the ballot. Those nine were hand counted on election night. An additional three Uniformed or Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act (UOCAVA) ballots came in electronically and were hand counted after polls closed.”

When asked for comment, Stuntz replied, “In light of the remarkably narrow margin of just three votes by which the election results were decided, I am requesting a recount to ensure the accuracy and integrity of the electoral process. Such a close outcome not only highlights the possibility of human error or discrepancies in vote tallying but also underscores the vital importance of each individual vote. A recount serves as a necessary step to confirm that every vote was counted correctly and that the democratic will of the electorate is faithfully represented. It is a testament to the value of transparency and fairness in our electoral system, and a safeguard against potential oversight in a race where every single vote carries significant weight. I look forward to the outcome of the recount.”

When asked for comment, incumbent Rebecca Verner replied, “I would like to express my sincere appreciation for the diligent work and countless hours contributed by our election officials and volunteers. This election has been a perfect example of how every vote counts, and I hope that this example encourages more people to get out there and make their voices heard.”
