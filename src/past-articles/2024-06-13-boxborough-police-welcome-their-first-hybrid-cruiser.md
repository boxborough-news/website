After waiting for over two years, Boxborough Police Chief John Szewczyk was in the right place at the right time this spring to bring the department its first hybrid police cruiser, a 2023 Ford Police Interceptor Utility pursuit vehicle.

The Chief explained that Ford (the only U.S. company producing hybrid police cruisers) has had production problems and was overwhelmed with orders. Szewczyk had almost given up the idea of adding a hybrid cruiser to the department’s fleet until he happened to learn that a nearby police department had received ten hybrids and only needed nine.

Szewczyk acted quickly, and a new hybrid cruiser now belongs to Boxborough. He also learned that Ford has decided to produce only passenger hybrids and will not resume production of hybrid police vehicles until 2025. The Chief said, “We are very excited to begin transitioning our fleet to more sustainable, environmentally friendly vehicles. We sincerely thank the community for their support.”

Officer Michael Turner, one of the officers assigned to the new vehicle, explained that it’s far more economical to be using the electric mode rather than gasoline, especially because cruisers must remain idling for long periods of time. He said that cruisers must be prepared to move at any moment, and the dispatch system, mobile camera, and mobile data terminal (for checking license plates and other information) “draw electricity from the lithium-ion battery, allowing the gasoline engine to shut off.” He added, “The gas engine runs only intermittently to charge the battery, so no plug-in is needed.”

The new cruiser is equipped with highly reinforced doors and a newly designed, very safe “cage” for transporting people.

Chief Szewczyk said, “Our department recognizes that transitioning to electric vehicles is just one aspect of making an impact on sustainability, and we will continue to look for other ways we can play our part to ensure we have a sustainable environment well into the future.”
