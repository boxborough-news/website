The late Jeanne Steele Kangas expressed her life-long love of Boxborough by leaving a $50,000 bequest to Boxborough “for the maintenance, restoration, repair and/or renovation of the 1794 Levi Wetherbee (Steele) farmhouse” and a second $50,000 bequest “to the Boxborough Historical Society for its unrestricted use.”

At a June 12 ceremony in Town Hall, Jeanne Kangas’ widow, Robin Lazarow, explained, “Jeanne made these meaningful bequests because she was passionate about history, was a lifelong resident of Boxborough, raised her family here, and was actively involved in many important town initiatives and issues over the decades.”

Lazarow presented a $50,000 check to Christopher Hydak, Chair of the Steele Farm Committee, and a $50,000 check to Kate West, President of the Historical Society.

Robin Lazarow pointed out that Jeanne “served as the first woman select person of the town, chair of the Steele Farm Committee, and President of the Historical Society …. She wanted to preserve the town’s history and honor her family history by making these bequests."

Lazarow added, “I hope these funds will be put to good use and that Jeanne will be remembered for her integrity, generosity, and lifelong love of Boxborough. She left an extraordinary legacy of public service, community involvement, and civil discourse. May Jeanne’s memory be a blessing.”

In response to the gifts from Jeanne Kangas, Select Board Clerk Priya Sundaram read a “Town of Boxborough Select Board Proclamation” acknowledging “the $100,000 bequest from Jeanne Steele Kangas to the Town of Boxborough from the Kangas Family Trust by Trustee Robin Lazarow.”

The proclamation reads, “Jeanne spent 51 of her 83 years on this Earth in service to the Town on 10 Boards Committees and Official Positions, and continues to give back to her beloved Town of Boxborough, its history, and the Steele Farm, specifically through her two gifts of $50,000 each to The Historical Society and the Steele Farmhouse. The Select Board offers its heartfelt gratitude to a woman who throughout her life and beyond continues to positively impact our wonderful community.”

The proclamation is stamped with the town seal and signed by Town Administrator Michael Johns on behalf of the Select Board. Attending the ceremony were members of the Select Board, the Steele Farm Committee, the Historical Society, and other members of the community.
