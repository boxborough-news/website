At its meeting on Monday, June 24, the Select Board voted in favor of beginning a two-year application process to potentially become a member community of Nashoba Valley Technical High School (NVTHS).

Boxborough is not currently a member of any vocational tech school. Select Board members expressed the opinion that becoming a member of a vocational school would create good opportunities for Boxborough students. Vocational schools typically consider applications from students in member towns before considering applications from students in non-member towns.

Select Board Chair Kristin Hilberg reported the NVTHS Superintendent and School Committee members she recently met with were “very welcoming” and indicated that they are open to Boxborough joining as a member community. Chair Hilberg indicated that Boxborough has also reached out to several other vocational schools in the area but none besides NVTHS indicated that they were able or willing to add another member town.

Before Boxborough could be accepted as a member of NVTHS, each current member community would have to vote at their own Town Meeting to allow Boxborough to join. Current member communities include Ayer, Chelmsford, Groton, Littleton, Pepperell, Shirley, Townsend, and Westford. Boxborough would also need its own Town Meeting vote and would need to demonstrate a commitment to “long term” membership in NVTHS.

The Select Board also voted to enter into an agreement to implement a “Renewable Energy Trust Fund Surcharge” that is required for Boxborough to become a Green Community. The town voted to approve the surcharge at the 2023 Annual Town Meeting (Article 41), and this vote by the Select Board “closes the loop” on the Green Community application process.

Town Planner Alec Wade reported that Boxborough’s application to become a Green Community is currently under review by the state.

Becoming a Green Community allows the town to receive designation funds and apply for further grant funding. The Green Community program is funded by the Massachusetts Department of Energy Resources to help municipalities reduce energy usage and invest in energy efficiency.

The Select Board also held its annual “appoint-a-thon,” during which it appointed and re-appointed both town employee positions and volunteer board and committee positions.
