Planting spring flowering bulbs is done in late fall. They are relatively easy to grow and most come back every year. If you have ordered bulbs from a catalog, they are sent to you around the correct time to plant them. You can also find bulbs at your local garden centers. Here are a few tips:

- If you receive a bulb order and can’t plant right away, open the box and spread the bulbs out to allow air circulation. Store them in a cool room (like a garage), but not where they could freeze.

- You can plant bulbs either in individual holes or in a garden bed. I use a bulb planter for individual holes as it has a handy depth marker on the side. Generally, bulbs look better in groups than in “onesies.”

- Look for a relatively sunny spot to plant. Bulbs do better with more sun. I have good luck with daffodils, grape hyacinths, snowdrops, crocus, scilla, and winter aconite. I love to see tulips in the spring, but I have not had a good experience with them coming back, so I treat them like annuals and replant every year. Or, I just don’t bother.

- Prepare the holes where you want to plant your bulbs. Make sure to loosen the soil and read the instructions on how deep they ought to be planted and how far apart. Not all bulbs have the same requirements.

- Once you have beds or individual holes dug, add some bulb food to the bottom of the hole and add compost to the hole, if you have some. Plant bulbs with the pointy tip up. If you can’t find the tip, look for roots and make sure they are at the bottom of the hole. Add a layer of dirt and pat firmly. Make sure to water them well so that the roots will have a good start. Keep the soil moist until the ground freezes.

- In order to **try** to keep chipmunks and squirrels from digging newly planted bulbs, I put “Repels All,” a granular pest deterrent, on top of the bulbs when I am planting, then add another layer of dirt, and then add more Repels All.

- Other gardeners sprinkle Bar Keeper’s Friend cleanser on the top of the soil as a deterrent. I have also tried cayenne pepper. Once the bulbs are planted, give them a layer of mulch for more protection and to cover up evidence of newly dug areas. It is hard to deter critters.
