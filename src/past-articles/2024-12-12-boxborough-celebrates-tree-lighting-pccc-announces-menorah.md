Around 4 p.m. on Saturday, December 7, friends and neighbors began to gather at the Boxborough fire station – walking along Massachusetts Avenue or carefully crossing from the Blanchard parking lot with the help of Boxborough police officers.

By 4:30 p.m., the crowd began to form a semi-circle around the tall pine tree near the decorated apparatus bays, and the students from the Blanchard BrassWind Ensemble and Blanchard Chorus took their places in front of the tree.

It was time for the much-anticipated annual Boxborough Tree Lighting, hosted for the first time at the fire station.

Boxborough Public Celebrations and Ceremonies Committee (PCCC) Chair Jennette Kollmann began the event by welcoming the crowd and unveiling the newest addition to Boxborough’s December celebrations – a town menorah built and donated to the town by Kollmann’s family, now on display next to the decorated tree.

Kollmann announced that the Boxborough Menorah Lighting will begin December 25 and continue each night of Hanukkah until the last lighting on January 1, 2025. Lightings will take place at 6:30 p.m., and all are welcome to attend. Interested families may sign up to “light” the menorah at [https://bit.ly/41pHRSs](https://bit.ly/41pHRSs).

Kollmann then introduced this year’s town honoree, Lavanya “Lala” Selvaraj, owner of Two Friends Chocolates in Boxborough, to say a few words. The whole crowd joined in a countdown, and Selvaraj flipped the switch to light the tree.

“I'm honored and humbled that Boxborough chose me as the town honoree,” said Selvaraj. “As a business owner, I can tell you that we couldn't ask for a better community or greater support. This amazing community has stood by us from the days our business started in my basement and here we are today. Days like this remind us why we chose to do what we do.”

With the tree lights sparkling, the Blanchard BrassWind Ensemble and Blanchard Chorus launched into a series of holiday favorites. Kids sang along, enjoyed free hot chocolate and cookies, and visited an ornament-making station.

“It was so much fun to perform for such a large and appreciative audience and I was very proud of how well the kids played in very cold conditions,” said Blanchard Band Director Chris Baird.

After two rounds of “Santa Claus is Comin’ to Town,” sirens could be heard in the distance, and soon the flashing lights heralded Santa’s arrival down Massachusetts Avenue and into the fire station parking lot. Kids of all ages flocked to the truck for the chance to greet Santa and receive their candy cane.

“The 2024 Boxborough Tree lighting was a huge success with an incredible crowd of roughly 400 and a wonderful festive atmosphere,” said PCCC Chair Kollmann. “This year’s new location at the Boxborough Fire Station allowed the PCCC to expand the event and allow for more community spirit, with a crafting tent and face painting and a grand entrance for Santa Claus down Massachusetts Ave.”
