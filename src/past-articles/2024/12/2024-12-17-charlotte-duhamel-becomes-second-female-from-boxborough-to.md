On Wednesday, November 20, Charlotte Duhamel became the second female from Boxborough to earn the rank of Eagle Scout. Katie Dallimore was the first Boxborough female to reach this achievement, which she did last year.

The Eagle Court of Honor was held at the Acton-Boxborough Regional School District’s Boardwalk Campus and was attended by family, friends, and members of the Acton and Boxborough Select Boards, as well as State Representative Danillo Sena.

At the ceremony, Duhamel received citations from the MA House of Representatives and the Boxborough Select Board. She received the Eagle pin for herself, and her parents and mentor also received pins. She then gave a speech reflecting on her time in scouts and thanking her mentor.

Duhamel's Eagle Project was renovating the park at Capernaum Place in Lawrence, which is a transitional housing building for families in need of financial assistance. She added seating and picnic tables, built a shed and filled it with donated toys, built corn-hole sets, and planted gardens. The work took place during August and September of this year, though the paperwork for the project took much longer.

Duhamel is a member of Troop 12 Acton, which is a mostly-female Scouts BSA Troop of approximately 20 members. Their members come from Acton, Boxborough, Concord, and Carlisle.

In 2019, when the Boy Scouts officially transitioned to Scouts BSA, allowing girls to join, she helped form the Troop. Last year she held the position of Senior Patrol Leader, which effectively runs the troop. This year, she is the Quartermaster, which puts her in charge of all the troop's gear.

One of her favorite trips with her troop was to Maine, where they spent a week canoeing, sometimes through rapids, and camping. She loved being far away from civilization with all the wildlife, including many moose and eagle sightings. The other activity she really enjoys with the group is orienteering.

Says Duhamel, “Something I love about scouts is how close we grew through our intense adventures. It's an amazing place because of how welcoming everyone is.” Scouting also helped her become an effective leader, overcome her quietness, and challenge herself both physically and mentally. She is now comfortable around snakes and reptiles, and even owns a lizard. She also pushes herself harder on hikes and has more endurance.

“All this confidence was encouraged by Scouts,” she said. “If you are considering scouting, you can check out any troop and find one that fits.” For more information about Duhamel’s troop, visit [actonscouts.org](http://actonscouts.org/) or email [T12-information@actonscouts.org](mailto:T12-information@actonscouts.org).
