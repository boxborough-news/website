At its meeting on March 13, the Acton-Boxborough School Committee held a public hearing on the FY2026 school budget, as required in advance of the final budget vote scheduled for March 20.

At the hearing, Superintendent Peter Light presented the district’s “Recommended Budget” of $116,791,164, which has increased from the district’s “Preliminary Budget” of $116,135,080.

After the Preliminary Budget was first presented in January, the School Committee voted to increase the budget by $150,000 due to expected state revenue, and the district accounted for additional revenue shown in the Governor’s proposed budget. However, the district has also identified additional expenses since January, primarily related to employee health insurance and special education costs.

Superintendent Light began his presentation at the public hearing by stating, “It goes without saying…this has been an extremely disappointing and frustrating budget to develop. We feel as though we are not providing our educators in schools with the tools and resources they need to be able to be their best for their students. I want to say with a real level of sincerity that that is probably the most frustrating thing to have happen as a superintendent of schools…when you just don’t have the resources you need to do what you want to for kids.”

During the School Committee’s discussion, Chair Adam Klein flagged that while the budget increase is only 3% this year, it is expected to be higher in future years. He estimated that in order to maintain services, there will likely be a 5.25% to 5.5% increase next year, which translates to a 10% to 12% increase to Boxborough’s assessment.

“We need a 5.25% to 5.5% increase in our operating budget next year to maintain the level, the relatively meager level, of services that we have now…,” said Chair Klein. “It’s been four years of austerity and there is nothing left to cut…”

The Committee also did a “first read” (an initial review without a vote) of a revised Graduation Requirements Policy regarding the district’s high school diploma requirements and “competency determination.” The district must define its own competency determination now that the MCAS testing requirement has been eliminated by state ballot vote. The policy changes would take effect with the Class of 2025. The revised policy is available at [https://bit.ly/3FwdOzU](https://bit.ly/3FwdOzU).
