On Monday, March 17, during a five-hour meeting with over 50 residents in attendance, the Planning Board conducted public hearings to consider proposed zoning bylaw amendments for Annual Town Meeting.

Select Board Chair Kristin Hilberg presented the proposed municipal governmental use zoning bylaw article that would allow municipal buildings in any district in town by right. Hilberg noted that it was “highly unusual” for the Select Board to bring a zoning article forward. She explained that as a result of investigating sites for the new fire station, it was discovered that the Boxborough Zoning Bylaw does not “clearly allow us to build municipal buildings anywhere in town.”

Hilberg gave a brief history of the Boxborough Zoning Bylaw and explained that in the original zoning bylaw (1965), municipal uses were specifically listed as allowed in every zoning district. However, in 1991, there was a recodification of the bylaw, and the use codes were reorganized and retitled and, as Hilberg stated, “somewhere along the way, municipal buildings disappeared.” Technically, “ they're not an allowed use anymore.”

Much research has been done by town staff, town counsel, and board members, but Hilberg stated there is no record indicating that municipal use was intentionally removed from the zoning bylaw and that this is “more likely an oversight, a scrivener’s error.” She continued, “without passing this article, we literally have no zoning mechanism to build any municipal facility anywhere in Boxborough.”

Members of the Planning Board weighed in and agreed that the missing municipal use definition is problematic and likely a mistake that must be corrected, but they disagreed on whether the use should be allowed in all districts by right. Specifically, the disagreement centers on the allowance of municipal buildings in the Agricultural/Residential (AR) District. Several members of the public voiced their opinions both in favor and against the inclusion of the AR district for municipal use.

The public hearing on this article will resume on March 31, when the Planning Board will be asked to render a non-binding recommendation on the article for the printed warrant.

The Board also held a public hearing on a proposed amendment to the zoning bylaw for a new “Business 2” district. Planning Board member Rich Guzzardi presented a revised version of the proposal that aims to convert certain areas along Mass Ave in the IC (industrial commercial) zone to a new designation of B2 (“Business 2”). Guzzardi's stated objective is “to enable our zoning on Mass Ave to better allow for retail, service, and other consumer based small businesses people desire while lowering the intensity of future development.”

Members of the Planning Board agreed with the objective, but expressed reservations about the readiness of the article for Annual Town Meeting. Several members of the public asked questions and voiced their concerns about the impact on existing businesses along Mass Ave.

The Board voted unanimously to defer the B2 zoning article to Fall Town Meeting. When reached for comment, Guzzardi stated, “While I think the board appreciates what the amendment is trying to accomplish, there is still more work to be done. Based on this, I decided it was in the best interest of the board to defer this to the Fall while we work on improving the amendment, and the board agreed…The intent hasn’t changed, it’s just a matter of how we do it.”

Just before midnight, the Board voted a decision on the application submitted by Bruce Saluk & Associates, Inc. on behalf of the applicant, Astro Crane, to construct two industrial storage buildings at 340 Codman Hill Road. In a unanimous vote, the application was approved with several conditions, including the requirement for a revised tree planting plan that must be submitted prior to the issuance of a building permit.
