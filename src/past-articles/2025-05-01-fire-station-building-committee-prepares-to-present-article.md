At its meeting on April 24, the Boxborough Fire Station Building Committee (FSBC) discussed its preparation for this year’s Annual Town Meeting (ATM), where it will be asking the town for $2.6 million towards building a new fire station at 72 Stow Road.

Article 2 of the Special Town Meeting within ATM, “New Firehouse Design Funding,” requests $2,622,445 for the “design and bidding” phase of the fire station project, and requests the funds specifically for “a new firehouse at 72 Stow Road.”

The FSBC chose the 72 Stow Road site in March, by unanimous vote, after a months-long site selection process that eventually came down to a comparison of two viable options: 72 Stow Road and 502 Mass Ave (the site of the current fire station).

At the time of the site selection vote, FSBC members acknowledged that there is opposition to the Stow Road site from abutters and other members of the public, but expressed they could not vote for the Mass Ave site because it is estimated to cost roughly $6 million more and is expected to be a less functional facility due to site constraints.

The FSBC also acknowledged at the time of their site selection vote that current zoning does not allow a fire station to be built at 72 Stow Road. Several months prior, the town’s Building Commissioner and Town Counsel opined that no municipal construction is currently allowed anywhere in Boxborough without a change to the zoning bylaw. While there may be a path to building a fire station at 502 Mass Ave without a bylaw amendment, through a special permit since it is the site of the current fire station, the Town’s understanding is that a zoning bylaw amendment is required in order to build a fire station at 72 Stow Road.

Accordingly, the Boxborough Select Board has put forward Article 1 of the Special Town Meeting within ATM, “Municipal Governmental Zoning Allowed Across All Zoning Districts.” This article would amend the zoning bylaw to allow construction of municipal buildings in all zoning districts, and thus allow a fire station to be built at 72 Stow Road.

With yard signs for and against Article 1 and Article 2 popping up all over town, both warrant articles are expected to generate much debate at Town Meeting.

At their April 24 meeting, FSBC members considered the possibility that Article 1 might not pass and asked themselves what they would do in that scenario, since Article 2 specifies that the requested funds are for 72 Stow Road.

FSBC members had different opinions on whether, in a potential scenario where Article 1 fails, the FSBC should abandon the 72 Stow Road site and pivot to the 502 Mass Ave site with an amendment to Article 2 on Town Meeting floor.

FSBC Chair Mary Brolin stated that she would consult the Town Moderator, Dennis Reip, about whether such an amendment on Town Meeting floor would be permissible. The FSBC will continue this discussion at its next meeting on May 8.

The FSBC also discussed its public outreach efforts in advance of ATM, including public forums on Monday, May 5, at 10 a.m. and 7 p.m. (hybrid format at Town Hall and on Zoom) and a series of videos about the fire station project that are available here: [https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/727/Fire-Station-Building-Committee](https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/727/Fire-Station-Building-Committee).
