At its meeting on April 28, the Select Board discussed a wide variety of topics including a vote to accept the Fire Station Building Committee’s recommendation for the location of the new fire station, the disposition of the current fire station at 502 Mass Ave, the approval of individual personnel contracts and collective bargaining agreements (CBAs), and a vote in support of a stabilization account for Acton-Boxborough Regional School District (ABRSD) health insurance.

The Select Board voted 4-1 to accept the Fire Station Building Committee’s recommendation to select the 72 Stow Road property as the site of a new fire station, with Board member Wes Fowlks dissenting, saying he disagreed with the recommendation.

Town Administrator Michael Johns asked the Select Board to consider what they would like to do with the existing fire station at 502 Mass Ave once a new fire station is built at 72 Stow Road.

Several Board members wanted to see the building leveled and demolished so as not to confuse residents. When reached for comment, Fire Station Building Committee and Select Board member Priya Sundaram explained that it would be more cost effective to demolish the building and include that cost (demolition) as part of the entire project's budget, rather than hiring a new contractor solely for the demolition. The Board also discussed the possibility of repurposing the building for some other use and the challenges associated with doing so, including the wetlands and high water-table. The Select Board decided to continue this discussion at a future meeting.

The Select Board also voted contracts for the Assessor, Building Inspector, and Land Use Director, as well as CBAs for Police, Fire, and the Department of Public Works (DPW). The Board did not provide the negotiated details, like compensation and benefits, because they said the contracts had to be signed by all parties before any information could be disclosed to the public.

When contacted by Boxborough News after the meeting, Johns said that individual contracts for the Assessor, Land Use Director and Building Inspector were in line with what the Personnel Board recommended for non-contract employees. Johns did point out that the Land Use Director salary was well below the market comparables, so an adjustment has been made in the new contract to bring it up a little closer to the market, to ensure retention in this critical position.

He also said that negotiated contracts for all Collective Bargaining Units included a 2.75% COLA (cost-of-living adjustment) increase in year one and a 3% increase in years two and three. Total costs were $45,000 for DPW, $80,000 for Fire, and $65,000 for Police. The cost of the CBAs will be voted as individual warrant articles at this year’s Annual Town Meeting.

The Select Board also heard from Adam Klein, ABRSD School Committee Chair, who explained the need to establish a Health Insurance Stabilization fund to cover the district’s share of health care costs for employees who need to join the ABRSD health insurance plan outside of annual open enrollment and district budgeting cycle.

Klein explained that the account will initially be funded through refunds from the Acton Health Insurance Trust as it completes paying run-out claims. It is unclear how long the initial funding will last, but the account can be funded through the regular budgeting process. The Select Board voted unanimously to support this action.

Select Board members also discussed their progress in achieving strategic short-term and long-term goals, including budget forecasting, financial stability, succession planning, professional development, customer service and a number of other goals.
