At its virtual meeting on Tuesday April 29, the Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA) held two public hearings to review the special permit applications from two homeowners. They also voted to allow minor modifications to a previously granted special permit for 95 Swanson Road.

Property owner John Halverson, an eight-year Boxborough resident, appeared before the board seeking a special permit to demolish the existing garage and rebuild a new one at his home at 429 Liberty Square Road. Halverson’s plan shows a 26 x 26 structure, which will be used as a two-car garage. All structures on the property meet the required setbacks, but Halverson must seek a special permit because his lot is non-conforming, i.e., it does not meet the 60,000 square foot minimum required in the agricultural/residential (AR) district, as specified in the Boxborough Zoning Bylaw.

The Board discussed the application and noted that the structure would be allowed on the lot in its present location if the lot were conforming. They determined that there was no increase in the nonconforming nature of the structure and voted unanimously to grant the special permit.

In a continuation of a public hearing for a special permit for 70 Summer Road, Paul Kirchner of engineering firm Stamski and McNary, representing owner/applicant Robert Doughty, presented an updated plan to raze both of the existing nonconforming structures on the property and construct a new house and detached garage in a different location.

In the original plan, only one structure – a house with an attached garage – was planned. However, Eve Isenberg of Inkstone Architects explained that there was a design preference for having two structures so that the house would have windows on all sides. The new design minimizes the nonconforming nature of the structures by not exceeding the current nonconforming front setbacks. In addition, the garage will be placed outside of the 100 foot wetland buffer zone, and less of the house area will be inside the wetland buffer zone. A Notice of Intent has been filed with the Conservation Commission who will determine whether an Order of Conditions can be issued.

The ZBA determined that the relocated structures are not substantially detrimental to the neighborhood and voted unanimously to approve the special permit.

In between hearings, the Board heard from Chris Crump, a representative from the engineering firm Kipcon. On behalf of Harvard Ridge Condominium trustees, Crump requested a minor modification to the approved special permit to reconstruct an apartment building at 95 Swanson Road that was destroyed by fire in February 2021.

The special permit was issued to reconstruct a building that was similar to the original building. However, according to Crump, the owners wish to reduce costs and update the style of the building by changing the originally proposed exterior finishes of brick veneer and stucco to a vinyl siding.

ZBA members agreed unanimously that this is a minor modification and that the original special permit is unaffected.
