Over the past few years, native plants have become very popular. That is because they are good for the environment. They support local wildlife, may require less water, and provide nectar for pollinators such as hummingbirds, native bees, butterflies and bats.

However, non-natives aren't necessarily bad. Non-native plants have been in gardens for hundreds of years. The reason we tend to use them is because they come in so many colors and forms, they thrive here, and they are readily available. Of course, we want to avoid planting any that are invasive.

Some of my favorite shade plants do happen to be non-native and include hostas, hellebores, astilbes, bleeding hearts, epimediums, Japanese painted ferns, and brunneras.

It may be difficult to plant only native plants, so one approach might be to plant what you can with natives, and then fill in with your non-native favorites.

Most of my gardens are 15 to 20 years old. They are mature and full, so I now buy very few plants and thus I have not really had the chance to explore natives. However, if I was starting a new garden today, I would include natives.

If you want to add native plants to your gardens, we have a local organization where you can learn more about them and purchase them as well: the Native Plant Trust, located in Framingham. The Native Plant Trust offers programs about native plants as well as a stunning garden known as Garden in the Woods. More information is available at [nativeplanttrust.org](http://nativeplanttrust.org/). You can also check out an organization called Grow Native Massachusetts at [grownativemass.org](http://grownativemass.org/).

I hope you will enjoy whatever plants you choose for your gardens.
