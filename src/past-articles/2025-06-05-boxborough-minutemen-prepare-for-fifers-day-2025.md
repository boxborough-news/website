It’s June, and that means Fifer’s Day is just around the corner. Fifer’s Day 2025 will take place on Saturday, June 21, at Flerra Meadows on Stow Road. The festivities start at 9:30 a.m. with the Fifer’s Four Mile Road Race, followed by a parade kicking off at 11 a.m. from the Blanchard School to Flerra Meadows, where the Golden Fife Award will be presented at noon. Hamburgers, hot dogs, chicken, sausages, soft drinks, beer, and hard cider will be available for purchase starting at noon, and a volleyball tournament will start at 1 p.m.

Throughout the afternoon, there will be live music by Rocky Woods Express and Final Mile, plenty of activities for kids, and more than 50 different booths and exhibits. The event is jointly sponsored by the Boxborough District Minutemen Company and the Town of Boxborough's Public Celebrations and Ceremonies Committee. The Fifer’s Day tradition goes back to 1967, when the newly-formed Boxborough District Minutemen Company celebrated the first “Fifer’s Festival” to commemorate local contributions to the American Revolution. While the town of Boxborough did not exist during the American Revolution, namesake fifer Luther Blanchard lived in the portion of Littleton that later became Boxborough.

Fifer’s Day has grown considerably since the 1960s, but it remains true to its roots: a parade, a cookout, activities for the kids … a quintessential small-town party. Boxborough News spoke with Minuteman Captain Chad Childers about how the Minutemen prepare for Fifer’s Day.

**When do the Minutemen start planning?**

Childers: “The Boxborough Minutemen elect a new Lieutenant in October each year who will be responsible for next year's Fifer's Day, and we reach out to the Town and Public Celebrations Committee in November, but regular planning meetings start in February. This year's Lieutenant is Rich Grady.”

**How many volunteers are involved?**

Childers: “There are 14 department heads and at least 30 other Minutemen who help make Fifer's Day come together, as well as other volunteers from the town who always show up to help with cooking and even volunteers from the high school. This event couldn't happen without the hard work of Ralph Hanrahan, George Neagle, John Fallon, Nathan Soule, Eric Michnovez, Tony Newton, Jim Howie, Dennis Reip, Scott Spalding, Gerry Gaebel, Tim Blankenship, Isaac Childers, and all the others who give generously of their time and energy.”

**What’s the biggest planning task?**

Childers: “Operations planning is probably the single biggest task, but Rich, Ralph, Isaac, Nate, Tony, and Tim do an amazing job. When I was Lieutenant, I was amazed at how smoothly everyone pulled together and how helpful everyone in town was.”

**What is the most fun part of planning?**

Childers: “Just getting together with the Minutemen is the most fun, we always find a reason to laugh and enjoy each others' company, even when working hard at planning and board meetings.”

**Are you planning anything new this year?**

Childers: “Yes, this year we had the 250th anniversary of the Minutemen's march to Concord so we came up with a great new design for our gear, and we plan to recognize this year's Boxborough Minutemen Memorial Scholars, Evelyn Bartley and Alyssa Zeena, in addition to presenting the Golden Fife Award, and the usual great food, local craft cider and beer, music, and kids activities.”

**And finally, you know we have to ask it… what color are this year’s t-shirts?**

Childers: “Like Nathan Hale, I regret... I will never reveal my secret. Volunteers who pitch in and help during Fifer's Day get a stylish pink Minutemen shirt and all the tasty french fries they can eat, though!”
