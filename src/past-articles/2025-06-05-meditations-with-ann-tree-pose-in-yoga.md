Last month we talked about the importance of trees in our lives and in the lives of all the animals we share this space with. This month we’re talking about the importance of Tree Pose as a Yoga position and one that I hope you adopt into your daily life after reading this.

Google “Tree Pose” (Vrksasana) and you find an infinite amount of information about various types, styles and modifications. For me, those modifications are crucial to our success. The two most important ones are: holding onto a wall to begin with and placing the bent leg lower on your standing leg, possibly even toes on the ground with the heel up the ankle.

As you try this, you cannot expect to stand on one foot without moving at all. Even trees in your yard sway and bend to the forces of the wind and rain around it. Your at-home Tree Pose will also be affected by mood, previous exercise, how you slept and how stressed your day has been. A Tree Pose while brushing your teeth will feel different than a Tree Pose right before bed.

The most important aspect of Tree Pose is not being self-conscious about wiggling in the position. I don’t want you to feel unsafe. You should not be at risk of falling so hold onto a wall or chair to eliminate that concern. But the fluctuations at the ankle allows us to become more comfortable with being slightly insecure, a little bit wobbly and more challenged. All of these fluctuations that you feel at the foot and ankle build strength and flexibility that is critical to staying upright as we age.

What I don’t want to see is people falling out of the pose repeatedly. This is ego and we need to ignore it. If you fall in Tree Pose each time you do it, you are over challenging your body to failure and are training your brain to fall out of the pose. Instead, lower your expectations and work on building the strength and flexibility at the ankle to support the one legged stance successfully.

When that becomes easy at a wall, then yes, move it into a room but don’t raise the bent leg foot as high up your thigh as the photos show. Practice it as a smaller challenge and graduate the effort forward in weeks instead of minutes or days.

Eventually, you will be successful in a one-legged stance with your foot placed high on the opposite thigh in the room without props, IF THAT IS A GOAL. If you practice it once a week and have those same expectations, it probably isn’t going to happen. If you practice it twice a day, your progress will be much faster.

I use Yoga as a tool to age as strongly and comfortably as possible. The combination of strength, flexibility and balance helps us physically and mentally as we move (or stand on one leg) through our life's challenges. We want our bodies to live in a comfortable state for as long as possible as we age. Tree Pose is a slow and steady challenge that will help us maintain the ability to do just that.
