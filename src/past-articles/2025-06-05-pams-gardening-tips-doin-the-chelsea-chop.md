The “Chelsea Chop” sounds like a dance, but it is actually a pruning technique named after the Chelsea Flower show in the U.K. It is a technique in which you cut back perennials in late May or early June to promote bushier growth. The stems should be cut back by about a half to a third of their height.

Perennials that do well with this technique are clump-forming plants such as Sedum, Phlox, Echinacea, Nepeta, Asters, Penstemon, Rudbeckias and Yarrow. If you decide to try it, please be sure to water well afterwards and consider fertilizing to promote new growth.

I look forward to trying this with my oversized Autumn Joy sedums that always get too large in late summer!
