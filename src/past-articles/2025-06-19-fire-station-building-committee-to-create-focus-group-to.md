At their meeting on June 12, the Boxborough Fire Station Building Committee (FSBC) continued to discuss how to move forward after Town Meeting voted down design funding for a fire station at 72 Stow Road. The Committee agreed to create a focus group to explore “out of the box” ideas and “come to consensus” around the best location for a fire station.

The Committee plans to invite a group of roughly 10 Boxborough residents to participate in the focus group, along with several members of the FSBC, the Town’s consultants, and the Fire Chief. Invitees will include those who have been vocal in their opposition to the fire station at 72 Stow Road. While some FSBC members expressed concerns about the goals and expectations of such a group, the majority of the Committee agreed that a more casual “roundtable” discussion may be more productive than traditional public input sessions.

The Committee also put more potential fire station locations “back on the table.” The list of alternative locations now includes 502 Mass Ave, 700 & 832 Mass Ave (two properties combined), 1300 Mass Ave, and 1414 Mass Ave. The Committee also made clear that 72 Stow Road is not “off the table” after the Article 2 defeat, as it is still believed to be the least costly and most functional location for a fire station.

The Committee has limited funds left under the current contract with town consultant Context Architecture, which has been helping the FSBC study potential fire station sites. The Town will need a new agreement with Context if further study is needed on multiple sites.

The Committee continued to discuss the two primary areas of public opposition to the fire station project: the size of the station and its cost to taxpayers. The Committee has asked Fire Chief John Kivlan to again reexamine space needs with an eye toward decreasing the size and cost of the project.

Some FSBC members also expressed concern about the costs of “kicking the can,” estimating that further delay will cost the town $125,000 per month.

Before their regular meeting began, the FSBC held an hour-long open forum to hear comments from the public. There were approximately two dozen people in attendance. Comments included concerns about size and cost, a request to think creatively about alternative sites, and feedback that it is difficult to find information about the project on the Town’s website.
