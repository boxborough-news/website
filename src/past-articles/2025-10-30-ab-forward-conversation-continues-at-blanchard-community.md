On Monday, October 27, ABRSD Superintendent Peter Light and the ABRSD School Committee hosted a community forum at Blanchard Memorial School to discuss the AB Forward district reorganization options. The meeting was one part of the district’s efforts to collect feedback from the community about the AB Forward reorganization. There is also a survey open through November 9, available at [https://www.abschools.org/read-the-plan](https://www.abschools.org/read-the-plan).

“AB Forward” is a strategic planning and reorganization initiative to “create an updated strategic plan as well as a set of 3-5 actionable options for district reorganization that could guide district operations for the next 5-10 years.”

The nine reorganization options involve different combinations of closing or merging elementary schools and/or buildings; reconfiguring schools and/or buildings by “grade bands”; and shifting sixth and/or eighth graders to different schools. The consultant is expected to cull the nine options down to 2-3 options to present to the School Committee in early December.

After an introduction by Superintendent Light and School Committee Chair Tori Campbell, the district’s consultant, Joe Costello of District Management Group, walked through the nine reorganization options. All three individuals then took questions and comments from the audience.

With respect to Blanchard, many commenters voiced criticism of Reorganization Option 5, Version 1, the “full grade-band” model in which Blanchard would house all Pre-K and kindergarten for the entire district, and students would move to other buildings for grades 1-3 and grades 4-6.

Some commenters were opposed to this option because it would eliminate the opportunity to have a K-6 elementary education at the district’s only school located in the town of Boxborough. Others were opposed to the grade-band model more generally, citing loss of student community and difficulty of student transitions from school to school.

Audience members also asked about financial implications, student outcomes, facilities logistics, and how the various options would solve (or not solve) the underlying budget problems.

Many speakers expressed a desire for more detail about each of the options, noting that it was hard to weigh in without more information about what the various options would look like - and how much money they might cost and/or save the district.

Speakers also expressed frustration with both the district and the towns of Acton and Boxborough regarding the budget challenges that have led to the AB Forward initiative. Superintendent Light encouraged the public to keep the comments coming.

He emphasized the importance of hearing community feedback as the district considers how to best reconfigure the elementary schools in the face of significant budget challenges. The School Committee is expected to solicit feedback from the public again in January.
