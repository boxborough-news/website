At their meeting on October 23, the Acton-Boxborough Regional School Committee held two separate but intertwined discussions about the AB Forward reorganization initiative and the 2026-2027 school budget. The Committee first heard a presentation on AB Forward from Joe Costello, a representative of ABRSD’s consultant District Management Group.

“AB Forward” is a strategic planning and reorganization initiative to “create an updated strategic plan as well as a set of 3-5 actionable options for district reorganization that could guide district operations for the next 5-10 years.” The nine reorganization options, released earlier this month, involve different combinations of closing or merging elementary schools and/or buildings; reconfiguring schools and/or buildings by “grade bands”; and shifting sixth and/or eighth graders to different schools.

Costello walked through the nine district reorganization options and clarified certain aspects of the various options. For example, Costello noted that, “[i]f the district determines that school closures or mergers are necessary during reorganization, it is most practical to consider schools with lower enrollment and less community preference.”

He identified Merriam School as having the lowest enrollment and Merriam and McCarthy-Towne schools as potentially merging. At least some of the reorganization options would, as currently presented, involve changes to Blanchard Memorial School, the district’s only elementary school located in Boxborough.

For example, reorganization option #5 includes a “full grade-band” model in which Blanchard would house all Pre-K and kindergarten for the entire district, and students would move to other buildings for grades 1-3 and grades 4-6.

The district is currently in the midst of gathering feedback from the community about the reorganization options, through focus group meetings and a survey. The consultant is expected to present 2-3 reorganization options to the School Committee in early December. The AB Forward survey is available here: [https://www.abschools.org/read-the-plan](https://www.abschools.org/read-the-plan). The Committee then moved on to preliminary discussions about the district’s 2026-2027 budget.

First, the Committee reviewed the district’s multiyear projections. “It is very unrealistic to think that the level of funding here [in the model] is available to us, however, we had to put together a multi-year model and this model is based on maintaining our services year over year going through FY31,” explained ABRSD Director of Finance and Operations Sheri Matthews.

The model shows that the biggest budget drivers are salary and benefits, including health insurance costs.

Superintendent Peter Light emphasized that no matter which AB Forward reorganization option is ultimately chosen, the AB Forward initiative “may not fully solve the problem,” given the budget gaps shown in the model.

Next, the Committee discussed draft Budget Guidelines for the 2026-27 school year. The budget guidelines are the School Committee’s instruction to the administration about how to develop the budget.

School Committee Vice Chair for Boxborough and Chair of the Budget Subcommittee Adam Klein stated that although there is a desire for a “level services” budget, that is, a budget that maintains the current services, the towns of Acton and Boxborough have indicated that they cannot provide the funding that would be necessary for a level services budget.

“We can’t cut any more and continue to deliver at the same quality,” said Klein, “so where are we willing to compromise… to find the balance between an excellent education and something the community can afford?” The Committee discussed how increasing class sizes may be a way to increase efficiencies and potentially lead to a better allocation of resources. The School Committee will discuss a second draft of the Budget Guidelines at its next meeting on November 6.
