One question I get frequently is “What can I do about foot pain”? Foot pain that is either chronic or intermittent needs to be addressed yesterday. The sooner you make changes to what is causing the issues, the better the results will be. There are many approaches and all should be experimented with to see what works best for your particular body at this moment in time.

Notice how I said that? The discomfort may feel similar from one episode to another but the fix can change from time to time, let’s look at anatomy first. The foot is a complex mechanical structure composed of 33 joints, 26 bones, and more than a hundred muscles, tendons, and ligaments. Its function is to bear weight, transmit force and allow locomotion.

Think of all the movements you do in the course of a day. Standing and walking come to mind, but also turning, pivoting, jumping, rotating and swiveling on uneven surfaces. This highlights the need for supportive shoes but we also need specific exercises that help develop the balance between the connective tissue and the muscles of the foot. Exercises can help prevent and cure issues that arise as we age.

One simple balance and strength exercise is simply standing up and slowly rocking the weight forward to the toes and backward to the heels. The key word there is slowly! See if you can feel the shifting of the weight forward as it broadens the ball of the foot region. Then, see if you can lift the heels some and concentrate on keeping all of the toes firmly gripping the ground.

Hold onto something if balance is off but keep going. Rocking backwards follows this; as the weight is placed onto the heels, the balls of the feet and toes can come off the ground. Keep the toes spread as far from each other as possible and move between weight forward and heels up and weight backward and ball of the feet up.

I do this exercise a few times a week. You can catch me doing it at the grocery store line, pumping gas, or simply in conversation with friends. It is easy to slip into your day and the benefits are innumerable. Practice this and begin to feel more comfortable in the foot. Your physical balance also increases as an added benefit.
