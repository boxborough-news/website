Motivate and inspire athletes. Build confidence. Keep girls in sports. These are the goals of “Own Your Game,” a Boxborough apparel start-up run by a team of two mother-daughter duos: Magda and Kiley Lyons and Christina and Alyssa Mazzone.

The moms have been friends since before their daughters were born; their daughters have grown up together. They teamed up to launch Own Your Game (OYG) about a year ago.

“During our usual daily catch-up we discussed how cool it would be to do something that we were passionate about,” said Christina. “That led us to our kids and how sports has been such a positive part of their lives, in particular for our girls. We also discussed how fortunate they are to play sports at a competitive level and not all kids are afforded that luxury.”

Magda and Christina wanted to create a clothing brand that was affordable, fun to wear, and would inspire girls to stick with sports. They and their daughters “brainstormed in Magda’s living room,” coming up with a name, a logo, and product ideas. Professionally, the two moms are in chemical engineering and cybersecurity, but they quickly jumped into the world of sourcing apparel, designing logos, and building a business.

Fulfilling orders is a team effort across their two houses. Magda and her daughter Kiley affix the OYG logos and/or customized designs to the apparel at their dining room table, then drive the products over to Christina and her daughter Alyssa, who handle packing and shipping logistics.

At first, their customers were local friends and neighbors. But after they solicited feedback, refined and expanded their products, they now sell online and at local sports tournaments. They just launched a new “Winter 2025” logo this week.

OYG also has a “brand ambassador” program, where female athletes in junior high, high school, and college promote the OYG brand on social media. A unique promo code for each ambassador allows them to earn 10% of the profits from sales associated with their code. Some of the brand ambassadors also help at the OYG booth at tournaments, selling OYG gear and publicizing the business.

Magda and Christina emphasize how participating in OYG teaches their daughters and the other brand ambassadors important life skills; they take orders, handle payments, and build confidence.

Kiley and Alyssa estimate that they spend a few nights per week on OYG, in between homework, practices, and tournaments. Alyssa, a seventh-grader, plays softball and basketball. Kiley, a ninth-grader, plays soccer and basketball. The girls handle social media for OYG, promote the brand to friends and teammates, and come up with new logos and phrases.

In the coming year, the OYG team hopes to increase their charitable giving by partnering with local nonprofits that support female athletes. They also want to partner directly with athletes who can serve as role models and resources for younger girls who want to pursue a sport.

Playing sports instills values that can translate to success in all areas of life, explained Magda and Christina, but so many girls wind up dropping out of sports by junior high; “we just want to help keep girls in sports.”
