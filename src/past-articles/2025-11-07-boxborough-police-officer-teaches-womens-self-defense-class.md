For the second time this year, the Boxborough Police in conjunction with Boxborough Community Services and the Council on Aging offered RAD (Rape Aggression Defense), a free, four-week (12 hour total) self-defense class for women that offers “realistic self-defense tactics and techniques.”

Boxborough Police Officer Erica Abro says she is “passionate” about the RAD classes that she teaches. Abro, a sexual assault investigator, has been a RAD instructor for seven years and taught both of the RAD classes in Boxborough, one in May and most recently in October.

Abro described the class as a mix of strategies that focus on reducing the risk of assault as well as learning actual self-defense tactics. The class addresses various scenarios and what to do in each case. Abro stresses that no background in self-defense or martial arts is needed. According to Abro, “any skill set, any age” can learn how to protect themselves using the information and techniques taught in the class. Her Boxborough students have ranged in age from 17 to 81 years old.

The first session of the class is discussion-based and teaches students about home safety as well as how to recognize and avoid subtle signs of danger outside the home. Abro says that her goal isn’t to scare students–it’s to prepare them by setting “them up to have…tools and (be able) to use them if they need to…and hope they never have to.”

After learning a series of physical techniques in the next two sessions, the final class allows students to practice those skills on “aggressors,” who are volunteers who play the part of the attacker. Abro touted the benefits of the class. “RAD is a wonderful opportunity for women in our community to build confidence, learn practical self-defense skills, and take an active role in their own safety. I’m passionate about this program and excited to see it offered here in Boxborough. I encourage everyone who can to sign up and take advantage of this empowering experience.”

When asked why she took the class, one RAD grad stated, "I wanted to feel more confident and prepared to protect myself if I ever found myself in a dangerous situation. It was a great opportunity to learn practical safety techniques in a supportive environment.” She added, “The class taught me more than just physical defense; it helped me become more aware of my surroundings. The instructors were patient, encouraging and fun which made the learning experience enjoyable.”

Another grad said she took the class because she is a “single woman and new to the area...and felt that it was important to gain tools on how to keep myself safe within my apartment and surrounding area.” She admitted that at first she was “nervous and felt awkward” in class, but she “walked away feeling confident and mentally strong.” She also commented that the class provided an opportunity to meet people and make new friends.

Given the positive feedback from students, Abro plans to offer another RAD class in 2026. For more information, reach out to Officer Abro at eabro@boxborough-ma.gov
