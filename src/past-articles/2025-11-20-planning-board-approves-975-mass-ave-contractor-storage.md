In a meeting on Monday November 17 that stretched past midnight, the Boxborough Planning Board voted 3-1 (with one member recusing himself as an abutter) in favor of granting site plan approval for the construction of an indoor, 8-unit contractor storage facility at 975 Mass Ave. The site is owned by Brett Gutheil of BHG109 LLC, who developed and owns the adjacent property at 881 Mass Ave that serves as building trade rental units. Gutheil was represented by engineering firm Goldsmith, Prest & Ringwall, Inc. (GPR).

During the public hearing, which initially opened on June 16, 2025 and had been continued six times since then, GPR engineer Limhuot Tiv presented updates to the application, provided a rendering of the proposed building as it would appear from Mass Ave, and answered questions from the board that focused primarily on the landscaping plan as it relates to screening the building from the road.

Throughout the public hearings, the Planning Board and members of the public have expressed criticism and concern regarding the architectural style of this and other industrial buildings along the Mass Ave corridor. The proposed building at 975 Mass Ave will have the same aesthetics of the building at 881 Mass Ave, a corrugated steel exterior with a single garage bay for each unit.

After viewing the rendering, Planning Board Chair Rebecca Verner and member Cindy Markowitz expressed concerns that the landscaping would not provide the “dense screen” required by the Boxborough Zoning Bylaw for many years to come and asked the applicant to change some of the selected species and increase the sizes of the new trees. In response, GPR Principal Bruce Ringwall stated, “We are meeting the letter of the bylaw…We meet the intent of the bylaw…We meet more than the bylaw asks for…The zoning doesn’t say that you can’t see a building from the road….”

Planner Alec Wade informed the Planning Board that unless the applicant agreed to another extension of the public hearing, the board would be required to render a decision that evening. The board then asked the applicant to grant an extension of the public hearing or an extension of the deadline to render a written decision. The applicant denied both requests, and the board worked for the next two hours drafting a decision and the conditions for approval.

While all board members voted (4 - 0) to approve the drafted slate of conditions, the vote was split (3 - 1) to approve the application with the approved conditions.

Board member Markowitz voted against approving the application stating, “my concerns are not really being mitigated by the conditions.” When reached for comment about her votes, Markowitz wrote, “I voted against the project because I did not believe it met the Decision Criteria of Section 2.5.7(b) and (c) of the Zoning Bylaw, nor the requirements of Section 4.5 (Route 111 View and Tree Protection) of the Site Plan Rules and Regulations…In my view, the project does not adequately protect community amenities, as it will be visible from the Steel [sic] Farm Trail…Architecturally, although similar to the adjacent building, the design does not harmonize with the broader neighborhood and town, which feature varied building styles. Finally, the project does not protect the Route 111 Corridor.”

Planning Board Clerk Rich Guzzardi, who voted in favor of the application, also provided a statement to Boxborough News explaining his vote and his concerns about the Mass Ave corridor. “I approved the site plan…based on the decision criteria in our zoning bylaw along with the conditions the board placed on the applicant…The Planning Board, constrained by the current zoning bylaw and rules and regulations, is not allowed to approve, deny, or condition an application based on architectural style. I have proposed zoning changes and raised the idea of design guidelines and other strategies that can play a role in trying to address these issues. I believe something must be done to help promote development along the Mass Ave corridor that both preserves our rural, New England heritage and provides for a healthy business base.”

BHG109 LLC must obtain additional permits to proceed with the development of 975 Mass Ave. They have filed an application for a special permit from the Boxborough Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA) to conduct a “building trade” usage in the Business (B) zoning district and a Notice of Intent with the Boxborough Conservation Commission (ConsCom) to conduct work in the wetland resource area.

On Tuesday, November 18, the ZBA voted to continue their public hearing until December 2. On Wednesday, November 20, the Conservation Commission voted to close their public hearing and will issue an Order of Conditions at their meeting on Wednesday, December 3.
