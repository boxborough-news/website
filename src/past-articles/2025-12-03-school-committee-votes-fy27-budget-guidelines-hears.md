At their meeting on November 20, the Acton-Boxborough Regional School Committee voted to approve the FY2027 Budget Guidelines. In their Budget Guidelines, the School Committee “directs the administration to prepare a budget that limits the budget increase to between 3.75% and 4.25%.”

The Budget Guidelines acknowledge that the budget limit will translate to a $2.3-2.9 million reduction from “level services,” that is, the cost of maintaining current services in the schools. The Guidelines also acknowledge this budget cut “is approximately 50% greater” than the cuts that have been made over the past several years.

In presenting the Guidelines for a vote, School Committee Vice Chair Adam Klein of Boxborough reiterated that savings from the AB Forward initiative “does not close the [budget] gap.”

The School Committee also heard the district’s Annual Enrollment Report. Deputy Superintendent Andew Shen explained that student enrollment continues to decline, and student needs continue to grow.

Shen also noted that the percentage of Boxborough students in the district continues to increase. For the past several years, Boxborough students have comprised 17% of the student body. Next year, this number is expected to increase to 18%, and by the 2029-30 school year, it is projected to be just shy of 19%. “Obviously, [this] has implications for the cost-sharing calculations,” said Shen.

The School Committee also held a “legislative visit” with State Senator Jamie Eldridge and State Representatives from Acton and Boxborough, Simon Cataldo and Dan Sena. Much of the conversation revolved around state Chapter 70 funding, which provides per-pupil state aid to districts around the Commonwealth.

School Committee Chair Tori Campbell advocated for changes to the Chapter 70 funding model, explaining that while “on paper” Acton and Boxborough have relatively high property values, the communities don’t have the capacity to absorb tax increases because there are so many residents on fixed incomes. “We really need help from the state,” said Campbell. “It feels like the state is not holding up its constitutional obligation to support education when we’re all in this position.”

The legislators responded that they are in favor of increasing education funding, but noted that the challenge is how to raise revenue to “increase the pie.”

The School Committee’s next meeting is scheduled for December 4. They are expected to hear an update about the AB Forward initiative.
