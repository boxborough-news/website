At their November 24 meeting, the Boxborough Select Board voted unanimously to direct the Town Administrator to prepare two draft budgets: a level-services budget and a level-funded budget. A level-services budget will maintain town services at the same level as the prior year, likely at increased cost. A level-funded budget will maintain costs at the same level as last year, with a likely decrease in services.

The decision by the Select Board to prepare two budgets followed a long and wide-ranging discussion about what “level services” means; why the town needs a Proposition 2 ½ override; how to compare and balance the town budget with the ABRSD school budget; and what should be done about the lack of new growth in town.

Boxborough Finance Committee (FinCom) Chair Tony Newton began the discussion by presenting FinCom's draft budget guidelines for FY2027. He explained that the expected “level services” budget growth of roughly 5.5% will require the town to increase the levy limit through a Proposition 2 ½ override. Newton noted that the Proposition 2 ½ override “is not an unexpected event, it’s not an emergency event, it’s not a panic event… we should see this as a planned and calm event.”

Proposition 2 ½ limits the amount of revenue a city or town may raise from local property taxes each year to fund municipal operations. An “override” increases the amount of property tax revenue a community may raise and becomes part of the base for calculating future years' levy limits. An override requires a vote at Annual Town Meeting and a ballot vote.

Newton shared that FinCom recommends preparing two budgets for FY2027: a level-services budget at less than 5.5% growth that will require passage of an override and a level-funded budget at less than 2% growth in case the override fails. Newton noted that limiting the town and school to 2% growth would mean staff reductions for the town and would be “devastating for the schools.”

Newton explained that there is disagreement among FinCom members about the 5.5% target in the “level services” budget, and disagreement about how to “share the pain” between the town and the school and absorb the school costs. Boxborough’s school assessment is a significant portion of the town’s overall budget.

Select Board Chair Kristin Hilberg expressed concern about what town services might be lost by “cleaving to a percentage,” i.e., the stated 5.5%. She noted that the Select Board will be “much more involved” in the budget process this year.

In the coming month, the FinCom and Select Board are expected to review the Town Administrator’s draft budgets and revisit the issues of potential services cuts, an override, and how to handle the cost of the school assessment.

The Select Board also conducted the FY2026 Tax Classification Hearing. The Board heard a presentation and recommendation from Interim Town Assessor James Doherty and voted to set a single tax rate for all property classes.
