At its November 18 meeting, the Water Resources Committee (WRC) discussed furthering its understanding of Boxborough’s water resources.

The Comprehensive Water Resources Report by consultant Weston & Sampson (W&S) concluded that the groundwater in Boxborough recharges at a rate that is higher than the water demand, such that the town's water supply appears adequate for the future. However, the report addresses only water quantity, not water quality.

Approximately half of the wells in Boxborough are private wells. The rest are “Public Water Systems,” which are defined as systems that provide water for human consumption to at least 15 service connections or regularly serve 25 or more people for at least 60 days a year. In contrast to public wells, the monitoring of private-well water quality is not required.

During the meeting, WRC Chair Les Fox expressed the importance of “getting a handle on the water quality of private wells, which must be done, for at least the immediate future, under tight budget constraints.” According to WRC member Bryan Lynch, the Board of Health is considering a requirement that (private well) water be tested upon property transfer.

The W&S report recommended the exploration of regional water-supply options with neighboring towns and with the Massachusetts Water Resources Authority (MWRA). The WRC will begin gathering information from Acton and Littleton regarding capacity, pricing, and potential intergovernmental agreements.

Lynch argued that a direct connection with the MWRA “should not be precluded.” The MWRA requires that a town possesses a water distribution system to connect, and Boxborough does have a small distribution system for the Hager Well, which may suffice. The WRC will clarify MWRA’s requirements and determine the cost of maintaining this option.

The next WRC meeting will be on December 16 at 7:30 p.m. via Zoom.
