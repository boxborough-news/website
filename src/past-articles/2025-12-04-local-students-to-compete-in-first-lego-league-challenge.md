On Saturday, December 6, a team of five students from Blanchard Memorial and Luther B. Conant Elementary schools will compete in the FIRST LEGO League (FLL) Challenge at Shrewsbury High School. The team, Poseidon’s Coders, includes Rohan and Nikhil Borad of Boxborough (5th and 3rd grade, respectively), and Shourya Peddi, Arjun Nambiar, and Advaith Rado of Acton (4th grade).

The FLL Challenge is an international STEM program for students ages 9–16 that combines robotics with real-world problem solving. Every year, FLL releases a new challenge based on a real-world, scientific theme. This year’s theme, UNEARTHEDTM, focuses on archaeology and how science and technology can help preserve history.

The competition includes an Innovation Project and a robot game mission. For their Innovation Project, Poseidon’s Coders explored how climate change threatens historic lighthouses along the New England coast and designed a rising seawall.

Sandhya Lohi, Bharath Chamarajanagara, Sanket Borad and Hetal Borad coach the team, with Lohi and Hetal Borad serving as the primary coaches for the Innovation Project. Borad described the concept and design to Boxborough News. “The concept uses a chamber with a floating wall panel that lifts automatically as water levels rise—no electricity, no motors. As the storm surge increases, water enters the chamber and pushes the wall upward to shield the lighthouse. When the water recedes, the wall lowers back into place. This design is hidden when not in use and responds instantly to sudden flooding.”

When asked about the team’s design process and her work with Poseidon’s Coders, Borad elaborated, “Building the prototype wasn’t easy. The team experimented with waterproof chambers and smooth sliding mechanisms, and even created a LEGO model with a motorized option. Through trial and error, they learned about buoyancy, hydraulic systems, and real-world engineering principles. Their rising seawall concept shows how innovation and teamwork can help preserve history while tackling modern challenges…”

“We (the coaches) enjoy working with the kids and see how they come up with ideas while we guide them,” said Borad. “This is a true pleasure. Also, I personally would like to thank Qualcomm (corporate sponsor) for their contribution in getting kids involved in STEM from an early age.” When Boxborough News asked what they like most about participating in FLL, both Nikhil and Rohan told us, “It’s fun building things with LEGO!”

To prepare for the tournament, the team invited Boxborough resident Dennis Reip, a registered professional civil/structural engineer (PE) to review their seawall design. When Boxborough News asked Reip for his thoughts, he responded, “While reviewing their lighthouse protection concept, I was impressed by the team's understanding of how climate change threatens our infrastructure as well as their enthusiasm for developing practical solutions.”

At the competition, the team will present their project to a panel of judges, who will evaluate it using rubrics and score sheets. There are 46 teams participating for this qualifying round on December 6. Approximately 30% will advance to the state competition, which is scheduled for December 13 at Worcester Polytechnic Institute (WPI). Only one or two teams from Massachusetts will advance to the [*FIRST®*](https://www.firstchampionship.org/) [Championship](https://www.firstchampionship.org/), which will be held in Houston, Texas in Spring 2026.​
