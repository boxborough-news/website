At its meeting on December 8, the Boxborough Select Board heard a preliminary budget presentation by town administration and discussed whether the town will need to pursue a Proposition 2 ½ override.

At its last meeting on November 24, the Board had discussed the likelihood that Boxborough would need a Proposition 2 ½ override next year and voted unanimously to direct the Town Administrator to prepare two draft budgets, one assuming that the override vote passes and one as a contingency in case the override vote were to fail.

After hearing the town’s budget presentation, Select Board Chair Kristin Hilberg opined that because the preliminary “A Budget” appears to come in under the levy limit, the town may not need to pursue a Proposition 2 ½ override and may not need an alternative “B Budget.”

Finance Committee (FinCom) Chair Tony Newton cautioned that there are “a lot of unknowns” at this point in the budget process and that it is too early to conclude that an override is not needed.

Town Administrator Mike Johns noted that the decision whether or not to pursue an override will be a policy decision made by the Select Board and the Finance Committee. The budget process will continue with the town’s “Budget Saturday” meeting on Saturday, January 3.

When asked for comment after the meeting, Johns wrote, “we're still analyzing each department's expenses for both A & B budgets, as well as revenue projections. This budget is going to be very close, and we already lack excess headroom in the levy, so Administration & Finance will be working very closely with FinCom, Select Board and department heads through a challenging budget year. We will all work very hard to protect services to the community, which also means protecting the people who provide those services, all the while continuing to be fiscally responsible with the Town's resources.”

The Board also heard a presentation from Town Clerk Becky Harris about potentially implementing an electronic voting system (also known as “clickers”) for a future Town Meeting. Harris, on behalf of herself and Town Moderator Dennis Reip, recommended that the town not pursue electronic voting at this time due to the financial and logistical costs not outweighing the potential benefits.

However, several Board members expressed their support for electronic voting, noting that it may make proceedings more efficient and may decrease the “peer pressure” of public voting, allowing voters to “vote their true preference.” The Board asked Harris for additional information, which she is expected to bring to the Board’s next meeting on December 22.

In other business related to Town Meeting, the Board scheduled the 2026 Annual Town Meeting (ATM) for May 18-21, 2026 at the Boxboro Regency and scheduled the town election for June 2, 2026. Town Administrator Mike Johns estimated the cost of holding ATM at the Regency to be $12,000. The town will need to vote at the March 2026 Special Town Meeting whether to fund ATM at the Regency. The alternative location is the Blanchard Memorial School.

The Board also unanimously approved a charter for the town’s new three-person volunteer Board of Assessors. The town approved the creation of a Board of Assessors at Fall Town Meeting in October 2025. Anyone interested in volunteering on the Board of Assessors can submit a letter of interest, including their relevant experience, to the Town Administrator and/or the Select Board.
