At its meeting on Tuesday, December 2, the Boxborough Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA) reopened the public hearing to consider the application for a Special Permit submitted by Goldsmith, Prest & Ringwall, Inc. on behalf of the applicant, BHG109 LLC. The property, located at 975 Mass Ave., is in the Business District and owned by Brett Gutheil.

The Applicant recently obtained site plan approval from the Boxborough Planning Board, but a Special Permit from the ZBA is required for the intended “building trade” use in the Business District.

The board reviewed a decision document, prepared by Associate Town Planner Ian Gilson, focusing on the criteria for granting a Special Permit and the conditions of approval, which include the applicant’s adherence to the Planning Board’s Site Plan Approval Decision.

ZBA Chair Mark Barbadoro asked the board to consider inserting approval conditions that would prohibit external storage of materials and overnight parking of vehicles exceeding 16,500 pounds. Both suggestions elicited considerable conversation and some disagreement amongst board members. Barbadoro indicated that his focus was on aesthetics and usage, noting that the requested use is for “building trades, not landscape contractors.” Ultimately, board members agreed to language that would allow temporary exterior storage of materials and overnight parking for vehicles under 26,000 pounds.

Barbadoro then invited members of the public to speak. Cindy Markowitz, who acknowledged that she was the lone Planning Board member who voted against site plan approval on 975 Mass Ave., stated she was “surprised that there wasn’t much discussion on the various criteria,” noting that the Planning Board’s decision wasn’t in the ZBA meeting packet until a few hours before the meeting. Markowitz stated she was “curious whether…board members had enough time to review the Planning Board’s decision and conditions…I’m just a little surprised there wasn’t more discussion and also that you folks felt sufficiently informed given the lateness of the population of the packet.”

Chair Barbadoro defended the ZBA's process and stated, “I think the board understands that they cannot change anything in the (site plan) decision, so whatever those conditions, are the conditions that we’re going to go with…and it’s our presumption that since (landscaping) screening is in the purview of site plan approval, whatever (the Planning Board) decides is what is right…I think that’s the reason why we’re not diving into the site plan decision and reviewing it really closely.”

ZBA Clerk Shawn McCormack commented that “this project has been in front of us (ZBA) for, I believe, seven different public hearings…and my understanding is that it was in front of the Planning Board for seven public hearings. That is an extraordinary amount of public hearings for a project this inconsequential. And I…resent the suggestion that we are not reviewing this adequately or vetting the project properly because the sole dissenting member of the Planning Board wishes we would come to a different result.”

Markowitz rejected the implication that she was hoping the ZBA would change their decision.

The ZBA closed the public hearing and voted unanimously to approve the Special Permit application with conditions.
