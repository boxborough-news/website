At its meeting on December 16, the Boxborough Finance Committee (FinCom) discussed preliminary budgets submitted by the town for fiscal year 2027 (FY27) and whether the town needs an operational override of Proposition 2½.

Proposition 2½, approved by Massachusetts voters in 1980, limits the amount of revenue a city or town may raise from local property taxes each year to fund municipal operations. This amount is known as the annual levy limit.

An “override” increases the amount of property tax revenue a community may raise and becomes a part of the base for calculating future years' levy limits. The result is a permanent increase in taxing authority.

In anticipation of an override, the town prepared two budgets: Budget “A” if an override is needed and approved by voters, and Budget “B” if an override is not approved by voters. The first pass at Budget “A” came in at 5.4% over last year, or $630,000 below the levy limit, and Budget “B” came in at 2.97% over last year, or $1.3 million below the levy limit.

FinCom Chair Tony Newton said he was “uncomfortable” with the $630,000 buffer because, in his opinion, it was too close to the levy limit. He suggested that the town come up with a compromise budget, which he called “Budget A minus.” Newton noted that last year, the town underspent the operating budget by $1 million, and said that if the town identifies what line items were unspent or underspent, it will be easier to reduce the budget further.

Newton said the first pass at Budget “A” does not include $300,000 for the overlay reserve or $30,000 for the assessor revaluation of properties, both of which improved the excess levy capacity. The “overlay reserve” is used to cover potential property tax abatements, exemptions, and uncollected taxes for the fiscal year.

Newton also said that there are no warrant articles for additional staff or additional staff hours in the FY27 budget. Assistant Town Administrator (ATA) Rajon Hudson said most of the reductions in the budget came from expenses and some reduction in staff hours, but no reductions in headcount.

FinCom members Michelle Ryan and Maria Neyland wanted specific information on which expenses were cut and why. Neyland noted that some expenses, like travel and conferences, were increased in the proposed FY27 budget. She cautioned that departments should not cut expenses and assume that they can later request a “reserve fund transfer” to cover a shortfall in their budgets.

Boxborough’s reserve fund transfer policy requires that the town set aside 0.6% of the operating budget to fund “extraordinary and unforeseen” expenses; currently, that amount is $175,000. Historically, the reserve is funded through taxation. Newton said that some towns use “free cash” to fund the reserve, which would provide an additional buffer between the operating budget and the levy limit.

“Free cash” is a term that refers to unspent funds left over at the end of a fiscal year. The annual budget approved at Town Meeting each year is a good-faith estimate of revenues and expenses, but it is never exact. Sometimes free cash is generated because revenue is greater than anticipated and sometimes because expenses are lower than budgeted. The use of free cash does not require any additional taxation.

After prolonged discussion, FinCom members agreed that a $1 million buffer between the operational budget and the levy limit is needed and asked for a revised Budget “A” from the town.

FinCom is scheduled to meet with the Select Board, town administration, town finance team, and department heads on Saturday, January 3, 2026 at 8 a.m. at Town Hall. The meeting is known as “Budget Saturday” and is an in-person meeting.
