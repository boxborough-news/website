At its meeting on March 18, the Finance Committee (FinCom) discussed the overall financial health of the town – including financial pressures on the budget, the town’s levy limit, and a potential Proposition 2½ override that the town will likely face in Spring 2026 for the FY2027 budget.

By way of background, Proposition 2½, approved by Massachusetts voters in 1980, limits the amount of revenue a city or town may raise from local property taxes each year to fund municipal operations. This amount is known as the annual levy limit.

An “override” increases the amount of property tax revenue a community may raise and becomes part of the base for calculating future years' levy limits. The result is a permanent increase in taxing authority.

Given the lack of projected growth (new construction) in Boxborough and the pressures on the budget, including projected increases in the Acton-Boxborough Regional School District assessment, town health and pension benefits, and the potential for fire station funding, the town’s budget will likely require an override next year.

FinCom Chair Antony Newton said that an override is a “cyclical event.” It gives the town additional capacity to meet expenses through taxation. The last time Boxborough faced an override was 20 years ago.

FinCom meetings are scheduled every Tuesday during the budget season at 7 p.m. at Town Hall. The final vote on the town budget is scheduled for Tuesday, March 25. The meetings are hybrid.
