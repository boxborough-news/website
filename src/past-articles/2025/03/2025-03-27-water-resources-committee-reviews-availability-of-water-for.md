At its March 18 meeting, the Boxborough Water Resources Committee (WRC) discussed with Boxborough Fire Chief John Kivlan the availability of water throughout town for the purpose of fighting fires.

The Boxborough Fire Department uses water from the fire cisterns and fire ponds to fight fires. Chief Kivlan explained that there are currently 28 cisterns and 19 fire ponds in Boxborough. The status of all of them is not known. The Chief is developing a map of the fire ponds and cisterns, so that the access to them and coverage that they provide can be determined. In addition, any repairs or upgrades required for the cisterns will be identified. The person or entity responsible for the maintenance of a fire pond or cisterns varies within Boxborough. Chief Kivlan is also investigating how similar towns (towns that lack town water) manage cisterns and fire ponds.

The WRC also discussed a draft interim report from Weston & Sampson, a consultant working with the Town of Boxborough to conduct a preliminary assessment of Boxborough’s water resources, future water needs, and wastewater demands.

The WRC’s discussion focused on the section of the report, in large part completed, that summarizes and maps current conditions in Boxborough. The section includes information such as soil types, aquifers, and impervious areas (such as roads and roofs). More GIS data and a breakdown of the various types of impervious surfaces are needed.

WRC Chair Les Fox reported that the Littleton waterline project is on schedule. This project will provide water to Boxborough residents living west of Interstate 495. The details of how water fees will be paid will be worked out with condominiums and homeowners in the area. Road closures related to the project will occur later in the year.

The next meeting of the WRC will be April 15 at 7:30 pm.
