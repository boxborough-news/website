At their monthly meeting on March 25, the Boxborough Housing Board (BHB) focused on the town’s MBTA Communities Act compliance and the Homeowner Opportunity for Preservation & Equity (HOPE) program.

Regarding town compliance with the MBTA Communities Act, the BHB discussed the work of the town’s 3A Advisory Committee, which is tasked with providing zoning alternatives for Boxborough’s MBTA Communities district.

BHB members expressed concerns about the possibility of “paper compliance” that would allow the town to meet the zoning expectations set forth by the MBTA Communities Act without any expectation of actually building multi-family housing in the newly zoned areas.

The BHB agreed to recommend that the town pursue more than “paper compliance” with the MBTA Communities Act. The BHB plans to write a memo to urge the 3A Advisory Committee to recommend only those districts where multi-family housing could actually be built.

The BHB also discussed past and future HOPE projects. First, the Board revisited a conversation about damages that occurred during the Boxborough Meadows roof replacements. Town Planner Alec Wade has secured a commitment from the contractor to fix damages now that the weather is improving. Second, the Board discussed their previous decision to add clear income eligibility guidelines for future HOPE projects; two members agreed to draft language that reflects the 80% median income amendment. Also in service of carrying out future HOPE Projects, BHB filled out an “expression of interest (EOI)” form to be considered for state grant funding in the amount of $200,000 to fund the next proposed HOPE project, replacing aging water-heaters across the 20 affordable housing units under the purview of the Housing Trust.

The last order of business was to discuss estate planning for Chapter 40b affordable housing unit homeowners. BHB Chair Al Murphy spoke with Stefanie Petersen of the Metro West Collaborative Development consulting firm about what the current law is and how the BHB could make sure homeowners on deed-restricted properties have a viable option for ensuring their homes stay out of probate court and continue to fulfill the town’s required subsidized housing inventory. The BHB will continue this discussion at the next meeting.

Finally, Boxborough Community Services Coordinator Wendy Trinks provided an update on the Boxborough Rental Assistance Program (BRAP); there are currently 17 active participants. Trinks has received positive feedback about the program’s impact. Up for vote at Annual Town Meeting (ATM) is a change to BRAP’s financial assistance disbursement, to increase the flat rate of $250/month to a need-based scale of up to $500 per month in rental assistance.
