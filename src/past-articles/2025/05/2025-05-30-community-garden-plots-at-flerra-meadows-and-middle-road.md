At its meeting on Wednesday, May 27, the Boxborough Agricultural Commission (Ag Com) discussed the status of the community garden plots at Flerra Meadows and on Middle Road (at the intersection of Depot Road). The Ag Com oversees both gardens. Plots at both gardens are still available. Gardeners can request a 10 x 20-foot plot ($20) or a 20 x 20-foot plot ($30).

To request a plot at the Flerra Garden, fill out the application on the Town website: [https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/DocumentCenter/View/507/Flerra-Meadows-Community-Garden-Application-PDF](https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/DocumentCenter/View/507/Flerra-Meadows-Community-Garden-Application-PDF). To request a plot at the Middle Road Garden, send an email to [middleroadgarden@gmail.com](mailto:middleroadgarden@gmail.com).

The two gardens differ slightly from each other in a few ways. The Flerra Garden is one of the many uses at Flerra Meadow Conservation Land, which is under the Conservation Commission’s purview.

The Conservation Commission has given the Ag Com the ability to run the garden, but since the garden is on conservation land, gardeners are required to use organic practices and must comply with rules and regulations regarding the use of herbicides, insecticides, and fungicides.

To read the rules and regulations for the Flerra Garden, visit [https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/DocumentCenter/View/506/Flerra-Meadows-Community-Garden-Rules-and-Regulations-updated--4-2024-PDF](https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/DocumentCenter/View/506/Flerra-Meadows-Community-Garden-Rules-and-Regulations-updated--4-2024-PDF). The Middle Road Garden is on municipal land, and organic practices are not required.

Another difference between the two sites is fencing. At Flerra, a seasonal perimeter fence, funded by Community Preservation Act funds, protects the entirety of the garden. At Middle Road, gardeners fence if they choose.

Lastly, at the end of each season, the Flerra Garden is tilled and seeded with a cover crop, which provides nutrients to the soil. At the Middle Road Garden, gardeners planning to return to the same plot make their own preparations for the next season. This allows them to plant perennials, such as asparagus, strawberries, chives, and oregano.

Both sites provide plenty of sun and a source of water. Gardeners must hand water their plants using their own containers; hoses and sprinklers are not permitted at either site. For further questions about renting a garden plot at either site, reach out to Agricultural Commission Chair Owen Neville ([oneville@boxborough-ma.gov](mailto:oneville@boxborough-ma.gov)).
