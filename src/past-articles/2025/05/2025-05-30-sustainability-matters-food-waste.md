According to the United States Department of Agriculture, between 30 and 40 percent of all food in the U.S. is wasted annually. Food waste occurs across the supply chain – from farmers growing excess to account for unexpected weather to retailers’ challenges in managing inventory and consumer overbuying.

Nearly half of all food waste happens at home, and discarded food is the single largest category of waste in municipal landfills. When food is wasted, the water and energy used to produce it are also wasted, as well as the land used to produce food that never gets eaten.

Use of land for agriculture contributes to decreasing biodiversity. Additionally, the use of pesticides and fertilizer (which are energy-intensive to produce) on food that is eventually wasted can lead to runoff affecting soil and water quality.

While reducing food waste across the supply chain will require cooperation among farmers, retailers, food service, and governments, there is still much we can do as consumers here in Boxborough to reduce food waste in our homes.

Ideally, we could prevent food waste entirely by only buying and serving what is needed. If it works for you, more frequent but smaller grocery shopping trips can minimize food that won’t be consumed. If you know you’ll be travelling, it can be a good idea to use up food that will expire while you’re away. Unopened food can be donated to the Acton Food Pantry.

In the event that food does go bad, it can be composted – either at home, if you have a home composting operation, or at the Boxborough Transfer station, if you have a transfer station sticker.

If you’re interested in sharing food or “rescuing” food that might otherwise go to waste, visit Boxborough’s FreeBee Market on Saturdays from May through October. You can bring excess food to share, as well as rescue excess food to bring home.

FreeBee Markets are held at 10:30 a.m. at 30 Middle Rd, on the church lawn across from Town Hall. More information about FreeBee Market can be found at [https://www.facebook.com/FreeBeeCommunity/](https://www.facebook.com/FreeBeeCommunity/).
