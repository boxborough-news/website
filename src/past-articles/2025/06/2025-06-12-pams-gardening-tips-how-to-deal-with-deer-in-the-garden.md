One of a gardener's biggest challenges is dealing with deer. It is heartbreaking to come out to find your garden has been nibbled on or worse. Unfortunately, deer are browsers by nature. There is no perfect solution except a deer fence which is expensive and may not be practical. So there are several strategies you can try:

Plant deer repellent plants: There are many plants that deer don’t like due to the scent, taste, or texture, such as daffodils, peonies, echinacea, rudbeckias, salvia, catmint, rosemary, lavender, ferns, boxwoods, and oak leaf hydrangeas. However, never say “never.” If they’re really hungry, they may eat them!

Plant the delicious plants we know they love, such as hosta and tulips, close to your house, as they may not be bold enough to get that close.

Use commercial deer repellents: There are many brands such as Deer Off, Bobbex Deer, and Liquid Fence. Check the labels to see if they can be used when bees are foraging. I use milorganite, which is an organic nitrogen fertilizer made from treated human sludge. It is made from human waste; it does not have a bad smell to humans, but does to deer. I just scatter it like chicken feed. The only problem is that you need to reapply if it rains, which is true of most deer repellents.

Use natural deer repellents: There are great recipes on the internet for egg-based repellents and spicy smelling and tasting mixes. Some people use hair – human or dog – sprinkled on their garden. Others swear by shaved ivory soap on the soil as well as hanging bags of Irish spring soap.

Honestly, it makes sense to try all these methods and change them often so the deer don’t get used to it.
