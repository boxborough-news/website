At the entrance to the third annual R.J. Grey Junior High School Civics Engagement Fair, visitors were greeted with a message reminding them that “When young people learn about civic skills and feel committed to making a difference, our democracy becomes healthier” (Tufts University Center for Information and Research on Civic Learning and Engagement).

Over 400 7th grade students focused on real-world issues of importance to their school and the greater world community. Students tackled issues such as immigration, affordable housing, disability access, pay equity, climate change, stopping hate crimes, bullying, veterans issues, and healthcare access, to name just a few. One student group analyzed the need for more restaurants in Boxborough and even proposed a zoning bylaw change to make that happen.

Seventh grade teacher Dr. Kathryn Contini said “The Civics project is a unique opportunity for R.J. Grey students to …. put their research, reading, writing, technology and critical thinking skills to work to create a persuasive presentation. The hope is that …. sharing these projects with Acton and Boxborough community members will spark change and inspire teens toward civic engagement.”

The Civics Fair was supported by the League of Women Voters – Acton Area (LWV-AA), who listened to student presentations, asked questions, provided feedback and commended students for their hard work, and by Staples, who printed the certificates at no cost to the project. Leslie Hogan, Steering Committee member for the LWV-AA and co-chair of the event, said that “student projects were diverse and inspirational.”

Student-led civics projects became a formalized portion of the junior high school curriculum through the enactment of state legislation S2631 – an act to promote and enhance civic engagement. R.J. Grey teachers approve student projects following the Department of Secondary and Elementary Education (DESE) guidelines.
