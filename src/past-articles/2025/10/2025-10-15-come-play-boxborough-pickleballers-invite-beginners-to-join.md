As the pickleball season winds down, players at the Liberty Fields courts are already looking ahead to the spring season, and they have one simple message for Boxborough: come play!

Pickleball is a fast-growing sport that combines elements of racket sports like tennis, badminton, and ping-pong. It is typically played on a smaller court, about a third of the size of a tennis court, with either two or four players using lightweight paddles and a perforated plastic ball. The goal of the sport is simple: rally the ball back and forth over a low net without letting it bounce twice or outside of the court.

In Boxborough, players in their thirties through their seventies, with occasional teens joining too, gather at the Liberty Fields courts (1100 Liberty Square Road in Boxborough) throughout the week for "open play," where anyone can drop in and join a game.

Boxborough resident and pickleball organizer Mark Marlow has also facilitated beginner-only sessions, making it easier for newcomers to learn and play comfortably.

Around ten players typically gather at two allocated courts and, based on this year's success, Marlow hopes to expand the program to next season. He has asked the Boxborough Recreation Commission to officially recognize the beginner sessions and provide three courts for play. While the times vary, most sessions take place in the mornings, and players plan to continue their meet-ups until the Recreation Commission closes the courts based on weather conditions, typically around mid-November.

To get involved, residents can download the app “TeamReach” and join the community with the code **01719PB** for updates and scheduling.

"Boxborough is already an awesome community," said Marlow, "...part of having a good retirement is staying healthy, and pickleball is a great way to do that. Enjoy the sport and enjoy the community."

Players describe pickleball as more than just a sport: it is a way to stay active, build friendships, and strengthen local connections. "For years, I came to the fields around 6:30 in the morning to exercise," said Lew Blatt, who joined right after retiring. "Mark (Marlow) formed a beginner group, and I instantly made six to seven new friends. Exercise and socialize, just the thing I needed for my retirement."

Christine Marlow, who voted for the pickleball courts at a town meeting years ago, echoed that sentiment. "It gives me a chance to play at my own level, exercise, and make new friends," she said. "I want more Boxborough residents to start playing. Beginners shouldn't feel intimidated; it's a relaxed and fun way to learn."

For others, the draw is the mix of fitness and friendship. Mahendran and Suresh, two regulars at the court, both discovered the sport through friends. "We actually became friends after playing here. It's a great combination of badminton and tennis," said Mahedran. "You get physical and mental fitness while building a community, too. It's awesome, and I've met so many new people."

Even as the outdoor season ends, there are opportunities to stay involved. "There are private clubs to join at low cost," said Mark. "Join us in the spring… It's a beautiful thing."

Whether you're an athlete or someone looking for a new hobby, the message from the Boxborough pickleball community is clear: grab a paddle, show up on a Saturday morning, and be prepared to get addicted.
