At their meeting on October 27, the Boxborough Select Board began a discussion about transfer station sticker enforcement and potentially increasing the cost of a transfer station sticker. Boxborough Department of Public Works Director Ed Kukkula reported to the Board that his department has recently increased enforcement of (and education about) the town’s transfer station sticker policies.

He explained that his team is often too busy to check every vehicle for a sticker, but they have been trying to note the license plates of vehicles that enter without a sticker so that the town can send out a sticker application to the relevant household. Kukkula agreed that more enforcement of the sticker policy at the transfer station entrance is needed, but also logistically difficult. Residents who wish to use the Boxborough transfer station must purchase a transfer station sticker each year. The cost of the sticker is $150 or $180 per year, depending on when it is purchased. The sticker is free for senior citizens and those with an Affordable Housing Trust Waiver. Kukkula estimated that the town issues roughly 1000 stickers each year.

Kukkula explained to the Board that the transfer station is currently “in the red” and the deficit is increasing each year. He noted that while the town’s total tonnage stays roughly the same each year, the town’s costs to dispose of the waste is increasing. Currently, trash disposal costs the town $107 per ton, recycling disposal costs $118 per ton, and food waste disposal costs $170 per ton. Select Board Chair Kristin Hilberg noted the tension between balancing “sustainability and fiscal responsibility.”

Chair Hilberg stated, “I think we should raise the cost of the sticker; private hauling has gone up substantially and we haven’t.” She then clarified that she is not in favor of charging a fee to seniors for a sticker. Hilberg indicated that the conversation about both enforcement and sticker fees will continue at a future meeting. The Board also approved an On-Premises Restaurant All-Alcoholic Beverages License for Thinking Flower Coffee Shop, Inc. of 629 Mass Ave, Suite C, after a brief public hearing.

During the hearing, Thinking Flower owner Divya Suryaprasad, a pastry chef, described the pastries and coffee she will offer in her “small-scale coffee shop and bakery” and explained how having a liquor license will help make her business “more sustainable.” She plans to offer wine and beer, and possibly cocktails, alongside her desserts. Suryaprasad expects to open Thinking Flower in early November once she receives her final permits from the Board of Health. Thinking Flower’s hours will be Wednesday-Friday from 7:30 a.m. to 3 p.m. and Saturday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.

Finally, the Board heard a presentation on a water report recently completed by consulting firm Weston & Sampson, working with the town’s Water Resources Committee (WRC). The report concludes that the groundwater in Boxborough recharges at a rate that is higher than the water demand, such that the town's water supply appears adequate for the future. However, the report addresses only water quantity, not water quality. Select Board members discussed ways in which the town might be able to encourage private well water testing and collect water quality data from homeowners.
