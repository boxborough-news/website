At its meeting on October 28, the Boxborough Housing Board (BHB) discussed the Homeowner Opportunity for Preservation and Equity (HOPE) program, estate planning regulations on affordable-housing owners, Boxborough Rental Assistance Program (BRAP) funding, and the Town’s housing production plan.

The HOPE program helps owners of BHB affordable units preserve the quality of their units by providing financial assistance for repairs. Phase I of the HOPE program, roof replacement at Boxborough Meadows, is now complete, and the Board has finalized the application and project guidelines for Phase II of the program, which is a grant program that offers up to $2,000 per household for repairs that are “necessary for the long-term viability of the unit.” The BHB is in the process of notifying eligible owners of this opportunity.

In an ongoing discussion, BHB members addressed the lack of estate planning options available to affordable-housing owners. The current regulations of the State of Massachusetts Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities (EOHLC) forbid affordable units from being held in a trust, which complicates estate planning. BHB members acknowledged that this policy has already had a negative effect on some Boxborough affordable-unit owners, and they voted unanimously to support the Citizens Housing and Planning Association’s (CHAPA) efforts to change the regulations.

The BHB will be applying for $69,000 in Community Preservation Act funds for BRAP for fiscal year 2027, which begins on July 1, 2026. This amount is unchanged from the current fiscal year. BRAP provides rental assistance to income-eligible households who are renting qualifying units in Boxborough. For questions about eligibility or assistance completing an application, residents may email Community Services Coordinator [Wendy Trinks](mailto:wtrinks@boxborough-ma.gov) (wtrinks@boxborough-ma.gov) or call 978-264-1735.

Associate Town Planner Ian Gilson met with the BHB to discuss the update of Boxborough’s Housing Production Plan (HPP). The previous plan was drafted in 2015 and has not been updated since then. For reference, Gilson researched and distributed HPPs from two other towns and discussed three possibilities for funding the Technical Assistance Program needed to update the plan. Boxborough’s HPP aims to “detail a plan to make available a range of housing that is affordable to low and middle-income families of all generations.” To view the current HPP, visit [https://tinyurl.com/BoxHPP](https://tinyurl.com/BoxHPP).

BHB member Phyn Vermin gave a brief presentation to the board on cohousing and intentional communities and how they might fit into Boxborough’s housing inventory. Board members will continue discussing this topic at their next meeting, which is on Tuesday, December 2 at 7 p.m. at Boxborough Town Hall.
