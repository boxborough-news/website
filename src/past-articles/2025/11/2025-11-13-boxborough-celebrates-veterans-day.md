On November 11 at 11 a.m., despite the overcast and near freezing temperatures, almost 100 people came together at the Veterans’ Tribute Memorial at the Sargent Memorial Library to honor veterans and their families. Included in the crowd were representatives of the Boxborough Fire and Police Departments, Town employees, staff and officials, Boxborough Scout Troop 1, and the Acton-Boxborough Madrigal singers.

Boxborough resident Ron Vogel, a Navy veteran, opened the ceremony, noting that this was its fifth year. He read the names of the four Boxborough veterans who had passed away in 2025 and those of the six Boxborough men etched on the memorial wall who died in service.

Select Board member Priya Sundaram and the Scouts led the Pledge of Allegiance followed by the Madrigals, who sang ‘America the Beautiful.’ After a musket salute fired by the Boxborough Minutemen, Boxborough Community and Social Services Administrative Assistant Ami Scheen and the scouts read the Governor’s Veterans Day Proclamation for the Commonwealth of Massachusetts.

Boxborough Historical Commission Chair Alan Rohwer outlined the history of Veterans Day, which started with a commemoration of the armistice that ended the fighting of World War I on the 11th hour of the 11th day of the 11th month of 1918.

Town Administrator Michael Johns, naval aviator and proud parent of multiple service members, started his remarks by reminding everyone that we live in an area where the earliest chapter of our nation’s history was written. “This is a story that began with ordinary citizens, including Luther Blanchard, becoming extraordinary patriots.” He urged everyone, when they look at the flag, to “remember that the colors mirror qualities we see in the veterans among us: red for courage, white for honor, and blue for loyalty.” Veterans, he said, “remind us that freedom is not inherited but earned and defended by each generation and that patriotism is not division but devotion, not words but deeds.”

Scheen also shared a message from the Town’s Veterans Services Agent James MacRae. In his remarks, MacRae noted that “Veterans Day is set aside each year to honor those who have served in the armed forces because remembrance matters. Remembrance is more than reciting history; it is reaffirming the bond between the freedoms we enjoy and the sacrifices made on our behalf. Celebrating Veterans Day keeps the story alive, reconnects civilians and veterans, offers gratitude in action, prompts reflection and responsibility, and preserves legacy.”

Lastly, Army veterans and Boxborough residents Nancy Warren and Wayne Richardson laid a wreath on the monument while Maddie Sjoblom, a Blanchard School alumna, played taps. Ron Vogel ended the ceremony by thanking all who had attended and also all who had a part in creating the monument.

Boxborough Veterans’ Services Agent James MacRae (978-929-6614) has regular office hours at Boxborough Town Hall Tuesdays from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m., appointments preferred. Additionally, MacRae is available to veterans, dependents, and veteran widows and widowers on additional days and times by appointment.

More information about Boxborough’s veterans’ services can be found at [https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/281/Veterans-Services](https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/281/Veterans-Services).
