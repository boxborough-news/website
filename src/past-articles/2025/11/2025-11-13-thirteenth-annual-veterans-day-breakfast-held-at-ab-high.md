Over 100 veterans and their guests gathered at Acton-Boxborough Regional High School for the 13th annual Veterans Day Breakfast sponsored by the Acton-Boxborough Regional High School National Honor Society and the Rotary Club of Acton-Boxborough.

Vice President of the AB National Honor Society and Boxborough student Sarah Yanagimachi said that the annual Veterans Day Breakfast is one of the “most meaningful” events sponsored by the AB National Honor Society. Julia Zhou, also a Boxborough student, concurred saying that the celebration of veterans is “our way of showing appreciation for their service to our nation.”

Veterans from all branches of the armed services (Army, Navy, Marines, Air Force, and Coast Guard) were represented at the breakfast.

Boxborough News gathered some interesting information about some of the Boxborough residents who attended the breakfast. Navy veteran Ray Landry was part of a team that opened a communications center in Spain, where they serviced the U.S. fleet in Europe and Africa. Army veteran Hoff Stuart was drafted during the Vietnam War; he was stationed in Alaska where his unit was assigned to clean up nerve gas shells buried in a lake. Steve Schmitt, who served in the Navy on a nuclear submarine, was deployed to conduct training missions in Scotland and the Mediterranean and Bering Seas. Bob Cooke, who joined the Air Force in 1976, was stationed in North Dakota before being sent to Turkey, where he worked in a security police unit, guarding nuclear weapons.

While service to the country wasn’t always convenient or comfortable, and often dangerous, they persevered to defend democracy.

Veterans’ Services Officer for Acton and Boxborough James MacRae spoke about veteran disability benefits and disability indemnity compensation benefits available to eligible survivors of veterans who died as a result of service-related disabilities.

MacRae also spoke about Agent Orange, which affected not just soldiers who served in Vietnam, but also those who served in Guam, the demilitarized zone in Korea, Thailand, Laos, and Cambodia. He explained that the “presumptive conditions” (diseases presumed to be associated with Agent Orange) have expanded over the years. Those presumptive conditions now include hypertension and ischemic heart disease (coronary heart disease), even when they occur later in life.

MacRae encouraged veterans to contact his office at 978-929-6614 to schedule an appointment. Additionally, he is available at Boxborough Town Hall every Tuesday from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. to provide support to veterans and their families.
