At its meeting on November 18, the Finance Committee (FinCom) continued to discuss its budget strategy for fiscal year 2027 (FY27). Using an historical ten-year average increase in the levy (taxation) of 5.5% and the levy limit of 4.3%, the town has projected a shortfall in the operating budget for FY27. Under the circumstances, the town will seek an operational “override” of Proposition 2 ½ for FY27 at Annual Town Meeting in May 2026, followed by a required ballot vote.

Proposition 2½, approved by Massachusetts voters in 1980, limits the amount of revenue a city or town may raise from local property taxes each year to fund municipal operations. This amount is known as the annual levy limit. An “override” increases the amount of property tax revenue a community may raise and becomes part of the base for calculating future years' levy limits. The result is a permanent increase in taxing authority.

Historically, the town has had two prior overrides. The first was in 1989, which initially failed, but eventually passed in 1991. The second override was in 2003. That override also failed the first time around, but passed in 2005.

Given the uncertainty of an override vote, FinCom plans to develop two separate operating budgets. Budget “A”, which requires an operational override, is a “level services” budget and would allow “up to” 5.5% overall growth, including the Acton-Boxborough Regional School District (ABRSD) assessment. Excluding contract positions and collective bargaining agreements, the budget guidelines also call for a “freeze on positions, hours, and salary adjustments.”

Budget “B” would be a “level funded” budget with growth limited to less than two percent. The town would have to make staffing reductions in order to meet that target. In both budgets (A and B), warrant articles would be funded through free cash whenever possible. FinCom Chair Tony Newton said that he reviewed the FinCom budget guidelines with Town Administrator Michael Johns, who agreed with the strategy. Newton has also been in contact with Select Board Chair Kristin Hilberg and will present the FinCom budget strategy and guidelines to the Select Board at its next meeting on November 24.

FinCom members had different perspectives on the budget and the override. FinCom Vice Chair John Connor said that an average levy increase of 5.5% means that “the town has been expanding services beyond inflation.” Member Michelle Ryan said that, with current economic indicators and the potential for reductions in federal and state aid, the town should not be increasing the operating budget at the 5.5% level. With an aging population on fixed incomes, Ryan said we should be working on an “eat your vegetables (kind of) budget.” She said that we need to protect our population before we are forced to make cuts to critical services. As it stands right now, Ryan indicated that she would not support the “A” budget.

Member Maria Neyland expressed concern about expenses like pensions and health care benefits that cannot be controlled by the town. The only way to control either of these expenses is to freeze hiring and additional hours for existing staff, she said.

During the meeting, Chair Newton asked School Committee member Liz Fowlks if the School Committee was preparing a “B” budget if the override was not approved by Boxborough taxpayers. Fowlks said, “we’re being conservative, looking at two different options.”

Boxborough News reached out to ABRSD School Committee Vice Chair Adam Klein, who said that the school committee was, in fact, planning to do a “B” budget. Klein said the school committee budget guidelines are projected at 3.75% to 4.25%, which would mean an increase in the Boxborough assessment of 5.58% to 6.17%, which is a function of enrollment.

The next FinCom meeting is scheduled for Tuesday, December 2 at 7 p.m. at Town Hall. The meeting will be hybrid.
