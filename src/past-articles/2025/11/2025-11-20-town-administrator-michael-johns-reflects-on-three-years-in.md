Three years ago, Town Administrator Michael Johns arrived in Boxborough with a firm belief in an “open door policy.” Three years later, he still stands by it. “Any resident, business owner, staff member, or board or committee member can call or stop by any time…it’s important to maintain communication.”

So how often does he get an unexpected knock at the door? “Every day” says Johns, “and I welcome that… I welcome any and all interaction. It does come with a price, and that’s ‘time’ – the weeks spill over into the weekend. But that’s okay… it comes back to passion… and the passion I have for this job.”

Johns lives in Wrentham; he commutes 50 minutes to Boxborough and 50 minutes back home each day. But Boxborough’s “community culture” drew him in.

Johns recalls watching all three nights of Boxborough’s 2022 Annual Town Meeting as part of his own due diligence while pursuing the Town Administrator position. He recalls being impressed by the level of engagement and passion of the residents who spoke on Town Meeting floor.

Boxborough is “welcoming and collaborative” said Johns. The Town Hall staff have collaborative relationships with the volunteer boards, and the boards have collaborative relationships with the many engaged residents. The Boxborough community “knows what it values and knows what it wants to be,” even if the community “sometimes disagrees on how to get there,” Johns observed.

Looking back over the past three years, Johns believes that one of the town’s biggest recent “wins” is also one of its biggest challenges going forward: employee retention.

When he arrived, Johns explains, morale was low and there was a “revolving door” in Town Hall and the police department. Since then, he says, the town has made a lot of progress towards getting wages and benefits up to competitive levels and hiring top talent.

Going forward, he says, the town needs to find “creative ways” to keep its employees, including by providing challenging work, professional development opportunities, and other “no-cost benefits,” like flexible working arrangements and a good workplace culture.

Another recent area of success, says Johns, is the town “making more progress toward building a new fire station in the past year than it has in the last 15 years.”

By this time next year, Johns would like to “make strides toward actually building a fire station” and finalize the establishment of “solid staff leadership,” especially with respect to the town’s finance professionals.

Johns also expects the issue of “smart economic development” to be a hot topic in the coming year. Johns notes that there is “division” among the residents on the topic of development – a division that is reflected in the current composition of the Planning Board. Johns says that Town Hall stands “ready to carry out the wishes of the community” but notes that the community will need to “find some middle ground” and come to a consensus on how to move forward.

When he’s not coming in early and staying late, Johns can be found doing “anything outdoors.” He regularly sails in Quincy Bay and is currently building a wooden sailboat in his barn. Johns also keeps bees, a hobby that he shares with quite a few Boxborough residents.

“I feel fortunate to be here in Boxborough,” says Johns, “for both the challenges and the celebrations.”
