In a joint meeting of the Boxborough Select Board and the Boxborough Planning Board on Monday, December 1, Town Planner Alec Wade and Associate Town Planner Ian Gilson presented on the Boxborough 2030 Master Plan progress and the Boxborough Climate Action Priority Plan, respectively.

Wade began by defining what a master plan is, giving a brief history of Boxborough’s Master Plan, and outlining the progress that has been achieved to date. A master plan, Wade said, “is the comprehensive roadmap that every community sets forth to become what it wishes to be…Boxborough is committed to being a rural and engaged community–that means maintaining the rural character that we always talk about, while continuing to provide a community that’s engaging for a diverse range of populations.”

Originally written in 2002 and updated in 2015 as “Boxborough2030”, the town’s vision for 2030, Wade explained that the Master Plan sets both aspirational and tangible goals for the community and proposes actions and timelines to implement those goals.

According to the 2026 Annual Progress Report, currently in development and scheduled to be published in early 2026, the success of a Master Plan can be measured by the number of actions completed and in progress. Wade explained that a total of 125 action items were developed in 2015, and he noted that 86% of the Plan has been “engaged,” meaning that goals have either been “completed” or are “in progress.” He then highlighted “2025 Areas of Success,” which included actions by the Economic Development Committee, the Sustainability Committee, and the Sargent Memorial Library.

Wade concluded his presentation by noting that in “2026, the Town will turn its attention to the next long range comprehensive plan. This plan will be a rewrite of the 2002 plan and 2015 revision.” Wade informed the Planning Board that a Long Range Comprehensive Planning Committee must be formed so that work can begin in 2027.

Planning Board Clerk Rich Guzzardi commented that the current Master Plan still has important actions that need to be fulfilled and said that he wants “to be careful that we’re focused” on closing out the critical pieces of Boxborough2030.

Select Board member Bob Stemple then asked if unfinished items from the Boxborough2030 Plan could be "rolled over." Wade replied, "yes, without a doubt," and explained that it is very common that communities roll action items from one plan to the next.

Residents can read the 2026 Annual Progress Report on the Town’s website. The public is encouraged to provide feedback by December 31, 2025: [https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/243/Master-Plan-Boxborough2030](https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/243/Master-Plan-Boxborough2030)

Associate Planner Ian Gilson spoke on the status of Boxborough’s Climate Action Plan (CAP), which has been in development for about a year and is expected to be completed in early 2026. Gilson acknowledged that “Boxborough has no formal document that guides (its) sustainability efforts as of yet” …and that the CAP is intended to guide the town’s future development.

Gilson presented the Boxborough Climate Action Priority Plan (B-CAPP), which was written by Tufts University Urban and Environmental Policy and Planning (UEP) masters students in partnership with the Boxborough Office of Land Use and Planning and the Boxborough Sustainability Committee. The B-CAPP aims to provide a “clear, locally grounded set of priorities and strategies that will inform the development of the town’s Climate Action Plan.”

Gilson ended his presentation by asking the Planning Board for their feedback. Chair Rebecca Verner thanked Gilson and expressed her excitement about the depth of the information presented. Member Mark White voiced concerns about potential costs of implementing some of the strategies and pointed out that it was a small sample of resident data (140) that is driving the plan. White suggested additional community engagement.

A copy of the Boxborough Climate Action Priority Plan is available on the Town’s website:

[https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/DocumentCenter/View/5453/Boxborough-Climate-Action-Priority-Plan](https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/DocumentCenter/View/5453/Boxborough-Climate-Action-Priority-Plan)

When asked to comment on the presentations, Town Administrator Mike Johns wrote, “As a community, we are fortunate to have so many people working very hard on the 2030 Master Plan, and equally as fortunate that we had a Tufts University (team)...working on such a great Climate Action Priority Plan, that is now advancing to the Town’s Climate Action Plan…I am very grateful that the community is in such good and capable professional hands as Director of Land Use and Permitting Alec Wade and Associate Planner Ian Gilson. It will be exciting to see how both of these living documents fold into the next master plan.”
