At the December 2 meeting of the Finance Committee (FinCom), Chair Tony Newton reported that the tax rate for Fiscal Year 2026 (FY26) is $15.39 per $1,000 assessed value, “slightly higher” than previously estimated in part due to actual property values and new growth coming in lower than expected.

During the 2024-2025 budgeting cycle, the Town Administrator, FinCom, and the finance team (Finance Director/Town Accountant, Assessor and Treasurer) conservatively estimated a townwide increase in new growth at $200,000 and an increase in the value of Boxborough properties at 5% over the prior year. Actual property values and new growth came in lower than expected; Newton said “new growth is declining faster than anticipated.”

According to data from the Town Assessors office as reported by Newton, the FY26 increase in property taxes for the average Boxborough home ($933,900, according to Newton) is $810. Last year’s increase for the average home was $896.

FinCom continued to discuss the Fiscal 2027 (FY27) budget. As previously reported, the town has projected that the FY27 budget will require an “operational override” of Proposition 2 ½ at Annual Town Meeting and at the ballot.

Proposition 2½, approved by Massachusetts voters in 1980, limits the amount of revenue a city or town may raise from local property taxes each year to fund municipal operations. This amount is known as the annual levy limit.

An “override” increases the amount of property tax revenue a community may raise and becomes part of the base for calculating future years' levy limits. The result is a permanent increase in taxing authority.

Given the uncertainty in the outcome of an override vote, FinCom plans to develop two separate operating budgets. Budget “A”, which requires an operational override, is a “level services” budget and would allow “up to” 5.5% overall growth, including the Acton Boxborough Regional School District (ABRSD) assessment.

Budget “B” would be a “level funded” budget with growth limited to less than 2%. Newton said there are “significant” increases in healthcare and pensions that the town has no control over and must be included in both the “A” and “B” budgets. Other expenses will have to be “substantially” reduced or adjusted to allow for those increases.

FinCom will take up the budget at its next meeting scheduled for December 16 at 7 p.m. The meeting is hybrid.
