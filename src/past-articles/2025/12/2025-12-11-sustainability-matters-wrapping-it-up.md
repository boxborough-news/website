‘Tis the season for wrapping and unwrapping gifts. Have you considered the environmental impact of all the wrapping materials we use during the holiday season? Bows, boxes, bags and wrapping paper contribute to a festive atmosphere in the short term, but in the long term can have a detrimental impact on the environment. The good news is we can make more environmentally sound choices about how we wrap gifts, and send less waste to the transfer station or trash hauler.

According to Earth911, of the 4-5 million pounds of gift wrap waste produced annually in the US, about half ends up in landfills. Wrapping paper that is shiny, sparkly or reflective likely has a thin plastic coating, making it unrecyclable. This is also true for decorative ribbons, bows and holiday cards laden with glitter. Putting unrecyclable paper in with your recycling can make the whole load unrecyclable. Before you toss wrapping paper in the recycling, try crushing it into a ball - if it stays crushed, it is very likely recyclable.

So, what to use as we get ready to wrap gifts? A good alternative is untreated wrapping paper - look for paper with a matte finish or wrapping paper that is actually labeled as recyclable. Better yet, is found paper - newspaper or brown paper bags that can be decorated for the occasion. And best of all is reusable wrapping material. Ribbons, bows, gift bags and boxes can be saved, stored, and reused from celebration to celebration. Reusable fabric wraps, fabric gift bags, and fabric scraps from sewing projects are great alternatives to paper wrap. This year I’m looking forward to using some fabric wrap that was recycled from saris.

*“Sustainability Matters” is written for Boxborough News by Ellen Hickey. Ellen is a Boxborough resident, enjoys the outdoors, and is interested in all things sustainable.*
