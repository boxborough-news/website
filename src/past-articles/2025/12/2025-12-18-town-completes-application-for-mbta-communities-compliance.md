On Wednesday, December 10, Boxborough Town Planner Alec Wade informed the Select Board, Planning Board, and 3A Advisory Group that the town has completed its application for MBTA Communities Compliance and has been placed into a status of “interim compliance.”

The town’s new MBTA Communities “overlay district,” which was approved by voters at Fall Town Meeting in October 2025, will now be reviewed by the state to verify that Boxborough has met the requirements of the law.

Passed by the legislature and signed by then-governor Charlie Baker in 2021, the MBTA Communities Act requires towns with access to MBTA amenities to change zoning regulations to allow multifamily housing by-right in a designated district or risk losing future grant monies. Boxborough’s deadline to comply with the law is December 31, 2025.

In the email informing the boards of this milestone, Wade wrote, “Thank you to the many of you who supported the work, participated in the process, and helped work toward compliance. Our [town’s] passage [of MBTA Communities zoning at Fall Town Meeting] with only 3 dissenting votes is a testament to the process and the level of engagement we achieved. I am not aware of another town that even comes close to our margin of approval…”

When reached for comment, Town Administrator Mike Johns shared the email that he sent to the boards just after he signed the application for compliance. “As you know, I live in one of the communities that failed the MBTA zoning at town meeting, then lost the legal battle fighting with the State. Before coming here, during my time serving on EDC, we were granted in excess of $3M in State grants; however, while fighting this issue, the community lost those grant opportunities and now is in the same position of required compliance. In my opinion, the transparent approach and highly effective public engagement process here, made a stark difference in the outcomes for the two communities…My hat’s off to all of you who made this a success!”
