At their final meeting of the year on Thursday, December 18, the ABRSD School Committee voted to remove two AB Forward reorganization options from consideration: Option 3v2 (close Merriam school, maintain five K-6 schools) and Option 6 (consolidate to three K-6 schools).

The Committee will now focus its study and deliberation on the two remaining options, as well as potential redistribution of students across the elementary schools.

Option 4 would “merge” Merriam and McCarthy-Towne schools to allow the Conant school to move into the Parker-Damon building after the current Conant building is closed. In this option, the district would maintain five K-6 schools. Superintendent Peter Light described Option 4 as a “targeted disruption” because the impact of the reorganization would be concentrated on a smaller number of students.

Option 5v2 would implement a “partial grade-band” model to create a “lower” school (grades K-3) and an “upper” school (grades 4-6) in both the Boardwalk and Parker-Damon buildings. Blanchard Memorial, the district’s only elementary school located in Boxborough, would remain a K-6 school. Superintendent Light described Option 5v2 as “systemic reorganization” because the impact of reorganization would be felt by a larger percentage of the student population.

However, the impact of Option 5v2 is also dependent on how students will be assigned to the reorganized schools. The district has developed three potential models for student redistribution: (1) re-assignment of current Conant students across the three buildings; (2) a “one-time complete re-lottery,” which would require all current K-4 or K-5 students to re-enroll through a lottery system; and (3) “geography-based” re-enrollment that would utilize a consultant to map out three school zones along with “flex zones.” In materials provided before the meeting, the district indicated that re-enrollment would include a “hometown priority,” but not the current “hometown guarantee.”

Although Blanchard would remain a K-6 school, two of the three potential redistribution models may result in current Blanchard students being moved to a different elementary school.

Deputy Superintendent Andrew Shen spoke about the benefits and drawbacks of the district’s longstanding “open enrollment” policy across elementary schools and asked the School Committee for direction on how to proceed with respect to enrollment. Committee members acknowledged that enrollment is a critical issue but did not provide the requested guidance at this time.

Superintendent Peter Light identified Option 5v2 as “overwhelmingly endorsed by the district's Senior Leadership Team as the most strategic, sustainable, and instructionally sound path forward.” Light explained that although neither option will “solve” the ongoing budget challenges, the consolidation of elementary schools in Option 5v2 would allow the district to use its limited resources more efficiently.

Before voting to whittle down the options, some School Committee members indicated that they would prefer to eliminate Option 4 along with Option 3v2 because both of the “targeted” approaches have led to community divisiveness. However, the committee decided that it was not prepared to eliminate both of the “targeted disruption” options and fully commit to the “systemic reorganization” pathway.

The final two options are all that remain from a list of thirteen possible district reorganization options developed by the district’s consultant DM Group in connection with the “AB Forward” strategic planning and reorganization initiative.

The School Committee is expected to continue its discussion of Option 4, Option 5v2, and enrollment policies in January. Its January 8 meeting will be reserved for public comment on AB Forward, and the Committee is expected to vote on a final reorganization plan at their meeting on January 22. AB Forward materials are available at [https://www.abschools.org/read-the-plan](https://www.abschools.org/read-the-plan).
