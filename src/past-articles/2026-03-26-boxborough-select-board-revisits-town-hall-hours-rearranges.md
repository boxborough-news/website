At their meeting on March 23, the Boxborough Select Board continued the ongoing discussion about Town Hall hours of operation, culminating in an announcement by the Town Administrator that Town Hall will no longer be open on Friday mornings, starting Friday March 27.

On January 9, the [Town Hall changed its hours of operation](https://www.boxboroughnews.org/archives/jan-jun-2026-news#h.24g6yksu74t8) to include Friday mornings from 8 a.m. to noon, in addition to Monday through Thursday, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. At their last meeting on March 3, Board members disagreed with each other about the pros and cons of expanded hours and some expressed concerns about the rollout process. The Board asked Johns to consult with town counsel before taking any further action.

At Monday’s meeting, Select Board Chair Kristin Hilberg began by apologizing for the way the new Friday hours were rolled out back in January, emphasizing that she “thought the sentiment of the Board was behind” expanding town hall hours and that “no harm was meant.”

Johns reported town counsel’s advice that town hall hours are “a management decision,” not a Board decision, and that the unionization of town employees does not prevent any changes to town hall hours. Johns also noted that the new union is not formed or recognized yet, and that the town is still weeks away from beginning union negotiations. Johns noted that as he “reassesses” the hours policies, he will be gathering information about “what the community needs and wants.”

The Board then turned to the 2026 Annual Town Meeting warrant and voted unanimously to rearrange the warrant articles so that the articles proposed by the Planning Board will be articles 7-12, not articles 44-49. The Board cited the historical trend of fewer voters attending the second and third nights of Town Meeting, when the Planning Board articles are typically taken up, as the reason for the change.

The Board also focused on the Planning Board’s proposed “Plastic Bag, Polystyrene and Disposable Plastic Reduction Bylaw,” a General Bylaw amendment. The Board first considered asking the Planning Board to remove this article from the warrant due to its potential impact on local business owners – one of which voiced opposition to the bylaw change during the Board’s public comment period earlier in the meeting. However, after reviewing the bylaw language more closely, particularly the noted exceptions, the Board unanimously recommended the article.

Before voting to recommend the articles put forth by the Personnel Board, the Select Board heard a presentation from Personnel Board Chair Allan MacLean and asked questions about the proposed changes to personnel policies.

In other business, the Board unanimously voted to authorize the Fire Station Building Committee to conduct the designer selection process and oversee the negotiation of a contract with the finalist, subject to final approval by the Select Board. Board members indicated that they expect to receive updates throughout the negotiation process.

The Board also briefly interviewed the two candidates for Associate Member of the Planning Board, Mark Barbadoro and Than Stuntz. The Board first voted 4-1 in favor of recommending Stuntz, with Wes Fowlks voting no, and then voted unanimously to “appoint” Stuntz to the position.
