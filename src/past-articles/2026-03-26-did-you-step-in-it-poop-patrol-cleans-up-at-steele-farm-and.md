Members of the Boxborough Minutemen Company and the Steele Farm Committee joined together on Saturday, March 21 to perform the unpleasant but much-appreciated job of cleaning up an abundance of dog feces from the trails and meadow areas of Steele Farm and Flerra Field.

Boxborough News reached out to Boxborough Minutemen Company Past Captain Tony Newton to learn how and why the clean-up effort was organized. Newton told us that this was the first year this effort was undertaken and was precipitated by his and his wife Pauline’s frequent visits to Steele Farm.

“Pauline and I realized the problem as frequent walkers at Steele (with our dog Benny) and thought this would be a good community project for the Minutemen. The Company agreed and supported it. Minutemen are a unique Boxborough institution and are quite visible for our ceremonial and historical activities. We are less known for the civic support we can provide, including organizing Fifers Day, clean up of Mass Ave, help with FreeBee Market, and others. We all enjoy being part of the small town experience.”

Minuteman and Steele Farm Committee member Ray Sanne worked with Newton to organize the clean-up logistics. The Steele Farm Committee purchased the “scoopers” for the job and will store them at Steele Farm for future use.

The volunteers jokingly referred to themselves as the “poop patrol,” making light of the nasty job, but dog feces has been identified as a human health risk.

According to the Massachusetts Department of Public Health, dog feces can contain parasites, bacteria, and viruses which cause significant diseases to humans and animals. The Massachusetts Department of Conservation and Recreation (DCR) notes the impact that dog waste has on water quality. “When pet waste is left on the ground, rain or snowmelt carries the bacteria into our surface water supplies…and…goes directly into our streams, rivers and lakes… Bacteria and other parasites, such as Giardia and Cryptosporidium found in pet waste can survive for long periods when left on the ground…During the next rainfall, these can get washed into the drinking water supply, compromising water quality.”

According to Newton, after completing their work at Steele Farm, the volunteers decided to “continue the fun” and scooped poop at Flerra Meadows, as well.
