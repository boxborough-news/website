This spring, the town will be undertaking a communications overhaul intended to improve the flow of information between the town and its residents by creating a centralized, cohesive communications system.

The project is led by a team of four town employees: Assistant Town Administrator Rajon Hudson, Director of Land Use and Permitting Alec Wade, Library Director Peishan Bartley, and Police Chief John Szewczyk.

This effort serves as their “Community Innovations Project” in connection with a “Leadership Matters” professional development course by the Public Sector Consortium. Between Fall 2025 and January 2026, the group attended four training sessions and will work with the Public Sector Consortium to implement the project by next fall.

The goal, said Hudson, is to “better coordinate town messaging in a unified and cohesive way” in order to “limit misinformation as much as we can.”

“For the town to run smoothly and to effectively address the needs of the community, we need empowered community members,” explained Bartley. “One important part of empowering community members is to make sure they are well informed so they have all the information needed to be engaged in town business. Everyone wants the best for the town, and every town employee wants to make sure we are providing the services that's needed and used.”

Added Wade, “it’s so easy for misinformation or miscommunications to grow… my objective is to centralize as best we can.”

The first phase of the project will consist of “fact-finding” to define the problem; in the second phase, the group will design and implement a solution.

In the first phase, beginning in the coming months, the team will collect feedback from stakeholders and residents about the different ways they receive information from the town. “We have RAVE, social media, the town website, listservs, and others,” said Hudson, “and we want to know what’s working and what’s not working.”

The team is examining information flow in the opposite direction, too. "It's not just about putting out information, but also acquiring feedback from residents as well,” added Chief Szewczyk.

In phase two, expected in late summer into fall, the team will design and implement a solution. They emphasized that they don’t yet know what the final product will look like because they need to gather data first. “We don’t have a vision yet for the final project,” explained Wade, “because first we need to diagnose how people want to receive communications and also where breakdowns in communication happen today.”

“We embarked on this project because we believe it will improve how the town operates,” said Bartley. “I believe in the necessity of communication, and the power of having information. I hope we will be able to set something up so that community members feel empowered and informed to actively engage in town activities.”
