Before sunrise on Patriots’ Day, the quiet of Middle Road will be broken by the steady tap of drumsticks, the boom of a cannon, and the voices of residents honoring a moment that helped launch a nation.

On Monday, April 20, the Boxborough Minutemen Company will gather at 6:30 a.m. outside the Boxborough Museum at 575 Middle Road to commemorate the events of April 19, 1775, when local militia members from what later became Boxborough answered the alarm and marched toward Concord. The annual ceremony, steeped in tradition, connects today’s residents with those who stood ready more than 250 years ago.

As the sun rises over Boxborough’s Old Town Center Historic District, members of the company will begin their observance with one of their most distinctive traditions: a drumhead election. In this historic practice, members confirm company officers by tapping their approval on a bass drum, a method once used by Revolutionary-era militia. Shortly afterward, the company cannon will fire, signaling that the Minutemen are ready to meet the enemy, just as their predecessors did in 1775.

From there, the group will march a short distance to Boxborough North Cemetery, where a commemorative musket volley will be fired. The names of Boxborough men who joined the fight on April 19, 1775, will be read aloud, ensuring their contributions are remembered.

Following the ceremony, the company will board a bus to Concord at 7:30 a.m., where they will march in the anniversary parade, continuing a tradition that echoes the original journey local militia took more than two centuries ago.

“This year will be our 59th Patriot's Day celebration,” said past Minutemen Captain Chad Childers. “I love it when we will fire the cannon and our little traditions like the drumhead officers’ election. We will then march up to North Cemetery where our captain, Rich Grady, will talk about our new Luther Blanchard birthplace memorial, read off the names of all 21 who marched with Littleton, Stow, Acton, Westford, and Harvard, and fire off a musket salute to them before we march back to the museum to board the bus to Concord for their parade. My favorite thing about our group is the camaraderie, but our purpose is to perpetuate the memory of the 19th, promote an interest in history for the youth of Boxborough, and give back to the community.”

While the uniforms, muskets, and ceremony reflect history, organizers emphasize that the Boxborough Minutemen Company is more than a reenactment group. The organization serves as a community service group, participating in local celebrations and educational events throughout the year. Membership is open to anyone 18 or older, regardless of gender or town of residence. Participants do not need to march or portray Revolutionary War soldiers to join.

The Boxborough ceremony is one of many Patriots’ Day events throughout the region. On Sunday, April 19th, the Boxborough Minutemen will participate in the Isaac Davis Trail March in Acton at 5:30 a.m. in honor of Luther Blanchard, Davis' Company Fifer. Later that day, at 7 p.m., the Boxborough Minutemen will also participate in a ceremony at Liberty Square in Littleton.

Weekend events in and around Concord also include living history demonstrations, immersive battle walks, artillery demonstrations, and historic house tours at locations throughout Minute Man National Historical Park.

For Boxborough residents, however, the day begins quietly: in the early morning light, with the sound of a drum and the echo of history. As the Minutemen gather once again outside the Boxborough Museum, they continue a tradition that bridges centuries, reminding the community that Patriots’ Day is not only about remembering history but also about keeping it alive.
