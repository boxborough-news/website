At their meeting on April 13, the Boxborough Select Board held a “preliminary” discussion about the town’s fee structure and possible increases to various fees, including the cost of transfer station stickers. The Board made no decisions, but asked Town Administrator Mike Johns to bring back a proposal for potential fee increases.

The discussion focused on the Boxborough Transfer Station sticker fee. Department of Public Works (DPW) Director Ed Kukkula explained that the Transfer Station is “constantly in the red and every year is going up by 10, 15, 20 thousand dollars in the red.” He noted that roughly 1000 households have a transfer station sticker, but only half these households pay the $150 fee for the sticker; the other half are provided stickers at no cost per the current town policy of charging no fee to residents over 65 years of age.

Kukkula suggested that “we’ve got to start with at least raising our sticker fees a little bit and maybe charging our seniors a small fee too.” Town Administrator Johns noted that there may be other options too, like charging for “extras” (like mattresses and appliances).

Select Board Chair Kristin Hilberg stated that she is not currently inclined to start charging seniors for a transfer station sticker. Select Board member Sam Anderson stated that he would like to keep that option “on the table” and explore charging a discounted fee for seniors.

Johns agreed to bring back a proposal for fee increases not just for Transfer Station fees but also for Recreation Commission program fees, inspector fees, and other areas where Boxborough’s fee structure may be “behind” comparable communities.

“The intent is not to make a profit,” Johns clarified, “but we should at least cover the cost of the service we’re delivering.”

The Board also discussed a proposal by Select Board member Wes Fowlks to rename the Boxborough Transfer Station in memory of former DPW employee Robert "Bo" Sokolowski for his many years of service to the town.

Members voiced appreciation for Bo’s service to the town, but several expressed hesitation about choosing one person among the many who have served the town. Kukkula suggested that the town consider a plaque commemorating multiple individuals.

The Board agreed to put the proposal on the agenda for a future meeting and invite public feedback.
