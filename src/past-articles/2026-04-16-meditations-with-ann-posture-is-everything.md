Every once in a while, something happens in life to remind us how important our daily posture truly is. It can be something acute that has been building for years of bad posture and suddenly you feel it strongly. It can be something that has slow, intermittent pain patterns that keep visiting us periodically until we pay attention.

But regardless of the feedback, the doctors and the physical therapy technicians give us exercises to help us correct a lifetime of imbalances, and we get frustrated with how slow the progress is. We forget that it has taken us years of unbalanced movement patterns to get where we are today.

What if I told you that you can begin to make a difference in your future body right now. Small and consistent changes can make a big difference in our perception of pain as well as change the biomechanics which cause higher rates of wear and tear. It is never too late.

Two concepts that I talk a lot about in posture is the idea of Doctors Office Tall. Everyone knows exactly what I am talking about. We go to the doctor and get our heights measured and we stand up as straight as we possibly can in hopes of coming out taller than we are normally. That concept should haunt you as frequently throughout the day as possible.

Each time you think of it, you should use all your available muscles that make you taller whether you are sitting, walking or standing. Keep repeating Doctors Office Tall and follow how the body immediately kicks in and gets taller.

The other concept is what I call Sensitive Feet. It means actively spreading our toes and pressing the foot evenly into the floor. My yoga teacher spoke of a foot being like a car. There are four pressure points (tires) that need to be used consistently for even use.

This develops our arches, ankles, knees and hips evenly so those areas are stronger with balanced muscular strength and flexibility. We have less pain and discomfort going forward as we age. Again, this awareness can creep into our day as we sit, stand or walk.

Once you have the feeling of these two awarenesses, combine them for an even more positive impact. You will feel the action from the ground to the top of your head and everywhere in between.

Repeat these actions as frequently as you remember them and hold for as long as you can focus. Sometimes that will be fleeting, maybe it will be a couple of minutes of consistent awareness, but regardless of the time, your body will thank you.
