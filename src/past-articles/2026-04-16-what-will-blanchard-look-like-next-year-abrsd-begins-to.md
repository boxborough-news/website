The Acton-Boxborough Regional School District has begun the process of implementing the AB Forward elementary reorganization for the 2026-2027 school year, preparing to move students and teachers to and from Blanchard Memorial School, the district’s only elementary school located in Boxborough.

The AB Forward strategic planning process began in Spring 2025 in response to the district’s ongoing budget challenges. In January 2026, the School Committee voted to close the Conant School, one of the six elementary schools in the district, and establish five reorganized elementary schools: Blanchard Memorial School (K-6), Boardwalk Campus Lower (K-3) and Upper (4-6) and Parker Damon Building Lower (K-3) and Upper (4-6). At that meeting, the School Committee also voted to end the district’s longstanding “open enrollment” policy effective June 30, 2026 and implement geography-based enrollment. On April 9, the School Committee voted on a new geography-based enrollment system to replace the district’s prior “open enrollment” system.

For Boxborough residents, the new baseline enrollment policy is straightforward: all of Boxborough is zoned to attend Blanchard. This means that all current and future elementary students in Boxborough (except for those who may be in specialized programs located at other campuses) have a “primary enrollment” to Blanchard.

However, there is an exception to this general rule. The School Committee also voted to implement a “flex zone” in the eastern part of Boxborough. This area, which includes the Reed Farm Road, Sargent Road, and Littlefield Road neighborhoods, as well as parts of the Flagg Hill Road neighborhood, has a primary assignment to Blanchard but a secondary assignment to the Boardwalk Campus.

The district’s new flex zone policy voted on April 9 states that the district has the discretion to “flex” students in the four flex zones (one in Boxborough and three in Acton) to their secondary assignment in order to manage class sizes across the schools. The district’s flex zone policy does not apply to currently enrolled students but it will apply to all future students starting July 1, 2026.

At a family webinar on Tuesday, April 14, Superintendent Peter Light assured Boxborough families that “we are not activating that flex zone this year; we do not need to nor do we foresee that we’re going to need to do that in the near future. It is really there as a future-looking flex zone….”

Similarly, at the April 13 Boxborough Select Board meeting, School Committee member Adam Klein explained to the Select Board that the Boxborough flex zone is designed for future needs; “if there were an influx of elementary age students in Boxborough and Blanchard was overenrolled with just Boxborough families there needs to be a relief valve…where we can balance that so we don’t sit overenrolled gigantic class sizes in Blanchard….”

Boxborough families continue to voice concerns about the flex zone and other changes to Blanchard, including larger class sizes and teachers being moved from Blanchard to the other schools.

In early April, the district released an “Elementary Staff Placement for 2026-2027” document, which showed that at least one current teacher at each Blanchard grade level will be moved to a different elementary school (in addition to movement between the other schools).

Many Blanchard community members expressed surprise and disappointment at the announcement, leading Boxborough Select Board members to ask School Committee member Adam Klein about these changes at their most recent Select Board meeting.

“It made sense to find a way to more evenly disperse the knowledge that our educators have and the methodologies they have… the best way to have that is to distribute that among the district,” responded Klein, noting a general community consensus that the different teaching methodologies of the old schools be incorporated into all the new schools.

“I think it’s really important that all of these new schools, and Blanchard, get the opportunity to step back and have a new group of people decide and ideate what they want their community to be,” Klein continued. “I think it would be unfair if Blanchard got to stay very much as it is now while all these new schools were created… I’m really excited to see how these new communities come together and how they create their key values and mission and identities…”

The next AB Forward implementation steps include student school placement in late April and a “peer support” survey for families that will be incorporated into the upcoming class placement process.

For more information about the AB Forward process and implementation, visit [https://www.abschools.org/read-the-plan](https://www.abschools.org/read-the-plan).
