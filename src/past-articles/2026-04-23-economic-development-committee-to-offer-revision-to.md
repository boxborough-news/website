At their meeting on April 9, the Boxborough Economic Development Committee (EDC) discussed Article 7 of the 2026 Annual Town Meeting Warrant, a “plastic bag, polystyrene and disposable plastic reduction” amendment to Boxborough’s General Bylaw.

After several members of the EDC agreed that the language in Article 7 was “too restrictive,” the EDC agreed to propose a revision to Article 7 that would limit the scope to only plastic bags.

According to the current text of the proposed amendment, “The purpose of this bylaw is to regulate the retail use of single-use plastic checkout bags, polystyrene, and disposable plastics, promoting the use of reusable and/or recyclable alternatives.”

Town Planner Alec Wade explained that, at the request of the EDC, he and Boxborough’s Building Commissioner worked on a “test case” with Boxborough business Two Friends Chocolates, which uses two types of packaging that contain polystyrene.

According to Wade, the Building Commissioner “is not sure that these would qualify for an exemption” because alternative packaging materials exist. Wade continued that the Building Commissioner was also asked whether Two Friends Chocolates might be eligible for an “outright exemption as a business” due to the nature of the business “in the high-end chocolate market.” According to Wade, the Building Commissioner said that “that is not an exemption that he would be able to grant because that would be showing favoritism to a specific business.”

Lala Selvaraj, EDC member and owner of Two Friends Chocolates, told the Board that the ban as written would prevent her from using “economical” packaging that she currently uses for her products and that alternatives would be much more expensive – and those costs would need to be passed to the customer. “We won’t be able to sustain in the market,” she said.

Boxborough Planning Board Chair Rebecca Verner, present at the EDC meeting, commented that the Town of Acton has had this polystyrene ban bylaw in place for four years and the majority of Massachusetts towns “have already enacted this kind of policy… so we’re way behind the times.” She also stated that there are “tons of health benefits and environmental benefits” of a polystyrene ban. The Planning Board is the sponsor of this Article.

Selvaraj noted that Acton first banned only plastic bags and then phased in a full polystyrene ban two years later.

EDC Chair Abby Reip suggested that the EDC propose a revision to Article 7 that would ban plastic bags but not ban polystyrene more broadly. EDC members agreed that it would be more effective to bring the proposed revision directly to the Planning Board in advance of Town Meeting instead of trying to make the change on Town Meeting floor.

Verner commented, “we’d be happy to have you at the Planning Board” and asked Wade to add the EDC’s proposal to a future Planning Board agenda.

When reached for comment after the meeting, Reip stated, “The EDC recognizes the negative impact that plastic and polystyrene have on health and the environment and are in favor of banning those items. Article 7 will impact several businesses in Boxborough and, as currently written, implements a 6 month time frame for compliance. It is our understanding that very few businesses are aware of the proposed bylaw, and we (the EDC) would like to see an extended timeline so that businesses have time to plan and can comply with the ban.”

The EDC proposal is expected to be on the agenda for the Planning Board’s April 29 meeting.
