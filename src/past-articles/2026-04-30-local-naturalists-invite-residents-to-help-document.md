This week, across backyards and trails in Boxborough and Acton, aspiring naturalists have been busy taking photos of birds, plants, insects, and anything wild they come across.

Why? The observations are being uploaded and compiled for the “Nature of Acton and Boxborough 2026” project on iNaturalist, an online community used to document and identify living things.

The project began April 24 and runs through May 4. Organizers are hoping more Boxborough residents will join in before it wraps up.

Boxborough residents Karla and Reeves Briggs told Boxborough News that this is their third year participating in the project and that they heard about it through the Boxborough Conservation Trust, which is one of the organizations collaborating on the project.

Anyone can participate. After creating a free account on [iNaturalist.org](http://inaturalist.org/) or the iNaturalist app, users join the project by searching for “Nature of Acton and Boxborough 2026.” To record an observation, users take a photo of “a plant, bird, insect, mushroom, anything wild — and the app will suggest possible species,” Reeves told Boxborough News.

The Briggs have submitted “dozens of observations” so far. Karla Briggs told Boxborough News that the project is not only a “fun way to get people outdoors enjoying nature,” but is also a way for “anyone to get involved with science. Contributing images helps build a better understanding of the species that live here in Boxborough with us…It can be very rewarding to look more closely at the world around you. This project makes it easy to learn new things, whether you contribute observations or just look through the observation list.”

When asked why this project is important, organizer and Acton resident Rob Gogan told Boxborough News, “Our main reason is to celebrate the wonder and diversity of wildlife in our towns. We hope that if people learn more about our many wild neighbors, maybe they will be more considerate stewards of the land we share with them. Also, though most of us are amateurs, we are contributing to data related to local ecosystems. Environmental scientists use iNaturalist reports to study individual species. In this era of climate change, we need to keep track of its impacts on nearby wildlife.”

According to Gogan, in just the first few days of the project, “participants logged hundreds of species. Among the highlights: a fisher, a bobcat, a mourning cloak butterfly, and delicate spring wildflowers like trillium and trout lily…In Boxborough, observers documented a ring-necked snake and even captured an unusual moment — a brown-headed cowbird egg laid in a phoebe’s nest, a real-life example of brood parasitism.”

“As of Day 5, we have 260 species submitted by 17 observers. If we are going to meet our goal of 1,000, we will need a lot more nature watchers,” wrote Gogan. Whether you submit dozens of sightings or just one, each observation adds to a growing community snapshot of local wildlife.
