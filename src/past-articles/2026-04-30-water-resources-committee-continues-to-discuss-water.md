At its meeting on April 21, the Boxborough Water Resources Committee (WRC) discussed how to engage Boxborough residents with the assessment and maintenance of Boxborough’s water quality through community outreach at Annual Town Meeting and at Fifer’s Day.

The WRC decided to focus its Fifer’s Day outreach on encouraging residents to test their water and to support a Town-sponsored well-sampling pilot program.

The Comprehensive Water Resources Report by consultant Weston & Sampson (W&S), completed in November 2025, concluded that the groundwater in Boxborough recharges at a rate that is higher than the water demand, such that the town’s water supply appears adequate for the future. However, the report only addressed water quantity, not water quality.

While the Board of Health requires water testing upon property transfer, not many property transfers occur annually. Board members acknowledged that the cost of water testing may be burdensome to many residents, and the Town lacks the funding for any large-scale water testing program.

Member Bryan Lynch explained that a water test that includes the measurement of “forever chemicals” can cost around $700. In addition, the water sample must be carefully handled to avoid contamination.

In other business, the WRC considered possible topics for a follow-on program to its water study. These include the determination of the capacity of the Hager well; the identification of any regions in which the water quality is insufficient; and the availability of the Town’s firefighting resources.

The Littleton-Boxborough waterline continues to progress. The hydrants are fully operational, and the procedures to connect to the housing units are being established. The WRC also discussed the clarification of regulations that cover well recharge areas.

The next WRC meeting will be on Tuesday May 26, at 7:30 p.m., via Zoom.
