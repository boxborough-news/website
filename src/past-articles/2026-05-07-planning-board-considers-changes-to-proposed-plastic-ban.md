During its meeting on Wednesday, April 29, the Boxborough Planning Board engaged in a discussion with Economic Development Committee (EDC) members regarding the proposed “Plastic Bag, Polystyrene and Disposable Plastic Reduction” general bylaw, which is Article 7 on the Annual Town Meeting Warrant.

As currently written, the bylaw bans plastic utensils, plastic bags, and other polystyrene-based plastics. If passed by Town Meeting, the regulations would take effect six months following approval from the Massachusetts Attorney General.

EDC members asked the Planning Board to consider a phased approach rather than applying a six-month enforcement deadline for all of the prohibited items. Specifically, the EDC proposed retaining the six-month timeframe for plastic bags and disposable plastic utensils but allow between two and three years for businesses to comply with the other prohibited items.

Most Planning Board members were receptive to the phased approach, but wanted shorter timelines. They agreed to consider prohibition in two phases: first phase beginning January 1, 2027 and second phase beginning January 1, 2028.

EDC Chair Abby Reip noted that the Planning Board has required extensive business outreach for previously proposed bylaw changes and asked whether affected businesses had been notified of this proposed bylaw and what feedback had been received. Town Planner Alec Wade explained that a survey had been sent to six businesses, but only two responded. He also indicated that there were two additional businesses that would be affected by the bylaw that had inadvertently been left off of the contact list.

EDC member and owner of Two Friends Chocolate, Lavanya (Lala) Selvaraj spoke during the meeting and provided an example of how her business would be impacted if the proposed bylaw were to pass as currently written. Selvaraj had volunteered to serve as a “test case” for the proposed bylaw and submitted several packaging items for review by Boxborough Building Commissioner/Code Enforcement Officer Ed Cataldo. Cataldo found that one item contained polystyrene, and although the bylaw allows for exemptions, Cataldo indicated that he would not grant such an exemption in this case.

Planning Board members asked Wade to work with Cataldo to explore changes to Section 3 (regulations) and Section 4 (exemptions) before their next meeting, which is Monday, May 11 at 7 p.m. via Zoom.
