On Thursday, May 7, the Acton-Boxborough Regional School Committee held its first meeting since its April 9 vote on the district’s new elementary school enrollment system.

It was also the Committee’s first meeting since Acton’s April 28 town election. The voters of Acton elected incumbent and current Chair Tori Campbell and new members Dennis Burianek and Jacob Fay.

The Committee first heard a presentation from A-B Regional High School Principal Joanie Dean about the High School’s continued accreditation by the New England Association of Schools and Colleges (NEASC) and the multi-year process to receive accreditation.

The Committee also did a “first read” of proposed revisions to three district policies. The first policy was “Student Absences and Excuses,” the district’s attendance policy. The proposed revisions reflect the work of the district’s “Chronic Absenteeism Task Force” and update a formerly “barebones” document to a more detailed policy aimed at improving communication and collaboration between the district and families.

The second was the “Enrollment of Students” policy, revised to reflect the transition to geographic enrollment that the School Committee voted at its April 9 meeting. The Committee also reviewed the accompanying procedure document, “Elementary School Placement & Flex Zone Management Procedures.” Committee members offered several suggestions intended to clarify how the flex zones will operate in specific scenarios.

At their April 9 meeting, the Committee voted on the district’s new system for elementary school enrollment. The new system includes three geographic zones corresponding to the three elementary campuses; “flex zones” for balancing enrollment in future years; and “transition policies” for current students. The “flex zones” are four designated areas (one in Boxborough and three in Acton) that have a second alternate school assignment in addition to their primary geographic assignment. If the district needs to balance enrollment in future years, they can assign incoming students (and siblings) living in flex zones to the alternate school instead of the primary geographic assignment. In their motion to approve the flex zones, the School Committee asked that the district bring back for approval before the end of the school year the administrative procedure that will be written to implement the flex zone policies.

The third revised policy reviewed by the Committee was the “Student Transportation Policy” and accompanying procedure, both updated to reflect the transition to geographic enrollment.

The draft policy documents are available to the public at [https://abschools.community.diligentoneplatform.com/Portal/MeetingInformation.aspx?Org=Cal&Id=218](https://abschools.community.diligentoneplatform.com/Portal/MeetingInformation.aspx?Org=Cal&Id=218).
