At its meeting on Monday, May 11, the Planning Board voted unanimously to amend Article 7 on the Annual Town Meeting (ATM) Warrant, the proposed “Plastic Bag, Polystyrene and Disposable Plastic Reduction” general bylaw, to prohibit only plastic bags. As written and printed in the warrant, the bylaw bans plastic utensils, plastic bags, and other polystyrene-based plastics.

During the Planning Board’s previous meeting on April 29, members of the Boxborough Economic Development Committee asked the Board to consider a phased timeline rather than applying a six-month enforcement deadline for all of the prohibited items, citing the lack of outreach to affected businesses and the need to provide more time for businesses to comply with the regulations. An amended bylaw with a phased timeline was included in the Planning Board’s May 11 meeting packet.

Prior to the board’s discussion of the amended plastic bylaw, Planning Board Chair Rebecca Verner recognized members of the public who were present and wished to speak. Ten individuals spoke, citing support for Boxborough’s small business community and urging the Planning Board to either “pass over” (i.e., withdraw) the article or amend it to prohibit only plastic bags. “We want to make sure that small businesses are heard…we don’t have too many businesses in town and we want to make sure they can thrive,” said the first speaker.

Planning Board member Chris Dowdy moved to amend the bylaw so that it only prohibited plastic bags; the motion was seconded, and a discussion ensued with Planning Board Chair Verner and member Cindy Markowitz voicing their opposition to the amended language. Planning Board Clerk Rich Guzzardi and members Mark White and Chris Dowdy acknowledged the public’s concern for small business and the desire to bring a bylaw to Town Meeting that would likely pass.

Defending the revision, Guzzardi said, “...We’ve brought (the bylaw) this far…I’d rather have something at Town Meeting rather than…pass over.” The Board then voted unanimously in favor of the edited version of the bylaw, which would ban the sale and distribution of single-use thin-film plastic checkout bags.

The board discussed other articles and their respective presentations for ATM. During the discussion of Article 9, a zoning bylaw amendment to expand the purview of the Design Review Board (DRB) to include the Business and Business-1 zoning districts, Guzzardi, the sponsor of the article, informed the board that he was leaning toward passing over the article, due, in part, to the Finance Committee’s unanimous vote not to recommend it.

Guzzardi told the board that he had reached out to Finance Committee (FinCom) Chair Tony Newton to address FinCom’s concerns, but came away with the understanding that FinCom members would not change their recommendation and were concerned about the impact to Boxborough businesses. Guzzzardi stated, “...as the sponsor of this, I do not want to go to Town Meeting where you’ve got two boards diametrically opposed on something that we need two-thirds majority on…I don’t believe we’ve done our homework on this.” Guzzardi’s motion to pass over the article passed in a split 3 - 2 vote, with opposition from Verner and Markowitz.

In a statement provided to Boxborough News, Markowitz wrote, “Residents of Boxborough have expressed a clear desire for a more attractive Route 111 corridor. Extending the Design Review process now to the Business District will improve the corridor’s appearance without creating an undue burden on businesses. ‘Architectural style’ is already part of the Planning Board’s Site Plan Review criteria, and guidance from the Design Review Board will strengthen that process.

The current Design Guidelines address important issues such as building massing and spacing that remain relevant today. Improving the visual quality of the Route 111 corridor sooner rather than later will also enhance nearby commercial property values and strengthen the town’s tax base. There is no need to delay this effort.”

Guzzardi also provided a statement to Boxborough News supporting his position to pass over. “While I support design standards in the Business and Business-1 districts, after the warrant was published it became apparent through feedback and further review that more work is needed.

That includes updating the Design Guideline to appropriately address commercial style buildings suitable for the allowed uses, developing clear and measurable criteria, and establishing a timely review process. There is no urgency that compels us to try to get something that is incomplete and not fully supported passed at this Town Meeting. Coordination with the DRB, land owners, businesses, and other boards and committees is needed, which can be done over the next few months with the potential of bringing this back for Fall Town Meeting.”
