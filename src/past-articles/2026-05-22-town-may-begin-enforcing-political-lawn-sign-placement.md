The town election is Tuesday, June 2, and many candidates are eager for voters’ support, placing their lawn signs along roadways throughout the town.

On Thursday, May 21, local candidates for office received an email from Boxborough Town Planner Alec Wade asking them to move their lawn signs six feet from the edge of the road.

“Signs within the public right of way are not allowed,” the email states. Candidates are asked to “move their signs a minimum of six feet from the edge of the road, along any and all streets where they are present.” The message also issued a deadline to comply, which is 8 a.m. on Tuesday, May 26. “When our offices reopen Tuesday morning,” Wade wrote, “our team will reevaluate the conditions and decide whether further staff intervention is necessary.” Wade also reminded candidates that they should not “move, alter, or destroy another candidate's sign.”

This was the second such notice sent to candidates alerting them that the Office of Land Use and Permitting had “received several reports of signs within the right-of-way that obstruct sight lines” and that “recently, (they) have received reports of tampering or destruction of signs. ”The first notice was sent on May 6 and did not include an enforcement deadline.

On May 11, the Town of Boxborough also published a “reminder” on social media that “Political lawn signs are permitted in Boxborough but must: Be placed on private property only… Not obstruct visibility at intersections or driveways… Not be located within the public right of way… Be removed within 2 days following the election.”

While the Boxborough Zoning Bylaw requires a permit for many types of signs, it specifies in Section 6.5.6(d) that “temporary non-illuminated political signs” do not require a permit. Under Section 6.5, "Prohibited Signs," the Zoning Bylaw states, "No sign shall be erected that creates a traffic hazard or obstructs sight lines..." According to the zoning bulletin posted at Town Hall and sent to candidates, “signs need to be far enough from the street to prevent obstruction of sight lines at driveways and street corners, and cannot interfere with the public right of way.”

On May 21, after the second notice was sent to candidates, Boxborough News spoke with Wade to obtain more information about the potential sign enforcement. Wade indicated that Building Commissioner Ed Cataldo has already “moved a few signs out of the right of way.”

Wade also explained why the town specified “six feet” from the roadway when asking candidates to move their signs. He emphasized that the town wants to enforce the sign rules uniformly. However, Wade acknowledged that the “right of way” measurement differs from road to road, and it is not always clear to residents where their property ends and the town’s begins. Accordingly, the “six feet from the roadway” language will be applied to all locations in town.
