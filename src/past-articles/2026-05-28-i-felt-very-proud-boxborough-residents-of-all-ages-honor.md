At 8 a.m. on Memorial Day, when the parade marchers began to assemble at Wetherbee Lane, the chance of rain was 90%, and surrounding towns had cancelled their parades.

But that didn’t stop the young members of the Blanchard Band and Boxborough’s Boy Scout Troop 1, Girl Scout Troop 84221, and Cub Scout Pack 26 from showing up with raincoats, umbrellas, banners, flags, and instruments protected with plastic bags, ready and willing to participate in Boxborough’s Memorial Day traditions no matter the weather.

The young marchers joined Boxborough’s veterans, the Boxborough Minutemen, Boxborough Select Board members, State elected officials, and the Boxborough Police and Fire departments to honor soldiers who died in service and all veterans buried in Boxborough cemeteries.

At Boxborough’s North Cemetery, parade participants and spectators heard remarks from Acton-Boxborough Veterans Services Director James MacRae and State Senator Jamie Eldridge. The Blanchard Band played the Star-Spangled Banner; Major Rick Barrett, Retired U.S. Army, read the names of the veterans buried at North Cemetery; the Minutemen fired a musket volley; and Taps & Echo were played by Blanchard Band members Greta Schmidt and Matthew Holzer.

After the parade turned and continued down Middle Road, the rain came down steadily. It was then that Boxborough’s Public Celebrations and Ceremonies Committee (PCCC) made the decision to end the parade at Town Hall instead of continuing to South Cemetery.

The very wet parade participants were ushered indoors to the Grange Room at Town Hall, and the ceremonies scheduled for Town Hall and South Cemetery were both held indoors at Town Hall.

Remarks were given by Pastor Fran Graveson of the Boxborough UCC, State Representative Dan Sena, and Select Board Member Kristin Hilberg. Major Barrett read the names of the veterans buried at Boxborough’s South Cemetery, and the Blanchard Band members played Taps & Echo again.

While the speaking continued indoors, the young members of Cub Scout Pack 26 and Girl Scout Troop 84221 filed back out into the rain to raise the American flag and the P.O.W. flag at Town Hall.

It was the first time either group had ever been asked to raise a flag, and both groups took the responsibility seriously – even meeting with Boxborough’s Cemetery Commission Chair Owen Neville for a training session ahead of time. They performed flawlessly, saluting the flag as they squinted against the raindrops.

“It was such an honor to be able to raise the flag during the parade. I felt very proud while doing it,” said Isla, an eight-year-old girl scout and Boxborough resident. “The parade was really fun even though it was raining; everyone was really impressive.”

Back inside, the solemn proceedings concluded, and the soaked kids were rewarded with a last-minute surprise: donuts and hot chocolate, compliments of the Boxborough Police Department.

“When I came to Boxborough, I remarked that the Boxborough Memorial Day parade represented the best of small town America,” said PCCC member and ceremony emcee John Fallon. “Forty plus years later, I feel the same way. It’s impressive to see the broad participation, even in the inclement weather, of so many people and especially so many children including the scouts and Blanchard Band.”
