by Nate Kim

Under the scorching sun on Friday, June 5, 2026, 413 seniors dressed in blue marched around the Leary Field Track to Elgar's "Pomp and Circumstance," some keeping perfect time with the music while others struggled to match the rhythm.

“I couldn't help but feel a bit sad,” said Henry Wang, a junior and National Honor Society Vice President who led the graduating seniors. "Knowing that I won't be able to see and greet all of my senior friends in the hallways hasn't gotten to me yet.”

Abhi Sanal, a graduated senior, had a different reaction. “Finally! I'm just so happy that it's all over.”

The ceremony opened with international greetings from students and the national anthem, performed by the senior vocal ensemble.

High School Principal Joanie Dean welcomed the graduates, friends, and families with her speech focused heavily on the sense of community and connection that characterized the Class of 2026.

The student speakers Solaina Chandra and Anna Li both addressed the duality of joy and uncertainty graduation brought. Both encouraged their classmates to embrace their authenticity against the anxiety of others' judgments as they enter the next chapter of their lives.

The ceremony spotlighted eight retiring faculty members including Janet Maxwell, Susan Ropiak, Rob Donaldson, Hye Kyung Dragone, Peter Cacciola, Karen Farrar, Michelle Hirth, and Andrea Koumjian.

Superintendent Peter Light presented the 2026 William L. Ryan Community Service Award to the Acton Food Pantry for their accomplishment in distributing more than 230,000 pounds of food since moving into their new building last year.

Following the award presentation, members of the senior band performed “Shining Star” by Earth, Wind & Fire.

The school then recognized Class Scholars, council officers, National Honor Society officers, and class leaders for their academic achievement and service to the community.

Following the recognition ceremonies, faculty members read the names of all 413 graduates as they crossed the stage to receive their diplomas. Superintendent Peter Light then formally certified that the graduating class had completed all of their requirements.

To close off the ceremony, retiring social studies teacher Rob Donaldson delivered the ceremony’s final remarks. He challenged the common saying that high school or college are “the best years of your life,” instead encouraging students to believe that their best years are always ahead of them.

With those final remarks, hundreds of blue caps soared into the cooled evening sunset above Leary Field. After four years of classes, competitions, performances, and friendships, the Acton-Boxborough class of 2026 officially became graduates.
