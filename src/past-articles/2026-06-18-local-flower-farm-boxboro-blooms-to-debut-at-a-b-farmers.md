At the end of Priest Lane, in a backyard next to a swing set, is a new “micro” flower farm, Boxboro Blooms.

“It’s small but it works hard,” Boxboro Blooms owner Stephanie says of her 1,000 square foot garden, where she’s growing more than 500 plants, including Sunflowers, Zinnias, and Dahlias along with less common varieties like Bells of Ireland, Snapdragons, and Yarrow.

Boxboro Blooms was founded last year and sells seasonal cut flowers in mixed bouquets, arrangements, and as individual flowers.

The flowers are sold primarily out of a self-service “flower shed” at the end of Priest Lane. The shed is open whenever there are flowers ready for sale and a QR code is displayed for payments.

On Sunday, June 21, Boxboro Blooms will make its debut at the Acton-Boxborough Farmers Market. Boxboro Blooms will be a vendor at the market every other week through October. The market is held every Sunday at the Elm Street Playground in Acton from 10 a.m. to 1 p.m.

On the weekends that Boxboro Blooms is not at the market (every other weekend starting June 27), the flower shed on Priest Lane will be open.

Although Stephanie has “always been a gardener,” this is the business’s first growing season and it’s her first time growing flowers at this scale. “It’s been quite the process and learning curve,” she says. She began preparing last fall, starting seeds in her house through the colder months. She also had a “pop-up” at Acton Coffee earlier this year, which generated interest in the new venture.

Stephanie is a former teacher and current mom of two young kids. With Boxboro Blooms, she wants to “build something that allows me to stay home with my kids and show them hard work, patience, and how to take care of things.” Her children are right alongside her as she works, she says, helping with the flowers and playing in the dirt.

Her goal is to offer her customers flowers that are local and unique - homegrown bouquets “that you won’t see at the grocery store.”
