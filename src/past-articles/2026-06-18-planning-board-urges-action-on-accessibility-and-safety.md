At their meeting on Monday, June 15, the Boxborough Planning Board met with Sue Carter of Places Associates, Inc., the Town’s engineering consulting firm, regarding ongoing safety and accessibility issues at the Enclave development on Stow Road.

At the request of Town Planner Alec Wade and Planning Board members, Carter has visited and inspected the development many times to ensure that the developer, Toll Brothers, has met the conditions set forth in the original site plan approval. Her most recent visit was in April of this year, and she issued a detailed report to the Planning Board in early May.

Carter identified numerous ADA (Americans with Disabilities Act) compliance and safety issues which have existed at the development for several years. Major concerns include excessive sidewalk slopes, inaccessible pedestrian routes, missing accessibility features, incomplete signage, and unresolved infrastructure issues.

Representatives from the Enclave Homeowners Association (HOA) were present at the meeting. They informed the board that representatives from Toll Brothers have met with them and reportedly acknowledged that significant corrective work is needed. While the HOA members expressed some satisfaction that the issues will be addressed, they also expressed disappointment that the substantial reconstruction of sidewalks and driveways will not be completed until Spring 2027.

Planning Board members expressed frustration with the prolonged delays. “Let’s get this moving…this is an over 55 community…(and) cannot have ADA non-compliance,” member Mark White said during the discussion. Board members strongly urged Town Planner Alec Wade to seek legal counsel’s opinion.

In other business, the Planning Board conducted their annual reorganization. Rich Guzzardi will replace Rebecca Verner as chair, and Chris Dowdy will replace Guzzardi as clerk.

Boxborough News reached out to Guzzardi, who provided the following statement: “I am happy to serve as Chair of the Planning Board and thank Rebecca Verner for her service in that role over the past year. The Board has a great deal of important work ahead of it, and success will require collaboration, discipline, and focus on our priorities. I appreciate the trust and confidence the board has placed in me, and I look forward to working together to move important issues forward for our community.”

The meeting opened with a presentation on the Specialized Stretch Code from Becky Caruso, the Clean Energy & Environment Legacy Transition (CELT) Municipal Fellow working with Boxborough's Sustainability Committee on outreach and education related to the Specialized Stretch Code.

Caruso explained that the Specialized Stretch Code is an enhanced building energy standard that would mandate stricter energy efficiency, renewable energy, and electric readiness for new construction. The Specialized Stretch Code, if adopted by the town, applies only to new construction; it does not require existing homes, additions, or renovations to be upgraded.

Noting that Boxborough already follows the Stretch Energy Code, Caruso told the board that the incentive for adopting the Specialized Stretch Code is that it could assist Boxborough in meeting state greenhouse gas reduction goals and make the town eligible for expanded state grant opportunities.
