On Thursday, June 25, the Boxborough Historical Society presented Jim Isenberg, age 96, with the Boxborough Cane, given to honor the town’s oldest living resident. Isenberg is the 12th recipient of the Boxborough Cane.

Isenberg grew up in Brighton, IL, a close-knit farming community of almost 700 residents where “everyone knew each other.” At the age of 10, he started taking on odd jobs, running errands for a nickel, mowing lawns, washing cars and carrying water to a local barber shop. He said he practically “lived on his bicycle” earning a Boy Scout merit badge in the process.

After high school, he went to Blackburn College and later to the University of Illinois, where he studied engineering, but decided to leave the university and enlist in the Navy during the Korean conflict. He was assigned to a ship in Norfolk, Virginia and traveled to Cuba, Greenland, Trinidad, Bermuda, and Venezuela.

Isenberg met his future wife Nancy at a U.S.O. dance at Newport News, VA. He moved to Massachusetts in 1955, married in 1956, and eventually made his way to Boxborough in 1968. He said Boxborough was a lot like the town where he grew up. It was “quiet and a good place to raise a family of four children.”

Boxborough was a little different back then. According to the 1970 census, Boxborough had 1,474 residents. Isenberg remembers more farm land back then, a volunteer fire department, and only one police officer in town, who according to Isenberg, drove a Cadillac.

Over the course of 37 years, Isenberg managed an engineering department at Raytheon, working on the Apollo Guidance Computer and Trident I and II.

Isenberg was active in the Unitarian Universalist Church in the Assabet Valley (formerly First Parish Church of Stow and Acton). He served as president of the church for 2 ½ years, the “clerk of the works” for construction of Fellowship Hall, the Finance Committee, the Buildings and Grounds committee, and generally pitched in wherever he was needed, even baking apple pies for the annual fund raiser.

When his wife Nancy passed away in 1992, Isenberg said his church gave him a “purpose in life,” and he found companionship with someone special, spending time traveling together.

These days, Isenberg spends time with his family including seven grandchildren and three great grandchildren.
