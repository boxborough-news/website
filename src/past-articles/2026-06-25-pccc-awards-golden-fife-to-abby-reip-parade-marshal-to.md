As the Fifer’s Day parade stepped off from the Blanchard Memorial School parking lot on Saturday morning, curious spectators waited for the big reveal: who would be in the first two cars?

This year, it was Abby Reip and Barbara Birt leading the parade. Reip received the Golden Fife award and Birt was named Parade Marshal.

The Golden Fife is awarded to a Boxborough resident who has demonstrated long-term volunteer service to the town. The Parade Marshal is someone who has done something exceptional for the town, but not necessarily long-term or as a volunteer, and does not have to be a resident.

When the parade reached Flerra Meadows and participants gathered for the annual Fifer’s Day ceremony, Public Celebrations and Ceremonies Committee Chair Jennette Kollmann invited Reip and Birt up to receive their awards. Below are Kollmann’s remarks:

“Abby Reip has served on a Boxborough board or committee for almost fifteen consecutive years. She has been a member of the Energy Committee, Recreation Commission, Building Committee, Community Preservation Committee, Planning Board, Personnel Board, and Economic Development Committee. She has also participated in special town projects like the Town Meeting Study Committee, the Complete Streets Committee, and the Village Center Working Group. Above all, Abby has been a force of connection, commitment, and community in our town.

Abby moved to Boxborough from Melrose in 2003. She quickly joined the Democratic Town Committee and met Francie Nolde, who invited her to join the Energy Committee (predecessor to the Sustainability Committee). She said yes and was appointed to her first town committee in 2011.

As she has served on so many town committees, she also volunteered for a long list of local organizations, including the Democratic Town Committee, AB Community Compass (formerly AB United Way), Household Goods, the Boxborough Garden Club, the Blanchard Band Parents Association, AB Friends of Music, and the Friends of the Boxborough Library.

Most recently, Abby founded Boxborough News, a volunteer-run independent news project, and continues to serve as co-editor for its weekly digital newsletter. She leads a team of volunteers who report on town government and community events to inform and engage Boxborough residents. Through this latest venture, Abby has tackled head-on a perpetual challenge facing small towns: how to share information with residents and encourage participation in local government.

Abby doesn’t hesitate to roll up her sleeves; she doesn’t shy away from taking on a leadership role; she welcomes the challenge of learning something new; and she is quick to make introductions and connections among the many, many people she knows around town. She takes pride in following in the footsteps of her father, who instilled in her the value of civic involvement and volunteerism by serving his community in Kingston, NY.

Abby continues to give countless hours and unfailing energy to our town, and we thank her for her contributions to Boxborough.

For the past decade, Barbara Birt has served as the editor of the Boxborough Council on Aging’s monthly Senior Buzz Newsletter. Almost every month, for ten years, she has put together a collection of news and events of interest to Boxborough’s seniors.

When she first took on the role of editor, she had no experience writing or editing (her degree from Middlebury College was in chemistry!). But, through her stewardship, the newsletter has expanded from four pages to eight pages. It is full of information about senior events and services, health and safety bulletins, and town announcements. The newsletter is sent to almost 1,000 Boxborough seniors.

Barbara moved to Boxborough from Brooklyn, NY in 1973. Her husband Dave was also active in town, serving on the Select Board, Personnel Board, and the COA Board. Barbara started volunteering in 2011, first joining the Friends of the Council on Aging. When Dave passed away in 2015, the COA asked Barbara if she would take his place on the Board. She agreed and has been a COA Board member ever since.

In addition to her decade of service on the COA Board and as the Editor of the Senior Buzz, Barbara also volunteers with the Friends of the Boxborough Library and Boxborough News. And when the town’s banner needed to be changed from “Selectmen” to “Select Board”, it was Barbara who sewed new letters for the banner.

In 2021, Barbara lost her home in the Harvard Ridge condo fire. After receiving support through the community’s fire relief efforts, she was soon volunteering with those who had helped her. As the fire relief project transformed into Boxborough’s FreeBee Market, providing gleaned food and other free resources on Saturdays throughout the summer months, Barbara became a regular FreeBee volunteer.

Most recently, in 2024, she joined the Boxborough Personnel Board – stepping up to serve the town when the Personnel Board was in dire need of new members.

Barbara is a behind-the-scenes mainstay in town, stepping up to help when help is needed. She continues to give her time and energy to our town, and we thank her for her contributions to Boxborough.”
