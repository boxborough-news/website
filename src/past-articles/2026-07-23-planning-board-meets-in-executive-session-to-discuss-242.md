At its meeting on Monday, July 20, the Planning Board met in executive session to “discuss strategy with respect to litigation” related to the “Appeal of Special Permit and Site Plan Review decisions, 242/244 Adams Place…”

The litigation dates back to 2023 when the Planning Board denied a Special Permit for a private common driveway and denied Site Plan Approval to construct a 140,000 square foot research and development facility at 244 Adams Place. The applicant, Boxborough Adams Realty Trust, filed their original application on May 23, 2022.

After opening the public hearing on the permits on July 18, 2022 and conducting ten subsequent (continued) hearings, the Planning Board voted unanimously to deny the permits on April 3, 2023. As part of the appeal process, the applicant applied for a “foundation only” building permit from the Town’s building commissioner, who denied the permit. The applicant then appealed the commissioner’s decision with the Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA). The ZBA affirmed the building commissioner’s decision, thereby upholding the denial of the permit.

The “Town of Boxborough Planning Board” and the “Town of Boxborough Zoning Board of Appeals” and the individual members of each board are named defendants in three linked complaints filed in Massachusetts Land Court in 2023 (May 2, October 24, and November 20). According to information obtained from the Trial Court of Massachusetts Land Court Department, “the parties came before the court on January 30, 2026 for oral argument…”

In a decision dated July 2, the court found in favor of the Plaintiff on three legal issues and ordered the parties to submit a status report on remaining issues within 30 days.

The Planning Board did not discuss the litigation in open session.

In open session, the Planning Board discussed potential zoning articles for the upcoming Fall Town Meeting, which begins Monday, November 16. As previously reported in Boxborough News ([https://www.boxboroughnews.org/news#h.9vmonat24wgu](https://www.boxboroughnews.org/news#h.9vmonat24wgu)), the MBTA Overlay District approved by Town Meeting in October 2025 failed to meet the state’s required net density of 15 units per acre. Therefore, the town must amend its bylaw to comply with the MBTA Communities Act. The Planning Board will conduct a public hearing on August 3, 2026 at 7:10 p.m. via Zoom to hear the public’s input on two possible options for correcting the bylaw.

Chair Rich Guzzardi asked Planning Board members if there were other zoning articles that should be considered for Fall Town Meeting. During a previous meeting, member Chris Dowdy requested that the board consider crafting a zoning bylaw prohibiting data centers in any district. Town Planner Alec Wade suggested that the board consider bringing a temporary moratorium on data centers to Fall Town Meeting. This would give the board time to write a well-researched and legally vetted bylaw to bring to a future town meeting.

The Planning Board also heard briefly from Fire Station Building Committee (FSBC) Chair Mary Brolin. Brolin thanked Town Planner Wade for meeting with the FSBC to set up the process and schedule for site plan approval for the new fire station, which will be sited at 984 Mass Ave. Municipal building projects are subject to the same permitting rules and regulations as privately owned projects. Wade informed the board that the FSBC expects to file their application “around October 19” and that public hearings would be scheduled for November 18, December 7, and December 21.

The board also discussed changing their meeting format from Zoom only to hybrid and starting at 7:30 p.m. instead of 7 p.m. Chair Guzzardi gave each member an opportunity to opine on the change, and all members supported it.

When reached for comment, Guzzardi wrote, “The Board has received a lot of feedback from community members on having an in-person presence for meetings and hearings. Based on the Board’s support, we will begin holding meetings in a hybrid format to provide an in-person experience with the flexibility of participating remotely based on members’ and participants’ personal schedules. I am hopeful this strikes a workable balance.”

The Planning Board’s first hybrid meeting is scheduled for August 17 at 7:30 p.m.
