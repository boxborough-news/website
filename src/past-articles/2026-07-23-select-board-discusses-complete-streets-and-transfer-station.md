At their meeting on July 13, the Boxborough Select Board heard a presentation by Town Planner Alec Wade about the town’s past, present, and future “Complete Streets” efforts. The Board did not vote on how to move forward, but asked Wade to propose a revised Complete Streets Policy for the Board’s future review.

According to the Massachusetts Department of Transportation, the “Complete Streets” program “provides Massachusetts municipalities with tools and financial support to address gaps in their local transportation networks. A ‘Complete Street’ is one that provides safe and accessible options for all travel modes—including walking, biking, transit, and vehicles—for people of all ages and abilities.”

Wade’s presentation focused on how to revive the Complete Streets program in Boxborough. He explained that the town’s current Complete Streets Policy is preventing the town from pursuing Complete Streets projects because it requires action by the Complete Streets Committee, which no longer exists. Wade noted that in revising the policy, the Board could choose to place responsibility for Complete Streets efforts with town staff instead of with a volunteer committee.

Wade’s presentation also included the possibility of a [Complete Streets project on Liberty Square Road](https://www.boxboroughnews.org/archives/jan-jun-2026-news#h.hlennpcls5pn). In April, a group of residents approached Wade to explore “increased safety and connectivity” along Liberty Square Road and adjacent neighborhoods. Board members agreed that Liberty Square Road is a good candidate for a Complete Streets project, but at least one Board member and public commenter mentioned that other roads should be considered as well.

The Board also discussed two matters related to the Boxborough Transfer Station. The first was the possibility of honoring certain transfer station employees with a plaque. The Board discussed potential criteria for adding names to such a plaque (possibly based on number of years of service) and whether there should be a plaque honoring employees at every municipal building. The Board took no votes on this matter.

The second was the possibility of the town charging residents a fee for the disposal of mattresses and appliances at the transfer station. Department of Public Works Director Ed Kukkula shared that he was in favor of charging fees for these items to cover the town’s cost to dispose of them, but also noted that a fee system may be operationally challenging. Kukkula and Town Administrator Mike Johns agreed to return to the Board with a proposal that would include both a list of fees and a plan for how the town would collect the fees.

Select Board Chair Bob Stemple updated the Board on the status of the town’s Diversity, Equity and Inclusion (DEI) Committee, which the Board discussed at its last meeting in connection with that committee’s role in the town’s creation of an Economic Development Plan. Stemple shared a revised “charge” for the DEI Committee, which Board members agreed to review for a future meeting. Stemple also noted that the DEI Committee is looking for new members.

The Board also approved [tree removal at the Old Town Common](https://www.boxboroughnews.org/news#h.l6xv7qsdhktk) and on Depot Road; authorized the town to scrap or sell the playground equipment that will be removed from Flerra Playground; approved a three-year municipal agreement with Littleton Community Television; and approved a series of budget transfers.
