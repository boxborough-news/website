Members of the Boxborough Planning Board and the Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA) have been apprised of recent changes to Massachusetts General Law (MGL) Chapter 40A, the state Zoning Act, effectuated by legislation signed by Governor Maura Healey on July 9, 2026.

On Tuesday, July 21, members of the ZBA met with Adam Costa, Special Counsel for Land Use Matters, to learn about the recent amendments to the Zoning Act (MGL c. 40). Attorney Costa summarized each of the amendments and answered questions from ZBA members that were specific to Boxborough.

Prior to the meeting, Attorney Costa’s firm, Mead, Talerman & Costa, LLC and the town’s General Counsel firm, KP Law, issued advisory memos highlighting the changes and the implications for municipalities. Attorney Costa’s memo described the changes as “meaningful and consequential” and noted that they are now in effect.

At the Planning Board meeting on Monday, August 3, Boxborough Town Planner Alec Wade shared a document identifying seven sections of the Boxborough Zoning Bylaw (ZBL) that are directly affected by the new state law and need to be amended. He offered “corrected” language for each of the bylaw sections for the board to consider.

One of the law’s significant changes impacts the variance standard. Currently, Boxborough’s ZBL categorically prohibits use variances, but under the new law, towns cannot prohibit use variances for residential uses.

Wade told the board that this does not mean that residential use variances must be granted, but that the ZBA must accept an application for a residential use variance. Wade provided an example in which a property owner with a buildable lot in the Office Park or Industrial Commercial zone, where residential use is prohibited, could apply for a use variance to build a single family home on the parcel.

Other substantial changes include extending the length of time allowed to begin building after a permit has been issued from 12 months to 24 months; allowing some changes to nonconforming lots or structures to be permitted by right; and increasing the length of time protecting the status of an abandoned nonconforming use or structure from 24 months (2 years) to “four consecutive years.”

When asked about the timeline for compliance and whether the board should move quickly on the bylaw changes, Wade replied that Boxborough is “not at risk of losing funding…the greatest risk is our inability to interpret the bylaw or respond to an application put forward to us in a manner that would be appropriate under Mass General Law.”

The Planning Board will meet with Attorney Costa on August 17 and will conduct a public hearing on August 31 to consider revisions to the Boxborough Zoning Bylaw in response to the changes in MGL Chapter 40A.

Relatedly, the State legislature is working on an “Economic Development Bill” that may also impact local zoning. According to a KP Law memo about the Chapter 40A amendments, the Economic Development Bill “contains several different zoning-related amendments, including ‘commercial conversion’ to multifamily housing by right, allowing land held by religious groups to be developed as multifamily housing by right, and codification of site plan review. These provisions are still in flux and have not been enacted into law.”
