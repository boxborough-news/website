At its meeting on Monday, August 3, the Planning Board conducted a public hearing to consider proposed Zoning Bylaw amendments to comply with the state MBTA Communities Act.

As previously reported in Boxborough News, the MBTA Overlay District approved by Town Meeting in October 2025 does not meet the state’s required net density of 15 units per acre. The state compliance spreadsheet used by the town’s technical consultant was found to have miscalculated the district’s compliance by overstating the unit per acre density.

At the Planning Board’s July 20 meeting, Town Planner Alec Wade presented two alternatives that would bring the Town’s MBTA Communities overlay district into compliance. “Alternative 1” would not change the land area already approved by Town Meeting; it would only change the bylaw’s written maximum density from 13 units per acre to 17 units per acre. “Alternative 2” would revise the district map to include an additional single-family lot at 1178 Hill Road and would then set the bylaw maximum density at 15 units per acre instead of 17.

Both alternatives were considered at Monday’s public hearing. A few members of the public gave input. Two Hill Road property owners each advocated for their parcels to be included in the overlay district, noting that without adding any land to the district, it was unlikely anything would be built since the district does not include any vacant land.

Planning Board members spent considerable time discussing which “Alternative” they would put forth to Fall Town Meeting on November 16. Member Mark White expressed his view that the “best route and easiest thing to get passed at Town Meeting is to increase density.” Member Rebecca Verner agreed. “Be cautious and careful,” she said. “Getting into compliance is the first step.”

Acknowledging the comments made by the public in favor of expanding the district, Planning Board Chair Rich Guzzardi said, “Nothing says that this is the end of the road on the MBTA Communities district…nothing prevents us from creating another housing overlay zone” in the future.

After closing the public hearing, all members of the board voted in favor of “Alternative 1” as the proposed Zoning Bylaw amendment.

Boxborough News reached out to Chair Guzzardi who offered the following statement: “The district was decided on after over a year of study, public forums, and hearings leading up to approval at Annual Town Meeting. This vote upholds that decision and, if approved at Fall Town Meeting, will get us into compliance.”

The Board also discussed other proposed zoning articles for Fall Town Meeting, with Planning Board Clerk Chris Dowdy advocating for a temporary moratorium prohibiting data centers in all zoning districts in town. Dowdy stated that a temporary moratorium will give the board time to write a well-researched and comprehensive zoning bylaw that can be brought to a future Town Meeting before the temporary moratorium expires.

Board members briefly discussed what defines a “data center,” with Planning Board Associate Member Than Stuntz calling for more research and cautioning against the possible exclusion of “data heavy” business types. The board will discuss the moratorium language at their next meeting on August 17 (hybrid format at 7:30 p.m.) and conduct a public hearing on the proposed temporary data center moratorium zoning bylaw during their meeting on August 31 (time to be determined).

Town Planner Wade gave a brief presentation on recent “sweeping changes” to Massachusetts General Law (MGL) Chapter 40A, the “zoning enabling act,” which he said “affects much of how this board operates…(and) how permits that are issued by this board (and) by the building department and other entities…function moving forward.”

The Chapter 40A changes were included in the state’s fiscal 2027 budget, which was signed into law by Governor Healey on July 9, and are effective immediately. Wade shared a document identifying the sections of the Boxborough Zoning Bylaw that are directly affected by the new state law and offered “corrected” language for each of the bylaw sections for the board to consider.

The Planning Board will conduct a public hearing on August 31 to consider revisions to the Boxborough Zoning Bylaw in response to the changes in MGL Chapter 40A.
