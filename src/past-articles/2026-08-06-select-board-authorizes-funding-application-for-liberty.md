At their meeting on July 27, the Boxborough Select Board amended the town’s Complete Streets Policy and authorized Town Planner Alec Wade to apply for design and engineering funds for a Complete Streets project on Liberty Square Road. According to the Massachusetts Department of Transportation, a ‘Complete Street’ is one that provides safe and accessible options for all travel modes—including walking, biking, transit, and vehicles—for people of all ages and abilities.”

Wade first presented to the Board the amended Complete Streets Policy. The amendments place the administrative responsibility for the Complete Streets program on the Town Planner (instead of on a volunteer committee) and state that the Town Planner will convene a Complete Streets Advisory Committee to “provide input, review, and make recommendations.”

The amendments also require a public hearing (unless waived by the Select Board) and Select Board authorization before the Town Planner submits any funding application for a Complete Streets project.

After the Board voted unanimously to amend the policy, Wade asked the Board for authorization to apply for “design and engineering funds for the entirety of Liberty Square Road, end to end, specifically to make Liberty Square Road a more complete street.” He added, “I don’t have exact details on what that will look like; that’s why we’re doing design and engineering.”

Wade explained that the application would be submitted through the Massachusetts Department of Transportation’s (MassDOT) “fully integrated application,” which evaluates eligibility for multiple funding programs, including the “Complete Streets” program. The application is due August 7.

Typically, this application would require a public hearing before the Board could authorize the application. However, the amended Complete Streets Policy allows the Board to waive the public hearing requirement when seeking design and engineering funding (as opposed to construction funding). Accordingly, Wade’s request to the Select Board for authorization also included a request to waive the hearing requirement.

Board member Priya Sundaram asked whether any other streets besides Liberty Square Road were taken into consideration for a Complete Streets project. Wade responded, “I certainly could consider other neighborhoods…Swanson Road, Stow Road, Burroughs Road, Hill Road, but I don’t know that I would receive the same neighborhood support that I have here, and I think this makes it an objectively more competitive application. So I feel confident saying to you that while yes there is more work to be done in exploring other neighborhoods and that’s absolutely on my list of priorities, the iron is hot, let’s strike now.”

In April, a group of residents on Liberty Square Road and adjacent neighborhoods approached Wade to explore “increased safety and connectivity” along Liberty Square Road. Wade told the Board that a survey conducted by the neighborhood group received more than 100 responses, the vast majority in favor of the project.

Wade emphasized the importance of this “community support” and that MassDOT looks favorably on projects that are driven by residents. Board member Sam Anderson added, “I would say if other communities are interested in a complete street they should organize and bring it to Alec just the way the Liberty Square folks did….”

Board member Kristin Hilberg asked whether the town could apply for funding for improvements to another street in town in the near future – before the potential Liberty Square Road project is completed. Wade responded that there are “multiple opportunities” for funding and indicated that the town could pursue projects on other streets simultaneously.

Members Hilberg and Anderson agreed that the Board should encourage the formation of neighborhood groups on other streets and “look for community leaders on Stow Road, Hill Road…” The Board voted unanimously to authorize the application for Liberty Square Road.

Wade indicated that he would expect to hear back from the state by December 2026 or January 2027. On Wednesday, August 5, Wade told Boxborough News that the application was “in progress” and he expected to submit the application the following day.
