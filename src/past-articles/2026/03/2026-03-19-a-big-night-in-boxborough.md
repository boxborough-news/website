On Monday, March 16, the conditions were perfect for a “Big Night” – a warm, rainy night just right for amphibians to emerge from their winter hiding and seek a vernal pool for the spring. Throughout Boxborough and surrounding towns, these lucky creatures were ushered across roadways by brigades of enthusiastic crossing guards armed with flashlights, reflector vests, and buckets, undeterred by the soggy conditions.

Rita Grossman and Ann Seymour are the coordinators for Boxborough’s “frog brigade.” Seymour first began volunteering with an amphibian crossing group from Acton, and Grossman joined her in 2021 when Depot Road was added as a crossing area.

“We pick them up and move them in the direction they are traveling – almost always towards the vernal pool, which is obvious by the cacophony of frog sound,” Seymour explained to Boxborough News in advance of this year’s Big Night.

This year, roughly 23 volunteers were stationed across Boxborough on Monday night – at Old Harvard Road, Littlefield Road, and two sites on Depot Road.

“We had heard about the salamander brigades last year and thought it would be super fun to join,” said first-time volunteer Caroline, who brought her two young daughters Gwen and Maren (pictured).

“We wanted to learn more about these beautiful amphibians and their pals and help them safely cross the street and it was an opportunity to do something totally new to all of us gals, together for the first time! With the added bonus of making new friends across age ranges and diverse backgrounds all with the love of nature as a commonality!”

Caroline’s daughter Gwen spotted the brigade’s very first creature of the night, “a teeny tiny spring peeper about two inches from her foot as we were walking.” And then her daughter Maren found the next. “I think their lower to the ground perspective was actually really beneficial!”

The girls were excited to find several salamanders as well. “What a treat to see creatures that live 90% plus of their lives underground out strutting their stuff and all knowing exactly where to go,” added Caroline.

Boxborough volunteers recorded a total of 201 live peepers (84 dead), 63 live wood frogs (11 dead), 2 live unidentified frogs (23 dead), 22 live spotted salamanders (5 dead), and 7 live red-backed salamanders (1 dead). Combined with the “brigades” operating in Acton and Littleton that same night, volunteers from the three towns saved more than 1,000 amphibians.

More information about the project can be found at [https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/791/Vernal-Pools-Amphibian-Brigade](https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/791/Vernal-Pools-Amphibian-Brigade).
