This Saturday, March 28, Boxborough members of the Bromfield Acton Boxborough (“BAB”) Rowing Team will be at the Boxborough Transfer Station to help fundraise for new rowing equipment and fleet maintenance.

Grace Connor, captain of the “Steady State Survivors” team, will be there along with eight of her teammates between 10 a.m. and 12 noon, selling baked goods and soliciting donations to support their fundraising efforts.

“Our two annual fundraisers are critical to our non-profit organization,” says BAB girls head varsity coach Holly Hatton. “Having the athletes directly involved gives them a sense of ownership for their equipment, which builds pride and diligence in caring for it on a day-to-day basis.” The team does not receive funding from the towns or school districts of the athletes.

This year, the team is also trying to set a world record for the length of time it takes to pull 100,000 meters on one erg. Erg is short for ergometer, a rowing machine that is used for “dry land” training when the team cannot yet get out on the water to row.

On April 12, at the Acton Recreation Center, the 8th grade team, the novice team, and the varsity team (totaling around 82 athletes) will combine and alternate pulling the erg for a short period in the U19 co-ed rowing team age category. For roughly five hours, rowers will take turns pulling on the erg for 30 seconds and then hopping off. During the switches from rower to rower, Hatton says, “our pit crew standing beside the erg will help with locking in the rower’s feet and keeping the wheel turning by pulling on the handle. Minimizing the transition time will be a key component!”

The team’s goal is to raise $15,000 as they attempt to top the co-ed U19 team world record of five hours, six minutes, 38.5 seconds set by Y Quad Cities Junior Rowing in Moline, Ilinois. in 2024, according to Concept2, the company that manufactures the ergs.

This season, Boxborough has five athletes on the BAB roster: seniors Patrick Tucker and Nix Klein; junior Grace Connor; ninth-grader Grant Weidman, and eighth-grader Chloe Mao.

“It’s been a blessing being on the team for so long and getting to see how the team changes from year to year,” said Tucker. “It’s great to see the team keeping some of its traditions with a twist. I think a lot of people will really enjoy the team-oriented aspect of this year’s Ergathon with us all rowing for the same goal. This is a great opportunity for the whole team to come together.”

Teamwork is also critical when eight rowers and a coxswain move a boat down a racecourse. “You all have to be engaged and have to work together to make the boat reach its maximum potential,” Weidman said. Weidman’s mom, Katie Davis, adds, “It’s a physically demanding and mentally challenging sport, and at the same time, it allows the kids to learn perseverance.”

The team’s first outdoor competition will be the Harvard Henley hosted on Harvard’s town beach on Sunday, April 19. Spectators are welcome.

Registration for the spring season will be open through March 31; students in ninth through 12th grades at ABRHS and the Bromfield School are encouraged to join at [barehillrowing.com/spring](http://barehillrowing.com/spring), and the team is happy to accept Ergathon donations at [barehillrowing.com/ergathon](http://barehillrowing.com/ergathon).

Registration for Summer Learn to Programs for ages sixth grade to adult opens on April 1. “A few Boxborough residents participated in this program last summer and loved it!” said Connor’s mother, Megan. “It is such a great way to get out on the water, have some fun and gain some tremendous exercise opportunities just up the road at the Bare Hill Pond in Harvard.”
