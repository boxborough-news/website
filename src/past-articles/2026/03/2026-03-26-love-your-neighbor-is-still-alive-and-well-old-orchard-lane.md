Earlier this month, a home at 29 Old Orchard Lane in Boxborough sustained significant fire, smoke, and water damage from a three-alarm fire on a Tuesday morning.

The Boxborough Fire Department was on the scene for approximately six hours, along with approximately 50 firefighters from surrounding communities. According to Boxborough Fire Chief John Kivlan, several factors made the fire challenging. “The fire was deep-seated, requiring extensive overhaul on the interior and exterior to reach burning materials. Additionally, the unseasonably warm weather increased physical stress on firefighters operating in protective gear, which contributed to the decision to strike a third alarm to rotate crews and maintain safe operations.”

The residents of the home, Gary and Kathie Schwarting, sustained no injuries. The cause of the fire has not yet been determined.

On March 24, Gary and Kathie Schwarting shared the following statement with Boxborough News:

“This is a letter of thanks to the many people who came to my wife, Kathie, and my aid on March 10 when our house caught on fire. We are especially grateful to the first responders. The first fire truck from the Boxborough fire department was at our house within just a few minutes of our 911 call. Their fast response prevented the fire from engulfing the entire house. This initial response was followed by an incredible number of additional fire personnel from Boxborough and surrounding towns. In addition, police officers and an EMT crew from Boxborough arrived to check out both Kathie and me, making sure we were not injured in any way. From then on until late in the afternoon many first responders from Boxborough and surrounding towns performed endless acts of kindness, from dashing into the house to retrieve prescription medications to searching for Kathie’s lost wedding ring after the fire was out. We are greatly thankful and indebted to each and every one of these heroic first responders for limiting the fire and for ensuring our wellbeing. The Boxborough Community Services Coordinator, Wendy Trinks, was also very helpful in getting us situated on short notice. We also want to express our extreme gratitude for the outpouring of kindness and love from our many friends and neighbors, who throughout the day, made sure we were sheltered and fed. We were overwhelmed by the offers to stay in peoples’ houses or to come for dinner, and more. Most importantly our friends and neighbors showed us that “Love your neighbor” is still alive and well. Boxborough is truly a wonderful community, and we will always remember this day as much for the outpouring of caring and generosity as for the actual fire.”
