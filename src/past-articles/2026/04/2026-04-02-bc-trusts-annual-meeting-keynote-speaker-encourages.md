Trevor Smith, an award-winning regenerative landscape designer and founder of ReEarth Solutions was the featured speaker at the Boxborough Conservation Trust’s 2026 Annual Meeting on March 31.

Smith’s talk focused on the importance of adding native plantings to home landscapes, emphasizing the need to address environmental changes by bringing our ecosystem back into equilibrium. He encouraged the BCTrust membership to see the beauty of native planting and to learn about how native plants work in harmony with the other residents of our landscapes from pollinators to birds to mammals.

When considering what types of native plants to include in your garden, Smith noted that Boxborough sits in Ecoregion 59, the Northeastern Coastal Zone. This Ecoregion crosses several planting zones, but what unites the region is the species of plants that are native and most beneficial to that area.

Massachusetts crosses several ecoregions, but plants that are native to Boxborough actually have more in common with parts of Maine and New York than they do with Cape Cod or the Berkshire area of Massachusetts. That, Smith explained, differentiates ecoregions from planting hardiness zones.

Smith discussed how native plants require much less mowing, weeding, and fertilizing than traditional lawns. Because they have deep, sturdy roots, they’re also naturally drought-resistant, while helping the environment. Smith also explained the differences between native plants, nativars, and cultivars and encouraged everyone to design a landscape they actually want to spend time in, even if that means mixing in a few non-native favorites with the essential native plants.

In his closing remarks, Smith emphasized the need for biodiversity to create climate resistant landscapes.
