On Saturday, May 2, the newly-minted Boxborough chapter of the Weed Warriors will hold its first “work party” to remove invasive plants from Patch Hill Conservation Land. The Boxborough’s Weed Warriors chapter is led by Boxborough’s Land Stewards Coordinator Dave Barnett. The Town of Boxborough, via the Conservation Commission, has recently joined the “Weed Warriors,” a free program managed by Sudbury Valley Trustees (SVT).

“By joining this program,” explained Barnett, “we now have access to the regional experience and expertise of the staff at SVT - as well as that of the volunteer weed warriors in other regional towns with established Weed Warrior chapters.” The goal of the Weed Warrior program is "to have a unified force of trained volunteers to tackle invasive species management throughout the Sudbury, Assabet and Concord River (SuAsCo) Watershed."

The May 2 work party in Boxborough is open to all interested volunteers and will focus on cutting and pulling Asiatic bittersweet vines. The group will meet at 9 a.m. at the Patch Hill Conservation Land trailhead on Liberty Square Road just north of Benjamin Drive.

Tools will be provided, but volunteers “are encouraged to bring their own digging tools (spades, mattocks, etc.) and/or lopping or pruning shears.” Volunteers are also reminded to wear long pants, closed-toed shoes, and work gloves. The work party is not recommended for children.

Says Barnett, “The Patch Hill Conservation Land is one of the Town's largest conservation properties, comprising nearly 220 acres of woodlands. With vernal pools and streams, Patch Hill has great ecological and wildlife habitat value. An extensive network of trails also provides recreational value and access to this important woodland.

This area, however, is being threatened in several locations by invasive plant species. The Town is currently working with Oxbow Associates to develop an inventory of plant species and an overall invasive species management plan that will be utilized over the next several years. We are initiating the Weed Warrior program at the trailhead on Liberty Square Road because it has been identified as one of the most threatened locations and of highest priority.”

According to Mass Audubon, "all invasive plants were first introduced to our area by humans as landscape specimens, or in some cases, accidentally. Because they did not evolve in our region, the natural mechanisms that normally control these species in their home ranges don't exist. As a result, these non-natives can out-compete, displace, and kill our native species."

When asked whether residents can independently remove invasives from Boxborough conservation land, Barnett replied, “the short answer to this question is that residents should first attend one or more work parties or training sessions, and once their knowledge has been confirmed they will be allowed and encouraged to remove invasives on their own.”

Can’t make it on May 2? “There will definitely be future work parties in this same location, and then additional locations in the Patch Hill Conservation Land as well as other conservation land throughout Boxborough,” says Barnett. “This will be a multi-year, ongoing effort!”
