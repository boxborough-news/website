Throughout April, the Boxborough Finance Committee (FinCom) has continued to investigate several variances and issues flagged by the town’s fiscal year 2025 audit. Roselli, Clark & Associates, Inc., a certified public accountant firm hired by the Town to audit its financial statements as of June 30, 2025, released their report to town administration on March 3, 2026.

In the summary letter, Roselli indicated that the town’s audit was delayed in part due to material variances and issues that needed to be addressed. After receiving the audit in late March, FinCom began delving into the reasons for the variances, asking the Boxborough Finance Director Honghoa Le and finance team how those issues have been, or will be, corrected.

FinCom members expressed concern about the impact of those issues on the town’s bond rating and “free cash.” Among the audit findings that FinCom discussed was the “inability to efficiently invest” $900,000 in excess cash during fiscal year 2024.

According to Town Administrator (TA) Michael Johns, the new Treasurer, Emily Roux, has corrected the problem, and the accounts are now being actively managed. The audit also found a $50,000 reporting error in OPEB (Other Post Employment Benefits) that originated in 2022 and continued for several years.

Additional findings showed that the town failed to fund $300,000 for OPEB (voted at Annual Town Meeting in May 2025) before the end of fiscal year 2025. According to TA Johns, there was no impact to free cash because the town reported OPEB funding as having been paid to the Department of Revenue, and the problem has since been corrected.

Some outstanding issues include a $200,000 cash variance; payroll withholdings that are out of balance and require reconciliation; and police detail balances running at a deficit. The FinCom will continue to discuss these issues with town administration and finance teams.

FinCom Chair Tony Newton told Boxborough News, “(FinCom) members have expressed concern at the multi-year errors highlighted by the audit. FinCom continues to investigate both the resolution of these immediate problems and how they can be prevented in the future.”

When reached for comment, TA Michael Johns said the “Administration and the Finance Team are implementing practices and procedures to ensure that recommendations made in the audit management letter are corrected and do not result in future audit issues.”
