Nate Kim, an Acton-Boxborough Regional High School junior from Boxborough, has won the Congressional Art Competition for the Massachusetts Third Congressional District.

Each Spring, the [Congressional Institute](https://www.conginst.org/) sponsors a nationwide high school visual art competition to recognize and encourage artistic talent in the nation and in each congressional district.

Students submit entries to their representative’s office, and panels of district artists select the winning entries. Winners are recognized both in their district and at an annual awards ceremony in Washington, DC. The winning works are displayed for one year at the U.S. Capitol.

Boxborough News reached out to Nate to congratulate him and ask some questions about his artwork.

Q: When did you first start painting? Do you or did you take lessons?

*After an inspiring visit to the Museum of Fine Arts, I started to study oil paint two years ago by watching many YouTube videos to learn how to paint like 19th century French academics who motivated me to oil paint, but I owe much of my foundation in the medium to Barbara Shapokas and her thoughtful mentorship at the Art Studio.*

Q: What made you decide to enter your painting into the Congressional Art Competition?

*I wanted to put my work out there, and I thought it would be fitting to enter a piece set in Boxborough into a competition that celebrates student artists across the district.*

Q: What is the title of the painting? Did you paint from a photo or did the subject pose?

*"Midsummer Indolence," the title of my painting, comes from writing my rationale for the MAEA competition earlier this year. The painting is set in a scene I took a picture of while working at Flerra Summer Playground last summer, and being in such a serene environment helped me relieve some of my stress from that summer, and that feeling is where the title came from.*

Q: When did you paint this painting? How long did it take to complete it?

*I painted it last fall during my Studio Art class at school. The painting itself took around five hours, but building the canvas and planning for the painting, like laying out the design and structuring the values and colors, took more time than painting it.*

Q: What is the medium (of the Flerra painting), and what is your preferred medium?

*I used oil paint for this painting, and it has settled itself to be my favorite medium to work with. That said, I've come to realize that oil painting borrows so much from other mediums that it feels incomplete to favor just one.*

Q: How often do you paint, and what are your favorite subjects to paint? What do you enjoy most about painting and/or creating art?

*I like to paint as much as possible, but recent time constraints have limited me to paint only about two to three times a month. From copying old masters' portraits when I started to learn oil painting, I've loved to paint portraits. Studying facial structure to explain the faces we encounter everyday has fascinated me, and capturing my close friends through drawings and paintings have been one of my favorite ways to celebrate my relationship with them.*

Q: Are you considering focusing on art after you graduate high school?

*I hope to continue learning or at least practicing painting after high school alongside studying my interests in politics and philosophy.*

Q: Will you attend the annual awards ceremony in Washington, D.C.?

*My family and I will be attending the reception ceremony at the Capitol on June 25th. I'll also take the opportunity of being in DC to visit the Smithsonian and study the work of painters I've long admired.*
