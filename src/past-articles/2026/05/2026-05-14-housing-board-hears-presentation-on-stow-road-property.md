At the Boxborough Housing Board (BHB) meeting on Tuesday, May 12, BHB member Joe Ferguson gave a short presentation outlining a proposed process for the disposition of the 70-72 Stow Road property, which was purchased by the Town in 2010 to build an affordable housing project, but has remained undeveloped.

Prior to Ferguson’s presentation, Housing Board Chair Al Murphy stated the objectives of the meeting. “This meeting will not get into the details…the object is (the) process,” he said. “This is a town-wide matter…we owe it to the town to make a recommendation” on the disposition of the Stow Road property.

Ferguson introduced his presentation by saying that he was looking forward to a positive discussion and acknowledged that his presentation was process focused and very high level. This is “a public real estate transaction,” he said. “We are assigned stewards of this land…(We need) a process to lead us to a thoughtful, intentional housing-policy driven and revenue-generating recommendation that is viable and approvable here at the local level.”

Ferguson framed the “problem to be solved” as “determining the highest and best use” for the town-owned land. He described the parcel as having “minimal physical and site constraints to develop housing” and noted that in addition to housing, there were possible community benefits and historic preservation opportunities.

Advocating for a collaborative process, Ferguson suggested the BHB lead public workshops with town administration and other boards to “deliver a viable, approvable, and comprehensive plan for housing and community benefits.” Noting that some development options would require special permits from the Zoning Board of Appeals or the Planning Board, he said it was important that those boards be looped into the planning process early.

Ferguson emphasized that there are a “significant” number of options for the property and recommended that the Housing Board commit to developing the site with housing. He also indicated that it was possible to solicit a “mission-driven developer,” and “tell them what we want…We can have control over this whole process. I think there’s a way to get the money we need…and the housing we need and want.”

Board members agreed that they should begin exploring the types of housing that makes the most sense for Boxborough. Working with the Office of Land Use and Permitting, the Board is applying for grants that will help fund a Housing Production Plan, which is meant to guide the board in developing and advocating for new housing that meets the needs of the community (see [https://www.boxboroughnews.org/news#h.auazeztzgigs](https://www.boxboroughnews.org/news#h.auazeztzgigs)).

The Housing Board’s next meeting is May 26 at 7 p.m. via Zoom.
