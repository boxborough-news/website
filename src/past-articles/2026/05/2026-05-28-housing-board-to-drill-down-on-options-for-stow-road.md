At its meeting on Tuesday, May 26, the Boxborough Housing Board (BHB) continued their discussion regarding the disposition of the 70-72 Stow Road property, which was purchased by the Town in 2010 to build an affordable housing project, but has remained undeveloped.

BHB Chair Al Murphy and board members agreed to schedule a series of meetings to “drill down into the various options” for the property. Murphy suggested that the first such meeting focus on the history of the property, referencing the work that was done by the Stow Road Concept Development Committee (SRCDC), an ad-hoc committee appointed by the Select Board early in 2012 that is no longer active. Murphy acknowledged that the SRCDC developed a series of objectives that “are different than today’s,” but that the information is still valuable.

Board member Phyn Vermin volunteered to give a presentation on cohousing for one of the Board’s future meetings. She suggested the BHB “reach out to neighboring cohousing communities” and ask the tenants/homeowners to talk about the process to get “the inside perspective.” Cohousing, as defined by the National Cohousing Alliance, is a type of intentional, collaborative housing in which residents actively participate in the design and operation of their neighborhoods…and consciously commit to living as a community. The neighborhood’s physical design encourages both individual space and social contact.

A member of the public spoke about his experience volunteering with Habitat for Humanity (Habitat) and suggested the BHB explore working with Habitat to develop the Stow Road property. BHB members were very receptive to the idea and agreed to add Habitat to the list of options.

In other business, Boxborough Community Services Coordinator Wendy Trinks, who manages the Boxborough Rental Assistance Program (BRAP) for the BHB, reported that the need for rental assistance is growing. Trinks indicated that there are currently six applicants on the waiting list. BRAP provides rental assistance to income-eligible households who are renting qualifying housing units in Boxborough and currently serves 15 households. BHB members acknowledged that managing the program on a larger scale may be difficult, but agreed to discuss expanding the program in the future.
