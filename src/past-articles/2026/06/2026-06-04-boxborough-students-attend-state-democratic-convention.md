On May 29 and 30, Democrats from across Massachusetts gathered at the DCU Center in Worcester. Among the more than 4000 delegates who attended the convention were Boxborough students Grace Connor, Andrew Tan, and Nate Kim, as well as Boxborough Democratic Town Committee members Megan Connor (DTC co-chair), Renee Smutney-Fritz, Steve Schmitt, Alethea Tan, and Dick Wagman.

“As a student, it was especially meaningful to participate in a convention alongside delegates with decades of political experience,” said Nate Kim. “It showed me that young people can have a voice in the process, as well.”

“As a first-time delegate and Youth Democrat, I was amazed by the energy and excitement throughout the convention," said Grace Connor. “Seeing people campaign for their candidates, hand out buttons and posters, and rally behind the speeches made the political process feel much more real. Being able to vote alongside delegates from around the state made me feel connected to the political process in a way that’s hard to experience elsewhere.”

Throughout the convention, the delegates heard speeches from Gov. Maura Healey, Lt. Gov. Kim Driscoll, U.S. Rep. Ayanna Presley, Auditor Diana DiZoglio, and Attorney General Andrea Campbell. The delegates also heard from U.S. Senator Ed Markey and U.S. Representative Seth Moulton, who is challenging Markey in this year’s Senate race. In his speech to delegates, Moulton centered his remarks on the need for generational change in Democratic leadership and called for a renewed vision for the party's future. Markey highlighted his progressive record and argued that Massachusetts should continue moving forward on issues such as climate policy and social justice.

After voting settled, Senator Markey won the party's endorsement in his reelection bid against Rep. Moulton, receiving 73% of delegate support. Moulton secured 27%, which is enough to earn a place on Democratic primary ballot on September 1 under party rules. The winner of the primary will face off against the Republican candidate in the general election on November 3.

"I thought the convention was a great experience because it showed what democracy looks like in action," said Andrew Tan. "The voting process was messy and inefficient at times, but that's also part of how democracy works." While Markey's endorsement victory was decisive, the results ensure that Massachusetts Democratic voters will have a choice in September as the two candidates continue campaigning across the state.
