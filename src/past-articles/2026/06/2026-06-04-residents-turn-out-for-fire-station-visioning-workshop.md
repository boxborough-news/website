Almost 40 people gathered in the Grange Room at Town Hall on Thursday, June 4 to participate in the Fire Station Community Visioning Workshop, sponsored by the Boxborough Fire Station Building Committee (FSBC) and run by Context Architecture (Context), the firm that the town has hired to design Boxborough’s new fire station.

Attendees were greeted at the door by Context Project Manager Emily Mowbray, who handed each person a card and instructed them to write some words that describe Boxborough. Once everyone was seated, Mowbray asked the group to share their descriptive words, which included rural, leafy, historic, and community, among others. She then asked the group how some of the attributes might be associated with a fire station building.

Mowbray then passed the baton to Context President & Principal-in-Charge Jeff Shaw, who turned the group’s attention to the four posters in the front of the room depicting photos of buildings, each centered on a theme.

One poster was devoted to Boxborough buildings and included photos of Town Hall, the library, some private residences, and the church. A second poster showed buildings with varied “shape and volume.” The third was devoted exclusively to fire station buildings, and the fourth focused on building materials, showing brick, shingle, and clapboard facades.

Shaw instructed attendees to place a sticker on any building photos that they found appealing. When the exercise was complete, the traditional, New England style buildings appeared to be favored by the participants.

Context Director of Projects Chris Logan closed out the workshop by telling the group about future opportunities for input, including Fifer’s Day on June 20 (in the FSBC booth) and the Fire Station Community Design Review and Listening Session on July 23. “Keep engaging…keep showing up,” Logan said.

After the workshop, FSBC Chair Mary Brolin provided the following statement to Boxborough News: “I am absolutely thrilled by the incredible turnout and energy at our community visioning session tonight. A fire station is more than just a building—it’s a cornerstone of our town, and seeing so many neighbors share their insight and vision ensures that the final design will reflect the heart and character of our community. We are deeply grateful for everyone who came out to help shape this exciting new chapter. Look for us at Fifer's Day to give more input.”
