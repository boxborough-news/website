The beavers in Beaver Brook have attracted a lot of attention lately.

Beaver Brook originates in Wolf Swamp in Boxborough and meanders north, crossing Mass Ave through the meadows on either side of Hill Road near Wetherbee Farm, flows through Paddock Estates, under I-495, and empties into Forge Pond in Westford.

In March of this year, a Hill Road resident wrote to the Boxborough Office of Land Use and Permitting with “concern(s) regarding a beaver dam on Beaver Brook.” The resident noted that they “have been informed by neighbors that this area is prone to significant flooding, reportedly reaching as far back as Bravo Pizza.”

Town staff investigated the issue at that time, visiting the site to confirm beaver dams as the cause of the flooding, and they began reaching out to the interested parties, namely, the Select Board, Conservation Commission (ConsCom), Agricultural Commission (AgCom), and the property manager at Paddock Estates.

When Paddock Estates was approved by the Boxborough Zoning Board of Appeals in 2015, the permit dictated that a portion of the development was set aside as open space for conservation and agricultural use. There is a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) between the Select Board and the ConsCom and AgCom that governs the monitoring and management of the land.

According to meeting minutes, the AgCom discussed the recent beaver activity and threats it posed at its meeting on May 12. The beaver activity along Beaver Brook leads to flooding, which threatens agricultural use of the land near Wetherbee Farm. According to the AgCom, the flooding is also a threat to the drinking well heads at Paddock Estates. The dams not only cause flooding upstream, but prevent water flow downstream; according to AgCom, this has impacted the fire pond cistern at Paddock Estates.

The AgCom voted to send a letter to the Paddock property manager recommending and authorizing trapping and killing the beaver and removing its dam, a solution which had been implemented approximately four and half years ago, the last time that beavers had dammed up the brook.

According to correspondence obtained by Boxborough News, Paddock’s property manager notified Associate Town Planner Ian Gilson on June 2 that he was “having a very difficult time locating a trapper for the beavers. It seems no one wants to do trapping anymore.” He asked “that the town reconsider the beaver deceiver solution,” which he described as “more of a long term solution.”

A Beaver DeceiverTM is a flow device that manages water levels in beaver-inhabited areas. It prevents beavers from building dams, while allowing the beavers to stay and maintain their wetland habitats. In 2025, the Boxborough Conservation Commission contracted with Beaver Deceivers LLC to install the device to prevent flooding in an area behind Blanchard School.

At their meeting on June 2, ConsCom members reviewed a letter that Sophia Price, a seventh grader at RJ Grey Junior High School and lives at Paddock Estates, sent to members of AgCom on June 1. The letter, signed by over 200 fellow students and teachers, implores AgCom to “approve the use of a Beaver DeceiverTM as a solution for the beaver currently causing flooding at a horse farm near The Paddock Estates where I live.” Price noted that “Paddock Estates ownership has offered to pay the cost of the device so there would be no financial responsibility for the town or taxpayers to fund it.”

ConsCom members supported Price’s position to use the Beaver DeceiverTM as a solution to the problem. When reached for comment, ConsCom Chair Liz Markiewicz wrote, “The Commission's policy is to strongly encourage the use of flow control devices (beaver deceivers) where feasible, allowing beavers, their dams, and the highly valuable wetland and wildlife habitat they create to remain in place.”

On June 9, Price attended the AgCom meeting to advocate for protecting the beaver by employing the Beaver DeceiverTM instead of the commission’s original order to trap and kill. As reported last week in Boxborough News, this issue was the topic of Price’s civics fair project.

(see[https://www.boxboroughnews.org/archives/jan-jun-2026-features#h.20ey7uftssq6](https://www.boxboroughnews.org/archives/jan-jun-2026-features#h.20ey7uftssq6))

AgCom members expressed their doubt that the Beaver DeceiverTM would work in this particular situation. Then one resident, who introduced herself as a wildlife expert, told the commission that beavers are territorial, and if they are removed, more will come. She also indicated that the Beaver DeceiverTM behind the Blanchard School is solving a similar problem. She pleaded with the commission to abandon the directive to trap and kill and ask Paddock management to reach out to Beaver Deceivers LLC for a humane and permanent solution to the problem.

AgCom members were swayed by the argument and moved that AgCom “back off from the previous recommendation and solicit and/or request town staff to solicit additional information and recommendations from the interested parties.” All members voted in favor.

Score one for the beavers…and for Sophia!
