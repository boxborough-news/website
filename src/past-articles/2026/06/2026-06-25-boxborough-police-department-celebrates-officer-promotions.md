On Wednesday morning, June 24, approximately 40 people filled the Grange Room at Town Hall to witness and celebrate the promotion of two Boxborough police officers and the welcoming of Boxborough’s newest police officer.

Police Chief John Szewczyk opened the ceremony, welcoming and thanking the friends and families of the police officers, noting that the officers would not be able to do their important work without the support from loved ones. He also noted that the importance of one’s job is secondary to self care, which includes devoting time to friends and family and spiritual, physical, and mental health.

Szewczyk provided an introduction for each celebrated officer, highlighting their background, experience, and accolades.

Ryan Ingerman, Boxborough’s newest police officer, is a 2026 graduate of the Lynnfield Police Academy. He was president of the academy class of 59 recruits and the recipient of the Emergency Vehicle Operations Course (EVOC) award. Ingerman served as a dispatcher and supervisor at the North Shore Regional 911 Center for six years. He also served in the Massachusetts Army National Guard from 2015 to 2021 and has served in the Air National Guard since 2023. Ingerman’s mother proudly pinned his badge, and Town Clerk Becky Harris immediately swore him into service.

Max Bressi, who most townspeople know as “K9 Koda’s handler,” was promoted to sergeant. A BPD officer since 2019, Szewczyk described Sergeant Bressi as someone “who doesn’t shy away from anything,” and consistently follows through. Bressi is the lead field training officer and fleet manager. Szewczyk credited him with putting Boxborough’s K9 program together and said that Bressi is a “role model of hard work and going above and beyond…I’m extremely proud of him…He has a bright future in law enforcement.” Bressi’s badge was pinned by BPD Sergeant Brett Pelley.

Nathan Bowolick, a twenty-year BPD veteran, was promoted to lieutenant. Szewczyk described Lieutenant Bowolick as a leader in the department to whom others turn for help. He thanked him for his role as accreditation manager, noting that the BPD would not have obtained reaccreditation in 2024 without his outstanding work. “We are very fortunate to have him…(this promotion) is well deserved,” Szewczyk said. Lt. Bowolick’s son and wife came forward to pin his badge.

Szewczyk closed the ceremony by congratulating the officers and recognizing their hard work. He reminded them that taking care of themselves and their relationships with friends and family come first. And he thanked the friends, family members, residents, and town employees “for the support you constantly give to the Boxborough Police Department.”
