At their meeting on June 23, the Boxborough Select Board heard a presentation from Becky Caruso, a Clean Energy & Environment Legacy Transition (CELT) Municipal Fellow working with Boxborough's Sustainability Committee, advocating for the adoption of the Specialized Stretch Code as a general bylaw.

Caruso explained to the Board that the Specialized Stretch Code is an enhanced building standard that would encourage energy efficiency, renewable energy, and electric readiness for new construction. The Specialized Stretch Code would apply only to new construction; it does not require existing homes, additions, or renovations to be upgraded. Caruso emphasized that adoption of the Specialized Stretch Code would also make the town eligible for state grant opportunities.

Board member Kristin Hilberg was skeptical about some of the data in the presentation and expressed concern that adopting the new code would make it more expensive to build houses in Boxborough.

Several Board members also expressed some concern that the presentation had incorporated an analysis of the town’s new fire station, which is currently in the design stage. Select Board Clerk Sam Anderson emphasized that a “green” fire station design is a separate issue from whether to adopt the Specialized Stretch Code.

Town Administrator Mike Johns noted that Context Architecture, the design firm that the town has hired for the fire station project, is scheduled to meet with the Sustainability Committee to discuss how to “go green as possible.”

Sustainability Committee Chair Kate Davies clarified that this presentation was meant to generate “early-stage discussion” and feedback. She also noted that the Sustainability Committee is motivated to move this issue forward in part due to the construction anticipated at the Park at Beaver Brook (former Cisco campus). She noted that the Committee wants the town to encourage green buildings and energy efficiency at the Park at Beaver Brook.

Select Board Chair Bob Stemple asked Associate Town Planner Ian Gilson to gather more information and present again at a future meeting. He also noted that the Select Board will want input from other committees as well, for example, from the Housing Board and the Economic Development Committee.

In other business, the Board heard from Economic Development Committee (EDC) Chair Abby Reip about her efforts to collaborate with Boxborough’s Diversity Equity and Inclusion (DEI) Committee on the town’s draft Economic Development Plan (“Plan”). Reip explained that the grant the town received to draft the Plan specifies that the Plan will incorporate minority viewpoints and perspectives, but that she and Town Planner Alec Wade have been unable to secure participation by the town’s DEI Committee. She requested the Select Board’s help in facilitating this collaboration.

Select Board member Wes Fowlks, also the Chair of the DEI Committee, noted that he has been unable to schedule recent meetings of the DEI Committee due to nonresponsive members and suggested that the Select Board may need to reconsider the charge of the committee. A broader discussion ensued about evaluating the town’s current DEI needs and potentially “re-defining” the goals of the DEI Committee.

Chair Stemple indicated that he would reach out to the current DEI Committee members regarding participation in the Economic Development Plan.
