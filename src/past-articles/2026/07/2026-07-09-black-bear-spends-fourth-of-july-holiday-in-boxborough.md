In the days leading up to the Fourth of July weekend, Boxborough residents reported several black bear sightings on the eastern side of town.

John Ehrlich of Davidson Road shared that a black bear visited his yard several times between June 30 and July 4. Most sightings were in the evening, roughly 7:30-9 p.m., with one visit around 8 a.m. “Looks youthful,” Ehrlich reported, “perhaps 1-2 years, solitary, no visible tags. Handsome critter.”

On Friday, July 3, Boxborough resident Susan Bak saw a bear cross Mass Ave (Route 111) while she was driving westbound around 4:30 p.m. “It was medium size,” she said. “It was just after Flamingo” [Market & Liquor] and before Sadler Lane. “He was running from my left [south] to right [north].”

“In that black bears have moved east, we must learn to live safely knowing they're around,” commented Boxborough Conservation Trust President Rita Gibes Grossman. “The Massachusetts Division of Fisheries and Wildlife has a good resource on bears and we strongly recommend everyone read it: [https://www.mass.gov/black-bears-in-massachusetts](https://www.mass.gov/black-bears-in-massachusetts).”

The Division of Fisheries and Wildlife reminds all Massachusetts residents to remove bird feeders, secure garbage, protect pets, and use electric fencing for chickens and other livestock. And, if there is a bear in your yard, “wait for the bear to leave, then remove or secure food sources on your property.”

Grossman also suggests that Boxborough residents report any bear sightings to the Boxborough Police Department and to the “MassBears” research project at Amherst College at [https://massbears.wordpress.amherst.edu/](https://massbears.wordpress.amherst.edu/).
