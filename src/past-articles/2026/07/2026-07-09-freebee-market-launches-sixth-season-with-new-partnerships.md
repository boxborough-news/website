Boxborough's volunteer-powered FreeBee Market returns for its sixth season on Saturday, July 11, 2026, at 10:30 a.m. on the side lawn of UCC Boxborough, 30 Middle Road. What began as an emergency response to the challenges of 2020 and the fire on Swanson Road has grown into a joyful community tradition centered on food rescue, sustainability, and neighborly sharing.

FreeBee was born during the pandemic and in the aftermath of the Swanson Road apartment fire, when Boxborough residents organized to support displaced families. As volunteers worked to meet immediate needs, one group discovered another gap: most food pantries were closed on Saturday and excess food normally given to them (“gleaned”) was going to waste. Volunteers began rescuing surplus produce and meat from local supermarkets and sharing it on the lawn of the UCC Boxborough. By 2021, the effort had become the FreeBee Market, guided by its simple motto: "Give what you can, take what you need."

“Over the past five years, FreeBee has grown into a series of free seasonal food-rescue and community sharing markets,” says coordinator Mary Pavlik. “We're still entirely volunteer-run, adapting to changing food sources and community needs.”

Regular visitors will notice an important change this year. The market is beginning in July rather than May, reflecting a positive shift in the region's food rescue network. Expanded refrigeration and storage at organizations such as the Acton Food Pantry mean more surplus food can now be distributed directly through traditional pantries, leaving less retail food available for FreeBee to rescue.

To adapt, FreeBee has formed a new partnership with Boston Area Gleaners, made possible by a charitable subscription donated by local resident Kristin Hilberg. The partnership will provide fresh, locally grown fruits and vegetables gleaned each week through October, supplemented by rescued food from local farms and supermarkets and bread from Nashoba Brook Bakery. As always, all food is free and intended to be enjoyed as soon as possible.

The organization is also introducing several behind-the-scenes improvements this season. Boxborough resident Nancy Settle-Murphy of Guided Insights has generously led a series of training sessions to strengthen operations and improve consistency; a grant from the Acton-Boxborough Cultural Council has helped fund new signage, volunteer development, and FreeBee's website.

“Our grassroots organization is flourishing because of our intergenerational volunteer community,” says Heather Fleming. “We're grateful for partners like [Applefield Farm](https://www.applefieldfarms.com/) and [Daisy Hill Farm](https://www.daisyhillfarm.us/) for seedlings, Boston Area Gleaners, and the community gardeners who help us by growing and sharing more fresh food each week.”

Opening Day on July 11 will feature the weekly food market along with free crafts and swap shop items, a bounce house, free hot dogs provided by the church, and information from local civic organizations.

The weekly free food rescues will continue to be held every Saturday from 10:30 a.m. to noon or until food runs out at 30 Middle Road Boxborough, with themed Community Market events planned each month through October, on August 15 (school supplies), September 12 (harvest/agriculture), and October 24 (costume exchange).

To volunteer or learn more, email [freebeecommunity@gmail.com](mailto:freebeecommunity@gmail.com).
