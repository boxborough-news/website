On Thursday, July 23, the Boxborough Fire Station Building Committee (“FSBC”) held a “Community Design Update” to share progress on the design of the town’s new fire station and hear questions and comments from the public.

In front of an audience of roughly 30 community members in the room and online, representatives from Context Architecture (“Context”), the town’s design firm, explained what the fire station design currently looks like as the town moves from the “concept design” phase to the “schematic design” phase.

Chris Logan and Jeff Shaw of Context shared details about the site layout and floor plan for a 20,700 square foot one-story building and explained how the fire department staff and the public would move throughout the space.

Shaw emphasized that although the project has come a long way, there is “still a lot more work to do” since the building is still in the early stages of design. “There is still a lot that can and will change,” he said.

After the 30-minute presentation, there was an hour of questions and comments from the audience, including questions about parking, the orientation of the building on the site, the roof type, concerns of abutters, why a one-story building was chosen over a two-story building, and both access to and visibility from Mass Ave.

After the Community Design Update, the FSBC held their regular meeting.

Town Administrator Mike Johns reported that the town did not close on the 984 Mass Ave property on July 17 and that the closing is expected to take place in the “next couple of weeks.” Johns did not identify a specific reason for the delay but stated that there are several outstanding items that the attorneys are working on. He noted that “it’s not unusual for a complex acquisition like this to sometimes be delayed… I want to note that there are no red flags… it’s just a delay in the acquisition.”

The FSBC also reviewed and discussed two site plan options and three building plans. Discussion included whether hitting a certain target square footage, e.g., 20,000 square feet, should be a goal in and of itself. The committee grappled with the various trade-offs that will need to be considered when working to minimize the cost of the project.

After the meeting, Town Administrator Johns offered the following comment: “We are at a positive turning point in the life of this project, as we move from conceptual to schematic design. I think all stakeholders, including residents, and fire department staff are seeing that questions and concerns have been listened to, taken seriously and addressed appropriately, to shape the new firehouse.”

The FSBC’s next meeting is scheduled for August 13 and will focus on design components related to energy efficiency and sustainability.
