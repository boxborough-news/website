The Boxborough Recreation Commission meeting on July 28 opened with a discussion of the Flerra Summer Playground (Flerra) program. The program went well overall, though they had to cancel on three separate days due to inclement weather. There was also some paperwork confusion at the start of the program. The goal for next year is to digitize all non-signature forms and have all counselor materials submitted before orientation.

The Commission also discussed how Flerra volunteer and counselor management has been challenging. As a result, plans for next year include capping Flerra volunteers at 50, limiting most to two weeks, interviewing returning green-shirt staff, and enforcing behavioral accountability more strictly.

There have been incidents at Flerra involving ground hornets and bees this year that have persisted despite visits from an exterminator. Flerra’s final week began with a skunk incident, handled by relocating campers. Animal control declined to intervene; the skunk is believed to be a known local animal.

The Commission also discussed the playground improvements planned for this fall at Flerra Meadows, noting that the project is on track. The playground renovation will include replacing the main playground structure and installing an ADA-compliant surface.

“Over the years, the current large structure has seen a lot of wear and tear,” Commission member Hilary Greven told Boxborough News last year, when first seeking Community Preservation Act (CPA) funding for the project. “So we are looking to install a new large structure to complete the improvements to the playground, providing a safe and exciting playground for residents and the hundreds of children who attend Flerra Summer Playground.” The funding was approved at the May 2026 Annual Town Meeting.

The equipment has been ordered and will arrive in mid-September; installation is targeted for late September, with a total cost of $275,700. ADA accessibility from the parking lot to the playground is a separate future project.

In other business, the Commission discussed portable pickleball nets for Liberty Field courts. Options from Acton, Stow, and Concord were discussed, and the $389 wheeled outdoor net was favored as the most durable mid-range option. The Commission also welcomed its newest member, Boxborough resident Justin Rowe.
