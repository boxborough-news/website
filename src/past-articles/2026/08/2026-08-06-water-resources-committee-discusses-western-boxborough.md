At its meeting on July 21, the Water Resources Committee (WRC) heard updates on the Littleton-Boxborough waterline project.

The project brings clean, treated public water to properties located on Beaver Brook Road, Swanson Road, and Codman Hill Road through a new pipeline running from Littleton into western Boxborough.

According to the Littleton Electric Light and Water Department (LELWD) website, “this project brings public water to about 1,100 residents living along the western edge of Boxborough, where drinking water wells are contaminated with PFAS and road salt.”

According to WRC Chair Les Fox, the water main from the Whitcomb Avenue Water Treatment Plant to Boxborough is completed. The hydrants are fully operational, and the procedures to connect to the housing units are being established. The project is expected to be completed by the end of 2026.

The project is paid for in part by state funding and in part by Boxborough property owners along the waterline, who will be assessed a “betterment fee.”

At the July 27 Select Board meeting, Town Administrator Michael Johns reported that the condominium associations to be served by the waterline have received grants from the state that will cover all of the property owners’ “betterment fees,” with excess funding expected to be applied to service connection costs.

Town Administrator Johns characterized this as a “huge win” and added that, “to say this is an enormously complicated process is an understatement.” The town finance team has been working with the state and the condo associations to work out the mechanics of the grant and the “betterment fee” payments.

The Water Resources Committee continues to discuss follow-on studies of water resources in Boxborough. Boxborough water needs can be grouped into two categories: (1) water needed for drinking and other domestic uses, and (2) water needed to fight fires.

Most of the suggested topics will require additional funding, which will have to be secured at a future Town Meeting. The topics include:

1. Devise a contingency plan to secure water from other sources in the event of insufficient water quantity and quality from existing wells.
2. Establish a system of monitoring wells to check water quality. Boxborough already has a number of monitoring wells. However, these wells are not uniformly placed across Boxborough, and many of the wells are too shallow to sample the deep rock fracture trace water sources for drinking water.
3. Determine the capacity of the Hager Well. The Hager Well currently supplies water to town buildings and the Blanchard school. Preliminary measurements have indicated that it is operating well below its DEP-permitted capacity. However, its maximum capacity has never been accurately measured because of the expense of doing so. For planning purposes, it would be useful to know the maximum available capacity if water needs to be supplied to homes and businesses in the area.
4. Prepare an updated map of the fire ponds and cisterns in Boxborough. The study would also determine whether they have been well maintained and provide adequate coverage. The study would propose solutions to any identified problems. The Committee discussed inviting Boxborough Fire Chief John Kivlan to the next meeting to hear his views.

The next WRC meeting will be on August 18, at 7:30 p.m., via Zoom.
