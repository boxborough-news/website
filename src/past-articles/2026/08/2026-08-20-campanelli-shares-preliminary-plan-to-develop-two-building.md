On August 17, the Planning Board met in a hybrid format, instead of their typical Zoom-only meeting. The agenda included a scheduled appointment with Russell Dion, Campanelli Partner in Charge for The Park at Beaver Brook; a discussion on zoning articles for Fall Town Meeting, including a temporary moratorium on data centers; and an executive session to “discuss strategy with respect to litigation per MGL Ch. 30A §21 (a) (3) (Appeal of Special Permit, and Site Plan Review decisions, 242/244 Adams Place).”

When reached for comment after the meeting, Planning Board Chair Rich Guzzardi told Boxborough News, “It was great to have the Planning Board back in person under the hybrid format. While we will work through managing the audio and online elements of the meeting, the discussion in the room was definitely more fluid. The board has received a lot of feedback on conducting in person/hybrid meetings, so as agreed to by the board at our last meeting we will continue to use this format and see how it goes.”

Chair Guzzardi welcomed Russell Dion, Campanelli Partner in Charge for The Park at Beaver Brook, who joined the meeting in person. Campanelli attorney Johanna Schneider joined the meeting via Zoom. Dion said, “The Park is in good shape. We have four technology-based tenants–Cisco, Medtronic, TUV, and Intel”…and a “food and beverage tenant, Craft Food Hall.” The next steps, he said, were to develop the three remaining lots that were permitted through the Open Space Commercial Development (OSCD) Special Permit, which was approved by the Planning Board in January 2026.

See Boxborough News article about the approval here: [https://www.boxboroughnews.org/archives/jan-jun-2026-news#h.4oiwfewj3i60](https://www.boxboroughnews.org/archives/jan-jun-2026-news#h.4oiwfewj3i60)

Dion detailed the "significant" investments that Campanelli has made in The Park, including the waterline, PFAS filtration, tenant improvements, and conservation work. “We want to continue our investment here in Boxborough, and my partners and I are very anxious to get started…” Campanelli’s goal, Dion said, is to submit site plan applications for Lots 600 and 700 in late September or early October.

Dion acknowledged that Campanelli does not have a tenant for either building, but that they are “very bullish and optimistic that (they) could have a new tenant in a new facility within the next 18 to 24 months.” Campanelli, Dion said, “feels good about the life science market” and would focus on a “different (type of life science) user,” one that would only need 60,000 to 90,000 square feet for light manufacturing, which is permitted under the Office Park zoning.

Town Planner Alec Wade commended Dion and his team for their work and willingness to get things done. Anticipating the site plan applications, Wade said, “I’m excited to see it come forward, and I think it will be a great opportunity to test the new expedited permitting method that we have put in place…I am excited to work with them (Campanelli).”

The Board spent considerable time discussing a zoning article, proposed by Planning Board Clerk Chris Dowdy, that would place a temporary moratorium on data centers in Boxborough. The moratorium would expire on December 30, 2027. Board members worked to define what constitutes a data center and had concerns that businesses that house servers or network equipment may be prohibited from doing business in town if the definition isn’t explicit.

Associate member Than Stuntz expressed his opinion, “The data center is not the problem; it’s the energy/electrical usage.” Members agreed that they need more information about local businesses’ energy consumption to help define and/or regulate data centers, and they asked Wade to gather energy usage data from Littleton Electric. The board will conduct a public hearing on the proposed warrant article on Monday, August 31 at 7:05 p.m. via Zoom.

After completing their business during open session, the board entered into executive session to discuss litigation pertaining to 242/244 Adams Place. This is the second time that the board has used executive session to discuss the Adams Place litigation. See July 2026 Boxborough News article summarizing the case here: [https://www.boxboroughnews.org/news#h.2hz4q8oehe10](https://www.boxboroughnews.org/news#h.2hz4q8oehe10). The board did not discuss the litigation in open session.
