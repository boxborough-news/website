No one can predict how many voters will turn out for the state primary election on September 1, but Town Clerk Rebecca (Becky) Harris is ready.

On August 12, Harris and Town Constable Owen Neville conducted a “test deck,” a state law requirement that tests the electronic voting system programming accuracy. Harris told Boxborough News that state law requires that the test deck be posted as a public meeting agenda and that the two town political committee chairs are notified in advance, which she did.

Harris explained that the state law dictates that “we run 50 ballots of each political party at primaries…We then mail the results tapes to the Secretary of the Commonwealth's election division.” It “went smoothly,” Harris said, and added that this year, there was one member of the public present.

Harris hires a temporary staff to help her run the election. “We typically hire three shifts of poll workers, which includes four check-in workers, a constable and a warden on each shift and then two of the check-in workers stay to be hand counters after the election is closed. We also hire two election workers who open and process the Vote By Mail ballots on election day,” Harris explained. “If someone is interested (in working the polls), they just need to contact me and I meet with them and train them.”

All registered voters are eligible to vote in the state primary, regardless of party affiliation. If you are registered as a Democrat or a Republican, you must be given that party’s ballot. If you are unenrolled, also known as independent, you can choose either the Democratic or Republican ballot.

Boxborough News asked Harris which ballot registered Libertarians would fill out. She replied, “Libertarian was recently removed as a party (for which a separate candidate can run and a separate ballot given), and is now just a ‘political designation’ just like all of the other non-D(emocratic) or R(epublican) parties, so they can choose either ballot. Choosing one ballot or the other on primary day does NOT change your voter registration, contrary to the way it used to be a long time ago.”

The last day to register to vote (or change your party affiliation) to be eligible to vote in the State Primary is Saturday, August 22. Town Hall will be open from noon to 5 p.m. Residents can also register online until midnight.

Saturday, August 22 is also the first day of in-person early voting at Town Hall from noon to 5 p.m. In-person early voting continues on Monday, August 24 through Thursday, August 27 from 11 a.m. to 2 p.m. at Town Hall.

On Tuesday, September 1, polls will be open from 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. at Town Hall. For additional information on the election and voting schedule, visit the town’s website: ([https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/211/Elections-Voting](https://www.boxborough-ma.gov/211/Elections-Voting)).

Who’s on the ballot? Democrats will choose between incumbent Senator Ed Markey and challenger Representative Seth Moulton for their “Senator in Congress” candidate. The winner will advance to the November 3 general election and will face Republican candidate John Deaton.

Other Democratic contested races are Governor’s Councillor (challenger Margie Palladino faces incumbent Mara Dolan). The winner of this race will not face a Republican challenger in the general election. Also, Middlesex Attorney General Marian Ryan faces a challenge from David Solet. The winner will face Republican candidate Michael Walsh in the general election.

On the Republican ballot, there is only one contested race: Mike Minogue and Brian Shortsleeve are running to be the Republican nominee to challenge sitting Governor Maura Healey in the November 3 general election.

So, what can we expect for turnout in the 2026 state primary? Boxborough News gathered statistics from the three previous state primary elections. According to data obtained from the Massachusetts Secretary of the Commonwealth and the Boxborough Town Clerk’s office, Boxborough voter turnout has consistently been higher than the statewide turnout in every state primary since 2020.

Further proof that Boxborough is a “rural, engaged community”!
