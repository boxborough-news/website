Over a dozen adults and children attended the Agricultural Commission’s meeting on Tuesday, August 10 and voiced their support for a non-lethal solution to the “beaver problem” in Beaver Brook.

Boxborough News previously reported on the beaver problem and a local student’s initiative to save the beavers from being removed and killed. (See [https://www.boxboroughnews.org/archives/jan-jun-2026-features#h.yfhmkpucsy9l](https://www.boxboroughnews.org/archives/jan-jun-2026-features#h.yfhmkpucsy9l)).

Beaver Brook flows through the meadows on either side of Hill Road near Wetherbee Farm and is the source of water for the fire pond at Paddock Estates. In March of 2026, a Hill Road resident reported a high level of water on their property that posed a threat to their wellhead. Town officials investigated the problem and determined that three beaver dams on Beaver Brook caused the flooding. The flooding also concerns the Agricultural Commission, whose interest is to protect the adjacent parcels for agricultural use. The beaver dams are also a concern of the fire chief, as they block the flow of water into the fire pond at Paddock Estates.

Initially, during their May 2026 meeting, AgCom members recommended breaching the dams and removing the beavers to alleviate the flooding. In June, after an outpouring of public support for saving the beavers, AgCom voted to “pause (their) previous recommendation (to trap and kill the beavers) and “solicit additional information and solutions from the interested parties.”.

Then, in early July, the lower (most downstream) dam was breached. Associate Town Planner Ian Gilson told Boxborough News that the most recent site visit indicated that the beavers have not rebuilt that dam. Furthermore, Gilson noted, the breaching “provided some relief to the fire pond.”

On August 10, Gilson reported to AgCom that “representatives from the Town and the Central Massachusetts Mosquito Control Project (CMMCP) conducted a site visit to evaluate beaver dams and associated flooding near Beaver Brook and Hill Road, and on the parcel owned by Paddock Estates LLC.”

Gilson’s report states “CMMCP determined that conditions warrant breaching the dams,” noting that “the inundated areas may provide mosquito larval habitat…Breaching the dams would help restore water levels and drainage patterns closer to their typical condition.” The report also highlighted CMMCP’s concerns regarding the restricted water flow into the fire-suppression pond serving Paddock Estates.

AgCom members listened to the public’s comments and spent over an hour discussing the issue. AgCom Clerk Bryon Clemence told the public that AgCom did not have the authority and decision-making power on this issue and that they (the public) should address their concerns to the Board of Health and its agent, Jim Gareffi.

And they did. Boxborough News was copied on eight of the emails sent to Gareffi, AgCom, and town officials pleading for a non-lethal, long-term solution to the problem, such as a Beaver DeceiverTM, which is a flow device that manages water levels in beaver-inhabited areas. It is intended to prevent beavers from building dams, while allowing the beavers to stay and maintain their wetland habitats.

Responding to the letters, Chair Neville wrote, “The Agricultural Commission's interests here are clear and specific to the one parcel. But we are also residents, neighbors, and concerned citizens. The impact on the life safety systems at Paddock is serious and consequential. At least three nearby properties have also been negatively impacted.”

Boxborough News spoke with Gilson earlier this week to obtain an update. While AgCom is a stakeholder (responsible for the management and maintenance of the affected parcels), Gilson explained, neither they nor the Board of Health have the authority to file a permit application to breach the dam, trap and kill the beavers, or install a Beaver DeceiverTM. Gilson explained that the decision lies with the property owner (Paddock Estates) to file an application to take action.

Gilson told Boxborough News that Boxborough Fire Chief John Kivlan will meet with Paddock Estates management to discuss the problem in the near future.
